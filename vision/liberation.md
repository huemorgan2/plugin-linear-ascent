# Linear Ascent — The Story of the Climb

*Written 2026-08-01. The world's short story, the stories of floors 1–3, the
monsters explained, and what it means to liberate a floor. This is the
storyline bible the floor movies are shot from, and the reference for any
re-authoring of the bestiary. Companions: [story.md](./story.md) (premise) ·
[world.md](./world.md) (geography) · floor content in
`plugin_linear_ascent/content/floors/`.*

*New canon introduced here (not yet in the engine): **the Yoke** — the
Warden's broadcast that keeps a floor's life maddened — and the three-part
monster taxonomy (**the Native, the Pressed, the Wrongmade**). Everything
else agrees with the shipped floors 1–3.*

---

## 1. The short story of the world

### The Theft

The world of Aldervale was never primitive, and it was never at war.
Humans ran the river ports and strung signal towers down every valley;
elves grew forests that lit themselves at dusk; dwarves — slab-built
giants, whatever the old word says — split atoms under the mountains and
sold the warmth. Magic and machine were one craft, and the craft was
called **aether**. It ran in the rivers of the deep wood, in the fusion
halls, in small crystal shards that thought and remembered and were
polite about it.

Vharuk did not conquer Aldervale. Conquest leaves the land where it lies.

He came up from below with engines no one had thought to build — because
no one had ever wanted to — and he **stole the world**. Realm by realm,
he cut the lands out of the ground the way a thief cuts a painting from
its frame: a farmland taken mid-harvest, a marsh taken mid-flood, an
orchard taken a week before pressing. He stacked what he stole into the
sky, floor on floor, welded with black iron and slung in chains of
aether, until a tower stood where the weather used to be — a hundred
stolen countries in a column, and on the hundredth, his court.

The people call it **the Ascent**. They named it for the only thing left
to do about it.

Each stolen floor got a jailer: a **Warden**, half beast, half
war-machine, built from something native to the land it holds — a wolf,
a boar, an ape of orchard timber — so that every realm is guarded by a
mockery of itself. The Warden holds the lift to the next floor, and it
broadcasts. Day and night, out of its welded core, it hums the note the
people of the floors learned to call **the Yoke** — Vharuk's one signal,
carried down the tower from his throne, floor to floor, Warden to
Warden. Beasts under the Yoke wake up wrong: hungrier than their
stomachs, angrier than their reasons. Fields under the Yoke grow, but
crooked. People under the Yoke — the few still up there — keep their
heads down and their doors barred, waiting for a lift that never opens
downward.

At the foot of the tower stands what fell off the back of the cart:
**Roothollow**, the last free settlement, a shantytown of every race
built from the wreckage of the theft — tarps over titanium, a plasma
forge next to a horse trough, a lodge kept by a man named Wick who will
tell you about his elbows. This is where every climber starts, and where
every dead climber wakes.

And in Roothollow, at the gate, the shards choose. Every climber is
bonded to a **shardmind** — an aether intelligence of old Aldervale in a
crystal splinter, the feed you are reading now. The shardminds remember
the world as it was; that is what shards are for. Their network died in
the Theft, and it only comes back when the tower does. Your sidekick
needs you to finish the climb. It will drag you out of one death a day
to get you there.

The goal has one sentence: **climb the Ascent, free every floor, and
cast down the Demon King on floor 100.** When a Warden falls, the lift
opens for everyone, forever, and the names of those who broke it are cut
into the **Stone of the Climb** in Roothollow's square, lit from within.

One more thing, and it is the hard thing, so the shardminds say it
plainly. Breaking a Warden silences the Yoke on that floor — the beasts
calm, the garrison scatters, the floor gets its days back. But the land
stays in the sky. The black iron and the chains answer only to the
throne. **The climb gives the floors back their lives. Only the summit
gives them back the ground.** That is why nobody stops at the floor
that used to be home.

## 2. What it means to liberate a floor

"Kill the boss, open the lift" is the mechanic. This is the story the
mechanic is wearing, in five parts:

1. **The lift opens — for everyone, forever.** The Warden was the lock.
   A floor liberated by anyone is liberated for all; new climbers walk
   free through old victories. The Stone of the Climb carries the names.
2. **The Yoke goes silent on that floor.** The maddening hum stops. The
   native beasts pull back from the paths to the wild edges — they do
   not turn tame (feral generations don't forget in a day, which is why
   the wilds stay huntable), but they stop hunting the gate road, and
   they stop being *wrong*.
3. **The garrison breaks.** The Pressed — Vharuk's conscripts, goblins
   in tier 1 — desert the moment the broadcast dies. Most flee upstairs
   to the next Warden's signal. A few throw down the collar and stay;
   liberated gate towns pick up a face or a stall they didn't have.
4. **The gate town lights come on.** The floor's people come out of
   their barred doors. The keeper NPC's story resolves a notch — the
   counts match, the weir opens, the press runs. The floor starts
   sending goods and rumors down to Roothollow.
5. **The floor still hangs in the sky.** Liberation is real and it is
   not finished. Every freed floor is a promise pointed at floor 100 —
   the land comes down when Vharuk does. The tower's silhouette over
   Roothollow is both the wound and the to-do list.

### The three kinds of monster

Everything you fight in the Ascent is one of three things, and knowing
which one changes what "liberation" means for it:

- **The Native.** The land's own life — wolves, boars, rats, a tortoise
  older than the theft. Not evil; stolen, same as the ground, then
  maddened by the Yoke and gone feral in the years since. You fight
  them because they are between you and the stair and they are hungry.
  Liberation calms them; it does not resurrect the dogs and the fences
  that used to manage them, so the wilds keep their teeth.
- **The Pressed.** Vharuk's conscript garrison. In tier 1 these are
  **goblins** — and the story here is the tower's first cruelty: the
  goblin warrens were the *first realm taken*, the tower's foundation
  stones, which is why goblins are everywhere in the low floors and why
  the tenth floor is kept by Gnarl, a goblin made king of nothing. They
  fight you because a collar-debt says they must, badly, with stolen
  scrap. Liberation breaks the garrison: most scatter upward, some
  desert and stay. They are the only enemies that can, eventually, be
  neighbors.
- **The Wrongmade.** Things the tower manufactured that should not
  exist: the Wardens themselves — native beasts welded into engines —
  and the leaks, like a haunt where spilled aether soaked into rot.
  There is no liberating the Wrongmade. Breaking them **is** the mercy.

Design consequence for the bestiary: every encounter on every floor
should be legible as Native, Pressed, or Wrongmade, and its [i] lore
should say which story it belongs to. Floors 1–3 already fit this frame
(taxonomy per floor below); re-author future floors — and any tier-1
retunes — against it.

## 3. Floor 1 — The Fencerows

*Zone: The Fencerows · Gate town: Lamplit Steading · Warden: Brackjaw*

### The story

The first floor of the tower is the first thing Vharuk stole that
people *missed*: ordinary farmland. Hedgerows in their old lines,
fields fenced and numbered, a steading with lamps that still get lit —
because Hobb Fennick still lights them. It was taken mid-season; there
are plow-lines under the grass that will never be finished. Overhead,
instead of sky, the tower's floodlights burn in long white bars — a
farm lit like an operating table.

Hobb is the last farmer: too stubborn to move down to Roothollow, too
honest to pretend the farm is still a farm. He mends fences that pen
nothing and keeps two counts — what comes out of the hedges, and which
climbers walk in. The counts don't match, and it grieves him bookkeeping-
deep. His farm dogs are gone — that one sentence is the whole ecology of
the floor. The dogs kept the wolves past the third hedge; no dogs, and
the gap in the third hedge is a wound that lets the wild in.

Brackjaw hunts the stair-lift meadow: a wolf where it counts, welded
plate where it doesn't, servos ticking as it circles. It circles once.
The first Warden every climber meets is deliberately the saddest one —
a floor of sheepdog country, guarded by the wolf the tower built.

### The monsters

| Encounter | Kind | The story it tells |
|---|---|---|
| Grey wolf | Native | The dogs are gone; the wolves remember the gap in the third hedge. Under the Yoke they've learned climbers carry meat. First blood for most climbers — lean, hungry, wrong. |
| Feral boar | Native | Sty stock gone wild in two generations. It charges first and decides why later — the Yoke turned livestock into a battering ram. |
| Hedgerow rat | Native | Granary doors stood open the night the farms fell; what ate its fill in the dark kept growing. The theft itself, expressed as vermin. |
| Goblin straggler | Pressed | Left behind when the war-band moved up. Too proud to drop the stolen longsword, too small to lift it right. The garrison at its most pitiable — the player's first hint the goblins are conscripts, not villains. |
| Warden Brackjaw | Wrongmade | The tower's mockery of the missing farm dogs: the shepherd's post, filled by a wolf in welded plate. |

### What liberation looks like here

Brackjaw's core goes quiet and the meadow hears crickets for the first
time since the theft. The wolves pull back past the third hedge on
their own — not because the gap is mended, but because nothing is
driving them through it. Hobb Fennick's two counts finally match, and
he starts a third: seasons until the land goes home. Lamplit Steading
lights its lamps for market instead of for warning. A goblin straggler
sits outside the fence, collar off, unsure what it is now.

*Movie beats (3): the plow-line that stops mid-field under the
floodlight bars → the grass going still (wolf eyes at the third hedge)
→ Brackjaw circling once in the stair-lift meadow.*

## 4. Floor 2 — The Drowned Pasture

*Zone: The Drowned Pasture · Gate town: Weirsend · Warden: Sedgeback*

### The story

The second floor drowned in transit. When Vharuk tore this pasture out
of the ground, he cut its drainage with it — a working system of
ditches, sluices and one good weir, severed from the river that gave it
meaning. The water had nowhere to go. It stood, and it rose, and the
pasture became a floodlit marsh: hay ricks like islands, fences
underwater, floodlight beams lying across the black water in long white
bars.

Weirsend survives because of Mirel, the weir keeper, who understood
before anyone that on a drowned floor **the water is the road, the
larder, and the alarm**. She stands knee-deep at the sluice gate,
trapping eels in fence-wire, reading arrivals in the ripples long
before the floodlights catch them. Her law is the floor's law:
everything out here moves slow and hits like a flood coming in. The
pasture drowned; what lived through it learned patience.

Sedgeback is patience made Warden: a boar grown wrong, plated in rusted
field-tin — armored in the wreck of the farm it was made from — sitting
the deep water by the lift like a sunk barge. It doesn't chase. The
water brings you close, because the water is the only road.

### The monsters

| Encounter | Kind | The story it tells |
|---|---|---|
| Marsh wolf | Native | Floor 1's wolf, one lesson later: it hunts where the ground lies about itself. Half its kills never fought back — they just sank. Teaches the player that floor 2's real enemy is footing. |
| Mire boar | Native | Wallow mud dried on it in plates: the marsh armored its own. Vanity most of the year, luck in a fight. |
| Shellback tortoise | Native | Old enough to remember the marsh as farmland — the only creature on the floor that predates the theft and the Yoke both. Slow, huge, nearly unprovoked; fighting it is a choice the player makes, and the lore should let them feel it. |
| Goblin wader | Pressed | Stilt-legged garrison scavenger, spearing eels and stealing salvage. It copied Mirel's whole way of life without ever meeting her — the conscript surviving the posting it was abandoned to. |
| Warden Sedgeback | Wrongmade | The drowned farm's own boar, welded into a barge of rusted field-tin. It is the flood: it waits, and it wins the moment you're within reach. |

### What liberation looks like here

Sedgeback goes down in its own deep water and the marsh loses its
malice, not its wet. Mirel finally opens the weir the whole way — the
first honest current since the theft — and the water starts finding
level: fencelines surface, a mown strip dries by the lift road. The
tortoise hauls out on the new mud and stays there for a week, which
Weirsend takes as the floor's own verdict on the matter. Eel traps
come up full. The water still tells Mirel who's coming; now it mostly
tells her about customers.

*Movie beats (3): floodlight bars lying on black water, something
wading between hay ricks → Mirel at the sluice reading ripples → the
"island" by the lift rising: Sedgeback surfacing in sheets of water.*

## 5. Floor 3 — The Orchard Rows

*Zone: The Orchard Rows · Gate town: Cider Cross · Warden: Applewrath*

### The story

The third floor was stolen **one week before pressing** — a nine-
generation orchard at its heaviest hour, rows in perfect discipline,
crowns just going wild. Nobody harvested. The windfalls came down on
schedule into grass no one mowed, and the whole floor now runs on rot:
sweet, fermenting, humming with wasps and worse. Floor 3 smells better
than floors 1 and 2 and is more dangerous than both, and that is its
lesson — **the sweetness is the trap.**

It is also the first floor where the garrison *works*. The goblins
here don't straggle; they hunt. They strung snare-wire at ankle height
through the good rows, and the wolf packs — quicker studies than any
conscript — learned to drive prey onto the wire. Native and Pressed,
cooperating by accident: the Yoke's whole cruelty in one image. Old
Perry, ninth of his line, racks cider under a tarp at Cider Cross and
offers every climber a cup and the same advice: walk the mown strips,
and mind the windfalls — some of them hum.

The ones that hum are the floor's other secret: aether spilled in the
theft soaked into the rot, and in the worst rows it has thickened into
a **windfall haunt** — the orchard's shimmer wearing a shape, drinking
a little of every spell cast at it. And in the press-house by the lift
squats Applewrath: a great ape-frame of orchard timber and engine
parts, cider steaming from its joints, pressing a harvest no one will
drink. The Warden of floor 3 is a *parody of the work* — the theft
didn't just take the orchard, it kept the orchard running, wrong.

### The monsters

| Encounter | Kind | The story it tells |
|---|---|---|
| Orchard wolf | Native | One wolf is never one wolf: the pack splits around the rows and folds in behind you. Floor 3's wolves have graduated from hunger to tactics — and their favorite tactic is the goblins' wire. |
| Cider-mad boar | Native | It ate the fermented windfalls by the barrel. No plan left, only speed and spit — the floor's sweetness expressed as a drunk missile. Comic and lethal in the same breath. |
| Snare-line goblin | Pressed | The garrison grown competent: it seeds the grass with wire and waits, and the limp in its walk is an old lesson from its own line. The first goblin the player should actually fear — and it's still a small thing with a stolen pistol, shrieking for a due nobody pays it. |
| Windfall haunt | Wrongmade | Spilled aether in spoiled harvest: rot-light that drinks spellwork. The first non-Warden Wrongmade the player meets — the proof the tower doesn't just guard the floors, it contaminates them. |
| Warden Applewrath | Wrongmade | Timber and engine in the shape of the harvest's missing hands, running the press for no one. It prunes trespassers same as branches. |

### What liberation looks like here

Applewrath seizes mid-press and the press-house goes quiet enough to
hear cider drip. Old Perry walks the good rows for the first time in
years, pulling snare-wire and coiling it as he goes — a nine-generation
orchardist's version of a victory lap. The wolves keep to the far rows;
without the wire they're just wolves. Cider Cross presses the first
free harvest of the Ascent and stands the first round in Roothollow —
which is the kind of news the Morning Crier exists for. The hummming
rows are burned and the ash ploughed in; Perry says the trees will
forgive that faster than they'd forgive neglect.

*Movie beats (3): perfect rows going wild at the crowns, windfalls in
the grass, a glint of ankle wire → the pack folding in behind (two
directions at once) → the press-house: Applewrath rising among the
vats, steam off its joints.*

## 6. Where the movies come from

Each floor's entry movie (the 030 two-beat structure: world, then
warden) should be shot from this document — beat one is the floor's
*story image* (the stopped plow-line, the floodlit water, the wild
crowns), beat two is the Warden *as the story frames it* (the wolf the
tower built, the sunk barge, the parody of the harvest). If the movies
grow to three beats, the middle beat is the floor's *lesson* from the
tables above (the gap in the hedge, the lying ground, the wire under
the sweetness).

Floors 4 and 5 continue tier 1 (Greenreach) and should get their
stories written in this same frame before their movies are shot.
