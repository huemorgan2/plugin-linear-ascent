# Linear Ascent — Story Ideas

*Three candidate storylines for the world's book — the narrative that does for
the Ascent what the great epics do for their worlds: takes the built canon
([world-lore.md](world-lore.md), the hundred floor files in
[floors/](floors/)) and threads three people through it until the world means
something. Pick one; the other two become mines for scenes and factions.*

**The fixed points every storyline shares** (from canon — none of these are
negotiable, all three ideas are built on them):

- **The three leads are the three player peoples:** a woman fighter of Men, an
  elf archer, a giant wizard (the "wizard-dwarves" of the old word). The story
  opens on the woman.
- **Their first meeting goes badly.** Trust is earned across floors, not given
  in chapter one. The story is the slow making of a fellowship, not the
  finding of one.
- **The climb is the plot.** Roothollow at the foot; a hundred floors; the
  boss floors (10, 20, 30 … 90) are act-breaks; Vharuk at 100. A freed floor
  *rises* — its people join the climb — so the host grows as the story does.
- **The mercy system is the moral engine.** Natives are cured by the blade,
  Wrongmade are evicted, and the Pressed — goblins, kobolds, orcs, imps —
  really die unless their collars are broken. What a character does about the
  Pressed *is* their character.
- **Other climbers exist.** Factions share the goal and fight over everything
  else: doctrine, gates, loot, order of liberation, and whether the tower
  should fall or be *kept*.

## Storyline 1 — "The Left-Behind"

*A revenge story that has to grow into a mercy story.*

**Logline.** A woman who fell off the stolen world as a child climbs it to
find the family that went up with the ground — and learns, floor by floor,
that the thing she came to kill is the only thing she must not become.

### The woman — Maren Vale

The night the Theft took her valley, Maren was six. Her mother threw her from
the hay-cart as the ground tore loose — threw her *off the world* — and the
last Maren saw of her family was the underside of her own farm, rising. She is
one of the left-behind: the folk who "fell off the back of the cart" and built
Roothollow out of the wreckage.

What she lost: everyone, and the ground itself — and, cruelest, the *address*.
She was six; she doesn't know which floor is hers. Every hedged, farmed,
lamplit floor of Men she enters, she walks it first in silence, measuring it
against a child's memory. She carries a bent fence-nail from home on a cord,
and a habit of counting things that don't add up.

How she started: raised by the trough-smith of Roothollow, pit-fighter at
fourteen, caravan guard on the freed low floors at nineteen, and a climber the
day a shardmind chose her at the gate — to her own disgust, since she wanted
no voice in her ear, only the climb. She fights like the pits taught her:
first, hard, and without ceremony.

### The meeting — and why it goes wrong

**Floor 2, the Rustwater Adit.** Maren has a kobold gang cornered and is
finishing them the pit way when an arrow takes the sword out of her hand.
**Fen Larchlight** — elf archer, lamplighter's line, doctrinaire
collar-breaker — will not stand by while surrendered Pressed are killed. The
kobold he saves bolts straight into the dark and brings back the garrison;
climbers die who need not have died. Each blames the other. Both are right.
That argument — *her dead friends against his freed slave* — is the engine of
their whole relationship, and it is never fully settled, only outgrown.

**Brammor of the Ninth Forge** — giant wizard — attaches himself to them on
the Counting Halls (11) uninvited, useful, and evasive. He is a mender of
aether-engines who asks too many questions about the tower's welds and answers
none about himself. The truth, which surfaces around the Frozen Sea (46) when
Fen finds a maker's-mark on tower-iron: the grav-chain that lifts the world is
built on *Brammor's own stolen work*. He is climbing to unmake the thing he
made. The reveal breaks the party — Fen walks — and the reconciliation, floors
later and at cost, is the story's midpoint hinge.

### The arcs

- **Maren: revenge → mercy.** She kills Pressed without flinching in act one.
  Then floor by floor the war shows her collared goblins guarding their own
  prisons, and at the Welding Halls (84) she finds where taken people of Men
  are made into soldiers — and must face that any welded thing she cuts down
  could be her own kin. Her climax is Malgrim (90): the freed man who chose
  the collar — everything she could become if the anger wins. Whether her
  family is ever found (a grave on a farm floor; a name in the muster-rolls
  at 90; a face in the Night Gardens at 96) is the book's cruelest decision,
  and should not be decided cheaply.
- **Fen: doctrine → judgment.** He begins with a rule for everything and
  learns, through the Court (98) where the willing cannot be freed, that
  mercy without judgment is just another way of not looking.
- **Brammor: guilt → authorship.** He hides, is found out, is forgiven badly
  and slowly — and ends the story using the maker's knowledge he was ashamed
  of to open the Last Door (99). The unmaking is his amends.

### The factions

- **The Cullers.** Climbers who kill everything — Pressed included — led by
  Serjeant Hale, the veteran who once pulled Maren out of a pit-fire and is
  the closest thing she has to a father. Her doctrine fight with Fen is also
  a loyalty fight with Hale, and Hale's end (turned on by his own after he
  culls a surrendered warren) is her act-two wound.
- **The Tollmen.** A company that "holds" freed gates and charges passage —
  legal nowhere, armed everywhere. Canon says a freed lift is free for all;
  the Tollmen are the test of whether the rising polices itself.
- **The Lamp Synod.** Fen's estranged kin: elves who demand the scattered
  lamp-woods be freed *first*, in order, and will divert or starve the climb
  to get it. Fen's break with them is his backstory; their siege of a gate
  the party needs is his act-three test.
- **Malgrim's Heralds.** The tower recruits. Some climbers are offered a
  collar of rank and take it — the willing, seeded through the host, surfacing
  at the worst moments. The traitor among the supporting cast should be
  someone the reader likes.

**Why this one:** the cleanest spine (one woman's loss → the whole world's),
the mercy system carries the theme natively, and every act-break boss doubles
as a station of her arc.
**Risk:** the missing-family thread must resolve *somewhere* or the engine
stalls; choose the floor and the answer early.

## Storyline 2 — "The Hundred Banners"

*A war epic about the rising itself — the gathering the tower was built to
prevent.*

**Logline.** A disgraced captain who led thirty climbers into a massacre is
dragged back onto the tower by a blackmailing elf and a guilt-ridden giant —
and must forge the squabbling peoples of a hundred floors into the one army
the Demon King cannot survive, while hunting the traitor who sold her company.

### The woman — Captain Ede Stonhal

Ede led the Shieldwall Company: thirty climbers, the best the low floors ever
mustered, broken at the Red Warcamp on floor 20. She came back down alone.
Canon already holds her wound: **Shieldwall's captives are the trapped folk of
floor 20** — her own people, alive, in Skarn's cages, for years, while she
drank her way to the bottom of Roothollow and trained other people's recruits
because she could no longer climb past floor 19.

What she lost: her company, her rank, and her nerve — but not, it turns out,
her people, which is worse, because they are *waiting*.

How she starts: refusing. The book opens with her turning the climb down
twice — and taking it the third time, not from courage but because **Rhovan**
shows her proof that the Warcamp was not a defeat. It was a *sale*.

### The meeting — a contract, not a fellowship

**Rhovan of the Grey Rides** — elf archer, agent of the Wake — did not come to
save Ede; he came to *use* her. The Wake wants the scattered elf-woods freed
on its own schedule, and a resurrected Shieldwall banner is the lever it
needs. He opens with blackmail (the proof of the sale, and the implication
she'll never see the rest of it unless she climbs) and lies by omission for
ten floors. **Vega Coalbrow** — giant wizard, forge-matron — joins at the
Anvil Commons (23) because the collar-steel on Skarn's cages carries *her
forge-mark*: stock she sold in good faith, or so she needs to believe. Three
people bound by leverage, guilt, and a ledger of the dead. The fellowship is
what grows in the cracks of the contract — the first time one of them does
something for another that no contract requires, mark it; that's the book
turning.

### The arcs

- **Ede: broken commander → general of the rising.** Act one is the return to
  floor 20 and the freeing of her own captives — the hardest reunion in the
  book, because the freed Shieldwall does not forgive her quickly, and some
  never do. From there the story widens: every freed floor sends fighters,
  and they arrive with grudges, doctrines, and eighty years of stacked-apart
  resentment. Ede's growth is learning that command of the rising is not
  penance for the company — it is the company, again, at scale, and this
  time she must be willing to be *survived*.
- **Rhovan: agent → deserter of his own faction.** The Wake's schedule will
  cost lives on non-elf floors; the floor where he disobeys it is the floor
  he becomes a person. His bow answers to him alone by act three.
- **Vega: absolution-seeker → armorer of the freed.** She learns her steel
  was not sold but *stolen* — and then learns the harder thing, that it
  doesn't matter; the collars are hers to break either way. She ends the
  story re-forging tower-iron into the rising's arms (rivet-steel, canon)
  and refusing to make one weapon more than the war needs.
- **The mystery spine:** who sold Shieldwall? The trail climbs with them —
  a Tollman ledger (30s), a Herald's cipher (60s), and the answer at the
  Herald's Gate (90): a name from Roothollow's own council, one of Malgrim's
  willing, seeded years before the book began.

### The factions (the real subject of this storyline)

- **The Wake** — elves-first, patient, secretive; funds the climb, steers it.
- **The Forge-Court** — giants who argue the tower should be *kept*: seize
  the engines, rule the column, put the floors down gently over a
  generation. Not villains; the most dangerous kind of ally, because
  half-right.
- **The Tollmen** — gate-holders and profiteers; they field the best-paid
  blades on the low floors and will fight the rising itself over a lift.
- **The Quiet** — keepers and floor-folk who *oppose* liberation, having
  survived reprisal before. The rising must win the freed, not just free
  them.
- **Malgrim's Heralds** — the tower's recruiters; the enemy's answer to the
  rising is always another willing collar.
- Set-piece beats: the **Banner War** among the barrow-floors (29–30), the
  **mead-hall parliament at the Frosthall** (60) where the alliance is
  actually forged (Jarl Hrimgar's freed hall as the rising's first capital),
  the muster before the **Herald's Gate** (90) where the hundred banners
  stand together for the first time — the exact image Vharuk built the tower
  to make impossible.

**Why this one:** it dramatizes the world-lore's own thesis (divide the many /
the gathering is the weapon) and gives every freed floor's people a face in
the host; the biggest canvas, closest to the "background book" feel.
**Risk:** ensemble sprawl — it needs the Shieldwall mystery as a spine or it
becomes a wiki with weather.

## Storyline 3 — "The Shard That Remembers"

*An intimate mystery about grief, memory, and the voice in the climber's ear.*

**Logline.** A weir-diver whose twin went up with the stolen marsh climbs the
tower bonded to a cracked shardmind that sometimes speaks in her sister's
cadence — and follows that voice up a hundred floors to a truth about the
shards, the Yoke, and what actually happened to the taken.

### The woman — Lio Marsh

Eel-trapper's daughter of the drowned pastures. The morning of the Theft, Lio
was under the weir, diving traps; her twin **Isa** was on the bank, shouting
something down through the water. The ground tore loose mid-shout. Lio
surfaced into a crater. She never heard the sentence's end — and the not-
hearing is the wound: twenty years later she still finishes that shout a
hundred different ways in her sleep.

What she lost: the other half of every memory. Twins keep one story in two
heads; hers is now half a story, and the tower has the rest.

How she started: salvage-diver in Roothollow's flooded quarter, famous for
staying down longer than the lamps last. She never meant to climb. Then, at
the gate, a *cracked* shardmind — one the Vault had written off as dead —
chose her, and its first words were in Isa's cadence.

### The meeting — one shard, three claimants

The crack in the shard is the meeting. At Roothollow's gate it does the thing
no shard does: it refuses to choose one climber. It names three. **Cael of the
Kennel Line** — elf archer, cold, transactional, whose family were taken to
the Pale Court's kennels (80) and whose whole climb is aimed at the Huntsman —
wants the shard because it alone can hear the Court's horns early. **Old
Mote** — giant wizard, Deep delver, the only living witness who was *under*
the world the night the engines rose — has followed this specific shard for
thirty years and trusts it more than he trusts people. Three strangers bonded
to one feed, hearing each other's worst moments narrated, sharing one
death-save a day between them: the fellowship as a forced marriage. They begin
by rationing the shard like water in a drought — the growth is the day one of
them *gives* their save away.

### The arcs

- **Lio: grief → release.** She climbs to find Isa; the mystery gives her
  Isa in pieces — a name in a tithe-ledger, a face in the Mirror Galleries
  (92), the truth in the Night Gardens or the Welding Halls. The hard,
  honest option this storyline should have the courage for: **Isa died in
  the lifting, and the shard caught her** — a drowning soul snatched into
  crystal by an aether-network dying at the same moment. The voice was
  never a ghost and never a lie: it is a *recording that loves her*. Lio's
  ending is not reunion but the finished sentence — the shout completed —
  and the strength to let the shard go silent.
- **Cael: hunter → keeper.** He gets his Huntsman at floor 80 and discovers
  revenge completes nothing; the hounds he frees — his family's line — need
  a kennel-keeper, not an avenger. His arc ends *below* the summit, which
  is its point.
- **Mote: witness → historian.** He carries the question the whole book asks
  — *what are the shards?* — and personally uncovers the answer at the
  Reactor Galleries (93): **the Yoke is broadcast through the bones of the
  shardminds' own dead network.** The tower runs on the stolen
  infrastructure of the things guiding every climber. The shards knew.
  Whether they concealed it from shame or strategy is the book's late-night
  argument — and the reason the fellowship's last act of trust is choosing
  to believe their own splinter anyway.

### The factions

Smaller and stranger than storyline 2's armies — this book's factions fight
over *truth*, not gates:

- **The Vault** — the shard-cult of Roothollow: archivists who bank, rank,
  and (some whisper) *breed* shardminds, and who want Lio's cracked one back
  at any price.
- **The Silent Bows** — climbers who cut their shards out and climb deaf,
  convinced the voices are the tower's second Yoke. After floor 93 the book
  must admit they were half right.
- **Relic-hunters** — freelance divers of dead floors who strip the taken
  world for sale; Lio's old trade, gone rancid, wearing her old friends'
  faces.
- **The Heralds**, as ever, buying the willing.

**Why this one:** the most personal; it aims the story at the game's most
distinctive fixture — the sidekick voice in the feed — and gives the shardmind
a stake worthy of canon ("your sidekick needs you to reach the top"). Smallest
cast, deepest cuts.
**Risk:** quieter than the other two; it needs the 93 revelation and the
Huntsman hunt to keep the middle floors hot.

## Choosing

All three keep the same skeleton — woman first, bad meeting, growth across the
boss-floor act-breaks, factions with a common goal and crossed knives, Malgrim
as the dark mirror, the Champion at 99 as the answered prayer, the descent
after 100 as the true ending. They differ in what the climb *means*:

| | Meaning of the climb | Scale | Spine |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The Left-Behind | A person unlearning revenge | Personal → epic | The missing family |
| 2. The Hundred Banners | A world learning to gather | Epic | Who sold Shieldwall |
| 3. The Shard That Remembers | Grief, and what memory owes the dead | Intimate | What the shards are |

They are also compatible: 2's factions can furnish 1's world; 3's shard-truth
can sit under either as a late revelation. The next step is to pick a lead
storyline and write the chapter outline against the floor list — one beat per
tier of ten, act-breaks on the Warden floors.
