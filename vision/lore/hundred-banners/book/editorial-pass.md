# The read-through & philosophy pass

*Applied after all 47 units are drafted. Two jobs: (1) verify the book ties —
every seed pays, every payoff was seeded; (2) lay in the keystone lines — the
words a reader carries out of the book, the way Gandalf's "many that live
deserve death" is carried out of Tolkien. Keystones are planted early, echoed
late, and always spoken in character. No more than one per chapter; a book
that moralizes on every page teaches nothing.*

## The keystones

**K1 — On judgment (the book's Gandalf-SEAT line — carrying that weight,
never that shape).** Sliver, chapter 3, the night after Ede kills the
surrendered kobold. Planted small, paid in full at chapter 36 when Ede hands
Berrin's judgment to the thirty.

> *"I have carried one-and-forty climbers, and every one was certain of
> something that killed somebody. A sword is quick, Ede Stonhal. It answers
> today's question and deafens you to tomorrow's. Spend your certainty
> slowly. It is the only coin in this tower that does not come back."*

Echo at ch. 36: Ede, giving judgment away — "I have learned to spend my
certainty slowly." No attribution needed; the reader will hear the shard.

**ANTI-COPY RULE (all keystones, all chapters):** a keystone must carry the
weight of the great lines without their shape. If a line scans like a famous
quote with the nouns swapped — the Gandalf mercy speech, "words are wind,"
"the man who passes the sentence," any of them — it is rewritten from its
own image until a well-read reader hears no one but us.

**K2 — On fear (the Quiet).** A keeper of the Gallows Fair, chapter 9:

> *"The tower is only the stone, captain. The prison is what we learned to
> want. You can break stone. Now break the wanting."*

Echo at ch. 40: Vharuk's offer is precisely "the wanting" — and the hundred
banners answering first is the wanting, broken.

**K3 — On gathering (the thesis).** Jarl Hrimgar at the parliament, ch. 21:

> *"Alone, each of us was a floor. Stacked, we were his tower. Standing
> shoulder to shoulder — for the first time in eighty years — we are a
> stair."*

Echo: the muster at the Herald's Gate (ch. 35 opening) — "not a tower. A
stair."

**K4 — On the chosen collar (the dark mirror).** Malgrim, ch. 36:

> *"You call it a collar. I call it the only hand that ever chose me. You
> would give me your freedom? I have watched your freedom, captain. It is a
> debt that walks."*

And Ede's answer — the book's rebuttal, earned across forty chapters: *"So
is love. Climb with us anyway."* (He refuses; that's the tragedy.)

**K5 — On making (the forge-oath).** Vega's mother's oath, first spoken
whole at ch. 38 to Ulfar, half-quoted at ch. 6 when Vega finds her mark:

> *"We do not own what we make. We owe it."*

**K6 — On time (the wyrm).** Cindermaw, ch. 19, closing the parley:

> *"I pity him. He built the one tower tall enough to see the whole world
> from — and in eighty years he has never once looked."*

**K7 — On smallness (the Sam-line).** Nix, ch. 20, on the Trapdoor Fields,
and again — changed — in the epilogue:

> Ch. 20: *"Big folk count floors. Little folk count steps. Steps are
> truer — a floor can lie to you, but a step is either under your foot or
> it isn't."*
> Epilogue: *"Turns out you climb a tower the same way you leave one.
> One true step, then the next."*

**K8 — On the banner (what it means).** Dun Harrow, ch. 8, handing it over:

> *"A banner's not the cloth, captain. The cloth is what we bury it in when
> it dies. A banner is the people walking behind it — and they're stood up
> again, so it's alive again, so carry it."*

Echo: epilogue's last image — the banner leaning on the horse trough while
the people it means drill in the square.

## Early hints to strengthen (plant → payoff)

- **The prologue boy is Berrin.** The boy should be *counting and listing*
  what rises — a born ledger-keeper — and should keep one stolen thing
  (a bakery's brass weight?) rather than grieve. Bread on the wind. Payoff:
  chs. 8 (courier smelled of Roothollow bread), 29 (granary), 33 ("Bread"),
  34 (unmasking), prologue re-read.
- **Sliver re-chooses (ch. 2).** Shards do not re-choose; give Sliver one
  hesitant, ashamed beat at the gate ("I am not supposed to be able to want
  things") → pays at ch. 30 (its confession) and 38 (alone-lit).
- **Aldis Bright.** The Stone of the Climb (ch. 1) should carry, worn nearly
  smooth at its head, the first name ever cut: A. BRIGHT — nobody remembers
  who that was → pays at ch. 39 when the Champion steps out of the door.
- **Hunt-law rhyme.** A skipping-rhyme of Men children (ch. 3 or 9): "run
  from the horn and the horn runs after; walk to the horn and the horn falls
  silent" → pays at ch. 28 when Ede walks toward the horns.
- **Hale keeps his gate.** Ch. 3: Hale's first rule of the pits — "lose
  your sword, lose your teeth, but keep your gate" → pays at ch. 22, the
  south door. (Never the literal phrase "hold the door" — that one belongs
  to another story and readers know it.)
- **The Break-collar cost ladder.** One collar, bought dear (ch. 4) → a
  squad's worth (ch. 32) → the mass casting (ch. 35). Verify each step
  states the cost so 35 feels earned, not free.
- **Zephyra's lateness.** Ch. 25, her parting words should promise wind
  "when the wind profits" → 35's late gales become a kept, worthless word.
- **"A ledger entry does not retire."** Must appear in interlude 1 (already
  in outline) so Malgrim's use of it at ch. 36 lands as an old rule.

## Read-through checklist (per part)

1. Seams: each chapter opens where the outline's previous entry left off.
2. POV discipline: no head-hopping; nothing the POV couldn't know.
3. Berrin: warm in every appearance before 34; zero winks; reread his
   dialogue as the traitor and confirm it recasts cleanly (dramatic irony,
   not cheating).
4. Names/facts against style-guide anchors (Dun one-armed from ch. 7 on;
   Calyne enters at 15, dies at 34; forty banners at ch. 16, seventy at 31,
   a hundred at 35).
5. No firearms, no modern idiom, ⁂ scene breaks, ~78-col wrap.
6. Word counts within targets; battle chapters carry their length.
