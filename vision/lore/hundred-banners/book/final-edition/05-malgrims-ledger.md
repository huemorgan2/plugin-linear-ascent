# Malgrim's Ledger, as Recovered

*Recovered from the writing-stand of the drum-throne on the day after
the Battle of the Herald's Gate, and carried down to Roothollow with
the rest of what the fall left legible. The hand is fine, and never
hurries, and never blots. The book ends mid-sentence; what was written
after it ends is set down in a note at the close.*

⁂

Item: the tenth floor is written off. The crown was issued as a joke
at the goblins' expense, and the joke has stopped being funny. The
King of Nothing was offered the enemy's word and refused it for the
crown; enter the refusal to his credit, for a creature that dies
keeping a worthless thing has at least understood keeping. One
cup-bearer unaccounted. Cup-bearers are not a line.

Item: the Warcamp is spent. Write off the garrison, the drums, and the
Warlord entire. Skarn priced his collar as a rank and defended it as
one, and I enter the flaw so it is not repeated: never issue a collar
to a creature that wants it. I am told there is one exception. I keep
his file myself.

Item: the woman climbs.

Item: sixty-three units of aged inventory recovered by the enemy at
the twentieth floor. I decline to enter a loss. Grief is a bond that
matures; every cage she opens, she carries. Let her portage.

Item: the gate-toll investment performs. Three factions taught to
hate one another at their own expense is the cheapest garrison on
these books. Recommend no interference.

Item: a goblin observed at the head of parleys. Entered under
sundries. There is no line in any ledger for what he is spending, and
a currency we cannot mint we cannot counterfeit either. Watch him.

Note to the willing below: feed her the trail slowly. Small findings,
honestly come by, at decent intervals; nothing that cannot bear her
checking, for she will check. A hope led is a hope steered. And
grieve well when the trail warms. You have always grieved
beautifully. It is why I collared you.

⁂

Item: three casks of oak-gall ink, four gross of candles, one pen.
The pen is for me. A herald's hand must never be seen to tire.

Item: contrary to my minute of the spring, I ordered the Tollmaster's
houses burned at the thirtieth floor, to starve the line. The burning
is a bad purchase: it has bought the enemy a quartermaster, for the
Tollmaster walked into their camp with his strong-boxes on his back
and his greed intact, and greed is the one recruit that never
deserts. Enter the loss against my name. A book that spares its
keeper is not a book.

⁂

Item: the Kingsbarrow account is closed at a loss. The drowned king
was not carried by steel; he was carried by grief given precedence,
and the mound, being honest, fell. They mourn well, and have learned
to burn grief in good order. Recommendation to the throne: deny them
occasions. They must not mourn at scale.

Item: the Broodthrone. The Matriarch kept her contract to its final
clause and stood her court aside while the crown-iron broke. I
decline to enter treason; a kept contract is the only piety the old
powers have, and she was ours by welding, never by word. Write the
fortieth relay off in full, and note for the archive: we held no
paper on her. See that this is never again true.

Item: they have taken a census. Nine peoples, forty banners, one
goblin. I confess the last line moved me; caution to my successor: it
will move you also, and it is meant to. They have added themselves
up, with chalk, on our own slate — the one arithmetic this house was
built to prevent. Against their clerks, nothing; clerks can be
replaced and martyrs cannot. Against their occasions, everything.

Item: our friend below requests extraction. Denied. His safety has
never lain in appearing on lists but in keeping them; no man is so
unseen as the one holding the pen. He asks what he is to us after so
many years, and I pay him the wage of the truth: he is the best-kept
entry in my books. A ledger entry does not retire.

Item, for my own account: when the fortieth's crown-iron fell, the
floor wept for relief. I remember the feeling. I chose against it
once, and every year the choosing comes easier. Let the record show
the collar is not heavy. What is heavy is choosing, and the collar
ends the choosing. In this one respect I envy nobody climbing.

⁂

Item: the Wyrm has let them pass. It predates our books and has said
so, to their faces. It pities the King. I have entered stranger
assets, but none that kept.

Item: the woman now keeps a roll of everyone who has ever climbed
before. Our friend keeps the roll. File under: economy.

⁂

Item: the feast bill comes due and is worth it. Forty servers and
twelve bows, spent to the last man, as designed. Against that column:
one standard-bearer, one serjeant of the old doctrine, a charter
scorched, a parliament that now tastes its own soup, and a general
who has stopped sleeping. I have bought worse for more.

Item: let no one below read cruelty in it. Cruelty is waste, and I
keep clean books. We charged sixty-eight lives for a truth the tower
usually prices in nations: a gathered world can be broken at one
table. They will not learn it. Hope never audits.

Item: the woman doubles her guard and guards the wrong thing — the
serving-lines, the galleries, the doors. Names were never our way in.
Love is. No taster sips at that.

Item: our friend performs grief convincingly. He always did; I
collared him for it. He wept for the standard-bearer and the weeping
was, in the fullest sense, sincere. That is the whole art of a
willing instrument: nothing in it is false but the sum.

Item: they climb for the chains. Advise the sky.

⁂

Item: the sky has answered, in her fashion. The Queen priced her
gales at a throne we never promised her, was refused, and scattered a
third of their host at her own expense. Enter under gifts. I would
not have spent an army to buy a delay; she spent theirs.

Item: the Pale Court has fallen. The Huntsman kept better books than
the Queen — every course entered, every quarry weighed, a thousand
years in fair hand — and it availed him nothing, because his ledger
carried one bad clause from before our time, and the woman found it
and walked in through it, toward the horns. The stolen realms are a
closed column. Assets that were liabilities dressed in crowns.

Item: they muster below the outworks, seventy banners, thirty-one
peoples, one charter. I have read the charter. It is short, which is
how I know it is dangerous. Long laws are written to be escaped.

Item: provision to the outworks doubled; the drums to beat without
interval. What the tower owns, the tower may spend, and I intend to
spend it by weight.

Item: our friend reports from their muster. The bread arrived before
the army, as ever, and the hounds took no notice of him. The
kennel-clerks swear a hound cannot be deceived about a man; either
they flatter their stock, or my friend is by now so wholly the thing
he pretends that there is nothing false left in him for a dog to
find. On quiet evenings it unsettles even me.

Item: the siege begins when they touch the first rampart. Open a new
page. Head it: expenditures.

⁂

EXPENDITURES

Item: the First Outwork. Their line of battle grows as it advances;
every collar their small squads break deserts, not to them, to the
future. There is no countermeasure in the manuals. The manuals never
imagined an enemy whose sappers dig with mercy.

Item: the Welding Halls are lost, and among the aged inventory a unit
recognized an officer of the enemy and said one word. The word is a
name I know. For the first time in the keeping of this book I have
considered a false page, and I enter the temptation instead of the
lie, which is how I know the account is ending.

Item: the spymaster of the Wake is dead, proving what she was: right.
Enter her with respect. Our friend is unmasked and climbing to me
with nothing but the collar. I have opened the gate. What else is a
keeper for.

Item: he asked, at the gate, what his service purchases at the last.
I told him gently, because it is true and gentleness costs nothing:
it purchases what he asked for on the night he knelt. To be
necessary. He is. Until the account closes.

⁂

Item: the day. They have brought a bell.

Item: I know what it is for. The priests sang into the casting, and
the smith's mark on it is the mark from the collar-steel, bought
back. There is nothing in the manuals, because no army before this
one ever cast its siege-engine as a mercy.

Item: drums doubled. The drum says what it has always said, and this
morning, for the first time in thirty years, I have written down what
it says and looked at the sentence.

Item: in my own rear ranks, stillness is spreading, in fifties, in
knots, like calm in a pot taken off the fire. My clerks look to me,
and I beat the drum, which is my post.

Item: the carriage moves. The singers step over their dead and do not
lose the line. Set it down fairly: they are the best troops I have
ever seen, and none of them is armed.

Item: the wager of the day is now plain, and I find, setting it down
in a fair hand while the yards decide it: their word against my drum.
The drum says there is nothing under the collar but the drum. The
bell proposes to ask each man singly what

⁂

*Here the book ends. Every leaf after it is blank, save two writings
never entered in the ledger proper. The first came down to the
rising's fires at moonrise:*

*Item: your bell. I heard it. Come up in the morning and tell me what
you think it proves, and I will tell you what I know it costs. Bring
the thirty. I have something of theirs to return — a ledger entry
does not retire.*

*The second, a page folded once, was put into the general's hands at
noon the next day by two freed clerks, with the ledger's last
delivery walking bound between them:*

*Item: delivered to the woman who climbs, one quartermaster, used. He
asked me a second time for extraction. I keep my answers: a ledger
entry does not retire. Item, final: the account passes to you,
general. See that it balances better in your keeping than it did in
mine.*

*The muster-rolls of the freed record the keeper of this book, at
the general's own instruction: a man of the forty-third, a tallyman,
taken twice; and that the second taking was the crime.*
