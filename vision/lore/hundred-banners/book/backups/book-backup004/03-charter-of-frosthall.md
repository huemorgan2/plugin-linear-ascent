# The Charter of the Frosthall, as Signed

*The original lies at Roothollow under glass. One corner is scorched, and
the scorch is three nights younger than the ink. This is the surviving
fair copy, collated against the original, marks and all. It is short. The
Herald of the King, reading it that spring, observed that short laws are
written to be kept.*

## The preamble

Being the agreement of the parliament of the banners, met at the
Frosthall upon the sixtieth floor, in the deep winter of the second year
of the rising; argued from first-frost to the signing; and agreed in the
whole hall on this, if at first on nothing else:

That the world was one country before the Theft, and shall be one
country after, and that no floor climbs alone.

## The first article — of the engines

The engines of the tower, the lifting-engines at its anchors and the
core that feeds them, shall be neither kept nor scrapped by any party
to this charter, whatever its strength on the day. Their fate shall be
decided by a freed world's own council, met on the ground, after the
throne; and until that council sits, nothing is kept, nothing is
scrapped, and no hand rests on any lever it was not sent to pull.

## The second article — of the gates

Every lift of a freed floor stands open to all, for nothing, forever.
No toll, no chain, no ledger at any freed gate; and no man is turned
back down a stair he is able to climb.

## The third article — of the stores

Gilder Voss, called the Tollmaster-General, is made quartermaster-
general of the rising: every store, train, granary, ferry, and toll-
house of the freed tower under his hand, at a fixed honest fee, haggled
for two hours as a point of honor and entered in the margin. His
accounts stand open to the parliament and are audited twice in each
year. The clerks minuted the general's remark on the appointment: that
there is no better warden for a lock than the man who has picked it.

## The fourth article — of the Pressed

The freed Pressed — the goblins, the kobolds, the imps, and every folk
the tower collared and the rising freed — hold a seat among the peoples
of this parliament, in their own right, and in their own names; the
names, where a name was lacking, being their own to choose, and no
one's to spell for them.

*A fifth matter, the woods, was put to the hall and failed, and nothing
failed is in a charter. It is minuted here because the mover of the
failed clause gathered her papers and stayed, which the hall remembered
later, and was ashamed.*

## The signatures

Jarl Hrimgar signed first, in the deep script, the pen a splinter in
his fist. Vega and Ulfar Coalbrow signed together, brother and sister,
one pen between them and the pen the worse for it. Mistress Calyne
signed small and plain. Gilder Voss signed with a flourish that used
more ink than his whole article.

Hobb Fennick, sent up from the first floor of the world with his counts
under his arm, signed for the keepers in a farmer's careful hand,
checking the letters twice; and after him the keepers of forty floors,
each in their own manner. Of those who could not write, the clerks
described the marks, as instructed:

- a tally-cord of the freed kobolds of the Adit, knotted once for each
  of them, tied through a slit cut in the margin of the page;
- the character a kobold of the winter camps had lately been taught,
  set down backwards, its owner declining to have it recopied, the
  name being hers whichever way it faced;
- the burned print of a smith's glove, from a forge-hand of the Gear
  Galleries who owned a name but did not yet trust a pen with it;
- and one paw-print, in hearth-black, which the hall-folk swore a
  cured hearth-wolf left unbidden, walking the table while the clerks
  supped. The clerks let it stand. Nothing in the four articles
  forbids it, and nobody wished to argue precedence with the dog.

Rhovan of the Grey Rides signed a name that may or may not have been
his; the clerks were assured it would verify, and did not try. Nix drew
the character he had settled on for himself, slowly, with his tongue
between his teeth; a tally-cord was heard being counted under the seat,
and is not minuted, and is minuted now. Dun Harrow signed one-handed,
steadying the page with the butt of the standard.

Berrin Or signed last but one, in a quartermaster's neat fair hand. The
compilers observe that it is the same hand as the Frosthall seating-
list, and let the two facts stand beside each other, as a charter would
want.

Ede Stonhal signed last. Fifty-eight banners hung in the rafters over the
wet ink, and the draught from the smoke-hole moved them together, and
the clerks, having nothing further to record, recorded that.
