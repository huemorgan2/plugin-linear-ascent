# From Malgrim's Ledger

Item: the Kingsbarrow account is closed at a loss. The drowned king was
not carried by steel; he was carried by grief given precedence. They
stood at his moat and named their dead until his court conceded the
deeper mourning, and the mound, being honest, fell. I can fault no
one's conduct. They simply mourn well. Grief is a fuel like any other,
and they have learned to burn it in good order. Recommendation to the
throne: deny them occasions. They must not be permitted to mourn at
scale.

Item: the Broodthrone. The Matriarch kept her contract to its final
clause and stood her court aside while the crown-iron was broken. I
decline to enter this as treason. A kept contract is the only piety the
old powers have, and she was ours by welding, never by word. Write the
fortieth relay off in full, and note for the archive: we held no paper
on her. See that this is never again true of any floor we cannot
afford.

Item: they have taken a census. Nine peoples, forty banners, one
goblin. I confess the last line moved me, and I enter the feeling here
as a caution to my successor: it will move you also, and it is meant
to. A census is not a list. It is the mirror a people builds in order
to see itself, and a people that has seen itself cannot afterward be
unseen. They have added themselves up. That is the one arithmetic this
house was built to prevent, and they have done it with chalk, on our
own slate. Recommendation: against their clerks, nothing — clerks can
be replaced and martyrs cannot. Against their occasions, everything.

Item: our friend below requests extraction. Denied. I have written to
him kindly, for he serves best when he is calm. He fears the wall of
names; I have reminded him that his safety has never lain in appearing
on lists but in keeping them, and that no man is so unseen as the one
holding the pen. He asks what he is to us after so many years. I will
pay him the wage of the truth: he is the best-kept entry in my books. A
ledger entry does not retire.

Item, for my own account: when the crown-iron fell, the fortieth floor
wept for relief. I remember the feeling. I chose against it once, and
every year since, the choosing has come easier. Let the record show the
collar is not heavy. What is heavy is choosing, and the collar ends the
choosing. In this one respect I envy nobody climbing.
