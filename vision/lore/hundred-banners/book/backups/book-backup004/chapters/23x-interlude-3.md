# From Malgrim's Ledger

Item: the feast bill comes due and is worth it. Forty servers and
twelve bows, spent to the last man, as was always the design — a
willing collar that comes home is a story, and stories are the one
cargo I will not ship. Against that column: one standard-bearer, one
serjeant of the old doctrine, a charter scorched, a parliament that
now tastes its own soup, and a general who has stopped sleeping. I
have bought worse for more.

Item: let no one below read this as cruelty. Cruelty is waste, and I
keep clean books. The kindness was ours: a gathered world is a world
that can be broken at one table. We offered them, at supper, the
lesson the King offers by architecture — that trust at scale is a
door left open — and we charged only sixty-eight lives for a truth
the tower usually prices in nations. They will not learn it. Hope
never audits.

Item: the woman doubles her guard and guards the wrong thing. She
watches the serving-lines now, the galleries, the doors. She has
put tasters at the benches. She has, I am told, rebuilt her burned
rolls out of the mouths of the living, nine days of oath and ink —
admirable, useless. Names were never our way in. Love is. No
taster sips at that.

Item: the spymaster and the archer have opened their books to each
other. This was foreseen, and is convenient. Two
hunters sharing one lamp cast one shadow; while they walk their
paper trail of hands and ledgers, the hand itself rests where it
has always rested — nearer the woman than any paper reaches, and
thanked daily for its nearness.

Item: our friend performs grief convincingly. He always did. I
collared him for it. The others below wear the collar the way a
beast wears a yoke, resenting the plough; he wears his the way a
clerk wears a wedding-ring — a thing chosen once, in daylight, and
never since regretted aloud. He wept for the standard-bearer, and
the weeping was, in the fullest sense, sincere. That is
the whole art of a willing instrument: nothing in it is false but
the sum.

Item: he has asked again what his service purchases, at the last.
Tell him what I tell every entry in this book, gently, because it
is true and because gentleness costs nothing: it purchases what he
asked for on the night he knelt. To be necessary. He is. No man in
the tower is more necessary than our friend, and no man will be,
until the day the account closes.

Item: they climb for the chains. Advise the sky.
