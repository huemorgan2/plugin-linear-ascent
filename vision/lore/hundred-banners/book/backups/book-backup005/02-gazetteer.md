# A Gazetteer of the Hundred Floors

*Compiled at Roothollow in the first year after the fall, from the reports of
climbers and the letters of the keepers, this gazetteer sets down each floor
of the Ascent as it was found and as it was left. Where the accounts
disagree, the keeper's word is kept.*

## Floors 1–20 · The Debt

**1 · The Fencerows.** Hedged farm country of Men, a whole valley of meadow
taken mid-hay under bars of light that never set. The field-folk barred
themselves in Lamplit Steading under Hobb Fennick, who counted the climbers
into the hedges against the ones who came out. The warden Brackjaw, a wolf
welded into plate, held the stair-meadow. When it fell, Hobb's counts
matched at last.

**2 · The Rustwater Adit.** A slab of the Giants' mountainside cut off at
the mine-mouth, still weeping orange iron-water, its ore-carts halted on
rails that ended in open air. The miners of Lampfall held on under Bergda
Ironkettle, pit-foreman, who read the mountain by the taste of its water.
Rustmaw, a pit-hound with an ore-crusher's jaw, hunted the flooded drift.

**3 · The Drowned Pasture.** Water-meadow taken mid-flood and never let
drain; hay-ricks stood as islands and the weir went on working, keeping the
wrong things wet. The trappers of Weirsend lived in punts under Old Nan
Sedge, who had not been dry in a year. Sedgeback, a boar under weir-iron,
wallowed the mill-pool. The old pasture tortoise outlasted it all, and was
left in peace.

**4 · The Lightless Glade.** An elf-wood whose trees once lit themselves,
put out in a night; past the first branches the dark was total. The
lamplighters of Lanternroot hoarded their last wicks under Ithren of the
Wicks, who carried an unlit lantern he refused to abandon. Palegleam, a pale
stag crowned in a cage of false light, stalked the wood. One lamp in the
Snuffed Hollow never went out.

**5 · The Flooded Mine.** The galleries below the Adit, sunk in black water
with the great pumps drowned beside them. The pump-crews of Pumpstead
rationed air in the dry levels under Durn Sumphand, master of pumps.
Sumplock, an eel welded through with a pump-valve maw, ruled the flood. A
steady knocking came from behind the Last Dry Gallery's sealed door, and was
answered in time.

**6 · The Threshold Dark.** The first true dark of the climb, a
guano-floored vault where the floodlights failed and the under-world began.
The delvers of Lastlight kept to the last lit stone under Sable Lastlamp,
who knew the vault blind. Duskspin, a sentinel spider spinning steel silk,
walled the stair. The Chimney held one grey smear of borrowed daylight.

**7 · The Orchard Rows.** Cider country taken a week before pressing, mile
on mile of windfall fermenting under the lights, the air drunk with it. The
pressers of Cider Cross kept the mother-tree's true grafts alive under Cesta
Pommet. Applewrath, a boar in press-iron and barrel-hoops, bedded in the
deep windfall. The old press-house held a single paper nest the size of its
rafters.

**8 · The Ashline.** Grey ash dunes the three realms called worthless, home
to nomads who answered no crown. Kesh of the Grey Wells kept Greywell's
water by hard law. The warden Cinderhide was a Waste-born ogre with its
collar fused to its skull — a conscript the tower made un-savable, and the
grimmest early kill. A whole tent-caravan lay preserved dry under one dune.

**9 · The Beacon Field.** A signal-heath of humming pylons flashing false
code, every light saying come here and none of them meaning safety. The
keepers of Pylon Rest shuttered against the strobe under Wick Harlow,
half-deaf from the hum. Glarefang, a wolf maned in strobing lamps, hunted
inside its own flicker. A grounded sky-skiff lay in the heather where a
false beacon brought it down.

**10 · The Kingsfield.** The muster-meadow where Men once raised their
hosts, frozen mid-gathering under a hundred rotting standards. Serjeant
Mella Rung of Bannerline kept a true color folded under her coat for years.
The warden was **Gnarl, the Goblin King** — a goblin crowned by the tower to
mock the field, welded to a throne of banner-poles, deadly and a punchline
and aware of both. The color flew on the Standard-Elm at last.

**11 · The Counting Halls.** The Giants' counting-houses, halls of
ledger-shelves and coin-dust where gate-sized abacuses hung frozen mid-sum.
The clerks of Ledgerstone kept doomed tallies under Tolla Brasspen, who
would not stop balancing. Brassbone, a brass beetle that counted every blow
it took and answered in kind, guarded the vault. When the theft was undone,
the last ledger closed even.

**12 · The Shearing Downs.** Bare chalk sheep-country taken at the shearing,
fleeces half-clipped, the wind never stopping. The shepherds of Woolgate
counted a flock the wind had scattered under Ewan Woolgate, who knew the
count was hopeless and kept it anyway. Ramsteel, a half-clipped ram helmed
in flint and steel, charged the combes. The Long Barrow beacon brought the
scattered in.

**13 · The Eaves.** The torn outer edge of the elf-wood, stranded a full
tier from its own forest, humming with the dead signal of a lamp-network
long snuffed. The edge-elves of Lastlamp kept a lonely watch under Nael
Edgewatch. Duskhide, an edge-stag racked with dead antennae, threw the loop
into the gloom. The Fallen Span still hung by one cable over the marsh
below.

**14 · The Gear Galleries.** Machine-halls the size of towns, belts and
flywheels turning on captured aether and carrying nothing. The machinists of
Sprocket Row oiled them anyway under Grib Sprocket, who kept the great
flywheel balanced by ear. Gearhide, a pit-fighter's fury on a frame of
meshing cogs, set the stair's cadence wrong. The first silence, when the
clutch was thrown, was worth the fight.

**15 · The Cable Roots.** A cavern strung floor to roof with cable so old it
looked grown, live and spitting blue static. The cable-crews of Wiregate
slept on dead lines under Fend Wiregate, one hand scarred white. Wirefang, a
troll grafted with live bundles, re-wove the stair's curtain faster than it
could be cut. The Junction Spire wore a crown of blue fire.

**16 · The Hollow Lanes.** Droving country worn shoulder-deep into
holloways, hedgebanks meeting overhead in green tunnels that closed behind a
traveler. The drovers of Hedgeharbor were penned by their own hedges under
Cobb Hedgeharbor, who no longer trusted his staff's notches. Thornhide, a
bull-boar hided in living holly, blocked the lane. A lane-map was scratched
behind the Halfway Inn's bar by a hundred hands.

**17 · The Glass Flats.** A plain of lightning-glass where every distance
lied and the mirages had thickened to near-solid. The gleaners of Shardside
rationed water and truth under Sira Shardside, who walked the flats
blindfolded by choice. Glassjaw, a mirror-scaled salamander fighting from a
dozen false positions, held the bluff. A merchant caravan stood fused whole
in the black glass.

**18 · The Millrace.** Mill-country taken at full flow, every wheel turning
and every stone grinding nothing, the water undercutting the ground it ran
through. The millers of Wheelhouse held on under Pell Wheelhouse, who kept
the great wheel true because a wheel let go crooked would tear the mill
down. Milldrown, a boar made a wheel-engine, rode the head-race. Afterward
the floor smelled of bread.

**19 · The Fusion Vaults.** The deep-forge's fuel-store, racked with glowing
aether-rods in a radiant dusk that sickened everything living in it. The
vault-crews of Half-Light counted their years by exposure under Hesk
Half-Light, grey-faced. Coreburn, a forge-remnant welded round a cracked
rod, sealed the blast-door with its own heat. The Breach stayed shut: half a
crew fused in the slag, a tomb no one reopened.

**20 · The Red Warcamp.** A seized mine-gallery pitched over with the tents
and drum-towers of the Red Orc host, the floor's own people caged beneath as
labor and hostage. Dara Shieldwall passed word between the pens and waited.
The warden was **Warlord Skarn**, himself first-taken, a conscript made a
general; his caged thousands were freed, not slaughtered, when the pens
burst and the drums went quiet. Break-collar was learned on this floor,
and the war changed its meaning.

## Floors 21–40 · The Banner War

**21 · The Burnt Common.** A grazing heath caught by wildfire the season it
was stolen, held forever at the moment after the burn, new green knee-high
in the char. The commoners of Ashfold guarded the parish seed-store under
Goodwife Ash Ashfold, who read the returning green like a calendar.
Greycinder, a boar shelled in ember-iron, bedded in the warm ash. The seed
had been hidden down a badger sett.

**22 · The Whisperwood.** An elf-wood that once carried the lamp-songs and
now repeated everything else, giving a spoken word back armed hours later.
The wood-elves of Hushfall spoke only in the Hush Hollow, under Sela
Hushfall, who signed above ground. Hearsay, a quoted shade welded to the
Sounding Oak, spoke with the whole wood's voice. Silence was the only
safety, until the true song was sung.

**23 · The Anvil Commons.** The giants' shared forge-floor, a hundred anvils
around one cold hearth, all ringing under no hand. The smiths of Hammerhome
went deaf beside dead forges; old Dwald kept one banked ember alive in a
clay pot, waiting for a hearth worth lighting. Anvilback, a bellows hound on
an anvil frame, fought on the phantom chorus. Each quieted anvil held the
tune of its dead smith.

**24 · The Broodwarrens.** Deep caverns packed floor to roof with silk, the
under-town of Silkstead swallowed streets and all. The survivors barricaded
the Sealed Vault under Wend Silkstead, a knife for silk and a lamp for the
dark. Cradlesilk, a brood-midwife grown monstrous, re-wrapped the stair as
fast as it was cut. One lamp burned in the only un-webbed room on the floor.

**25 · The Gallows Fair.** A travelling fair abandoned mid-season and still
running itself — the carousel turning, the rigged games working the empty
air. The fair-folk of Fairstead hid in darkened wagons under Pol Fairstead,
who would not light the Big Wheel until it was safe to draw a crowd.
Hookmaster, a fair-cat rigged with grabbing hooks, worked the ring. It was
cheerful, and it was wrong.

**26 · The Slag Barrens.** The tipping-grounds of the deep forges, black
glassy hills still ticking with old heat, picked by folk who made a living
from what the realms threw away. Grint of Clinker Row knew which heaps would
kill you by their color. Slagbone, a salamander hulked in crane-iron,
slumped whole tips onto climbers. A vein of good metal, dumped in by
mistake, lay buried in the largest heap.

**27 · The Mirefields.** Peat mire under a low sky, its safe paths marked by
cairns and led wrong by drifting corpse-lights; what the peat took it kept,
whole and brown. The peat-folk of Cairnside poled between firm islands under
Cairn Cairnside, turf-mother, who trusted memory and never the lights.
Mirebone, a boar shelled in bog-iron, wallowed the deep pool. An elder the
peat had kept remembered where the old road ran.

**28 · The Smelterworks.** The smelting-floor, furnaces roaring on captured
aether, molten runnels threading the stone, moulds casting tower-parts with
no hand to shape them. The smelters of Crucible Gate poured for the tower
they hated, under Bruna Tapfire, who held the master tap-plug against the
day it was worth pulling. Smeltjaw, a runnel salamander with a furnace in
its chest, fought on the tap-bridges. She pulled it.

**29 · The Tithe Barrows.** Grave-country of grassed mounds where Men laid
their dead with a tithe, gone restless as the fever soaked the grave-gold.
The wardens of Wakewater kept vigil under Sef Wakewater, who had not slept
since the mounds began to stir. Tithegrim, a risen barrow-hound saddled with
a rider of grave-gold, collected mound to mound. The vigil-book listed which
barrows would open next.

**30 · The Kingsbarrow.** One colossal mound ringed by a black moat, where
the king-dead of Men were drowned to keep them sleeping. The tower woke the
king wrong; what walked remembered only the crown. Mereth Wakesend,
barrow-queen, held the causeway and refused the summons. The warden was
**the Barrow King**, a dead sovereign commanding his drowned court — evicted
rather than slain, the borrowed aether driven out and the true king laid at
last to rest.

**31 · The Grey Rides.** The straight grey riding-roads of the elf-hunt,
coursed forever by a spectral hunt after quarry freed long ago. The
ride-keepers of Bridlerest walked the verges under Aline Bridlerest,
sounding a recall no horn answered. Tallyhorn, a grey courser welded to its
dead rider, compelled the living to run as quarry. The bridle-tally named
every horse and rider the hunt had taken.

**32 · The Deep Drifts.** The giants' deepest shafts, a country measured in
fathoms down, winch-ropes creaking under loads that would never come up. The
drift-crews of Ropewalk signaled into a dark that no longer signaled back,
under Orla Ropewalk, who trusted neither rope nor echo anymore. Deepwinch, a
blind digger welded into the headframe, dropped loads and swung cages. A
chalked tally in the deepest bothy named the drift that broke through.

**33 · The Troll Delvings.** Galleries clawed out by trolls who shore up
nothing — one slow cave-in held in place by luck. The miners of Delvers'
Rest propped their town as the floor fell around it, under Hune Delverest,
who mapped the safe ground hour by hour. Mossmaul, a moss-hided troll with a
millstone jaw, knitted its wounds as fast as they were dealt. The Elder
Vault's inner doors were barred from within.

**34 · The Rig Graves.** A drowned graveyard of the fen-farmers' great
harvest-rigs, hulls half-sunk, cores still ticking in the mud. The
salvage-folk of Rustwake dived the wrecks under Weld Rustwake, who knew
every hull by name. Rigrot, a rig-wight driving its flagship on rust-frozen
legs, lured boats with drowned beacons. Before the host climbed, a salvaged
rig walked the fen again — this time for the rising.

**35 · The Vent Country.** A cracked plain of steam-vents blowing on hidden
clocks, sulfur-crusted and warm to the boot. The tenders of Steamgate timed
every step under Sela Steamgate, who taught climbers to read the whistle,
not the steam. Ventmaw, a salamander carrying its own fumarole, blew the
vents in sequence to herd and scald. The floor was crossed by tempo or not
at all.

**36 · The Processional Way.** A willow-lined burial causeway walked by a
funeral a thousand years long, its passing-bells never still. The mourners
of Mourngate tolled for a burial that could not end, under Cole Mourngate,
who stopped his own ears against the bell. Mournhide, a coffin-bearer grown
vast beneath the master bier, pressed the coffin onto any passer. The dead
reached their grave at last.

**37 · The Broken Doors.** A tier of the giants' great bulkheads, all blown
off their hinges in a single season — proof that nothing the old world built
kept the tower out. The doorwards of Last Lantern held the one surviving
door under Bruna Lastlantern, shamed and unbending. Gatebone, a slab of
blast-plate on hinge-legs, slammed shut on climbers in the choke-points. The
unbreached bulkhead was the stair itself.

**38 · The Peat Cuts.** Industrial peat-country, house-deep trenches flooded
ankle-deep and hazed with midge, where the fever taught the preserved dead
to climb. The cutters of Turfside held the stack-field under Maddox
Turfside, watching the Deep Cut. Peatlock, a boar under bog-oak plate that
neither rotted nor tired, wallowed the bottoms. A peat-cured king was
climbing his own cut when the wardens put him back down.

**39 · The Echo Glades.** Festival glades that caught the last revel the
night of the Theft and replayed it forever; wander in and the glade danced
you until you fell. The glade-elves of Fainthollow stopped their ears under
Illa Fainthollow, who danced the edges to pull out the caught. Encore, the
revel's dead lead dancer, called encore on every ending. The true final note
had been kept un-played in the Green Room.

**40 · The Broodthrone.** A cavern spun into a throne-hall of silk, hung
with cocoons like lanterns, each a living captive kept as courtier or
larder. Wend Threadneedle led the last free captives, cutting cocoons open
one thread at a time. The warden was **Matriarch Vyx**, a spider-queen who
puppeted her own court against the climb and wrapped to keep, not kill. When
her master-lines were cut, a dozen floors' worth of the taken walked free.

## Floors 41–60 · The Gathering

**41 · The Moraine Gates.** A field of glacial rubble under a hard white
sky, balanced boulders and a wind that cut to the bone — the first truly
cold floor. The ice-folk of Coldquay hoarded warmth in the boulder-hollows
under Bruna Coldquay, ice-reeve. Coldjaw, a moraine troll shelled in ice and
erratic-stone, kicked avalanches down the ridges. Frozen scouts stood
upright in the tarn shallows, and were counted.

**42 · The Signal Deeps.** Cavern-halls racked with the old world's
memory-lattices, still humming; whisper once and the deeps answered a
hundred times, some of them true. The lore-keepers of Faintlight
cross-checked everything thrice under Fenn Faintlight. Echobone, a signal
husk welded to the Mainframe Spire, spoke with the whole archive's false
voice. The world's own map of the tower lay in the Cold Aisle, and was read
clean.

**43 · The Bone Garths.** The walled rendering-yards of Men, bone stacked
house-high, the fever soaked into it until the heaps began to walk. The
bone-wardens of Garthend laid them back down under Sef Garthend, who read a
heap's mood by its clatter. Garthbone, a hound in fused rendered bone,
reformed itself from any stack it stood near. The Great Stack was fired in
the old way, and the yards went quiet.

**44 · The Ogre Steps.** A hillside cut into hundreds of terraced kilns, all
still firing on captured heat, fire-goats grazing the hot steps. The
terrace-folk of Kilnrest tended a bake a thousand years done, under Grint
Kilnrest, who knew every kiln's temper by its glow. Kilnfist, an ogre welded
into a kiln-oven body, vented scalding blasts across the terraces. Banked at
last, the kilns fired armor for the rising.

**45 · The Sunken Chantry.** A pilgrimage chapel flooded to the waist,
thousands of vigil-candles floating and burning still on the black water.
The faithful of Bellmarsh kept the lights from the dry Bell-Loft under Mera
Bellmarsh; the drowned congregation kept its pews, and wanted company.
Bellrot, a drowned ringer welded to the cracked great bell, tolled a false
vigil. The true bell laid the congregation to rest, at peace.

**46 · The Frozen Sea.** The giants' coolant reservoir, frozen solid the
night the forges died — pressure-ridges heaved up like walls, leads of black
water opening without warning. The sealers of Floeside read the pack under
Orla Floeside, who sounded every floe with her staff. Floeback, a troll
shelled in pressure-ice, cracked leads beneath a climber's feet. The Locked
Fleet stood masts-up in the ice like a drowned town.

**47 · The Doorstone Moor.** A high heather moor of standing stones raised
to keep something shut; one dolmen cracked open and breathed a cold wrong
wind. The moor-folk of Stonewake kept the ring under Bran Stonewake,
appalled that a seal had failed on his watch. Doorstone, the moor's own
stone sentinel fever-turned to hold every door open, barred the way. What
was unsealed was never a beast — only a hunger — and it was shut in for
good.

**48 · The Black Meadows.** The elves' night-pastures, where dream-horses
were bred on moonflower and sleep; the fever turned the gift, and the herds
grazed the dreams of the living. The night-herders of Wickstead dared not
sleep at all, under Nael Wickstead, who kept the true dream-bridle.
Nightbridle, the herd's lead stallion in a fever-bridle, trampled sleepers
where they lay. Bridled true, the nightmares grazed kind again.

**49 · The Rime Forest.** A conifer forest flash-frozen standing, every
needle a glass blade, the whole wood ringing like struck crystal; stags
walked it with antlers of clear glass. The foresters of Firwatch moved in
silence under Hune Firwatch, lest the canopy come down. Rimehide, a logger
troll hided in regrowing rime-glass, felled the trees onto climbers. Rung
true, the Watch-Fir chimed the wood into gentle thaw.

**50 · The Cindermouth.** The mouth of a living caldera, its rim lined with
tribute-pyres kept burning a thousand years — the Waste-folk feeding their
own to appease what dwelt below. Sira Lastwater tended the pyres she hated
and hid the old free-rite. The warden was **Cindermaw the Wyrm**, older than
the Ascent, maddened to hunger and cured, not killed. The pyres were doused,
no life was given, and the freed wyrm remembered who freed her.

**51 · The Hanging City.** A city slung across a bottomless chasm on woven
spider-silk, lamps hung in the void like low stars; its spinner-partners had
turned, drifting the dark on balloon-silk and cutting the streets. Fenn
Skeinside of Skeinside walked the cables checking every splice. Skeinback, a
troll on silk-sails, dropped the bridges under climbers. The Undercatch net
below held a fortune in fallen cargo — and the fallen who lived.

**52 · The Cloudline.** Ridge-pastures piercing a permanent cloud-sea,
islands of sunlit turf over drops with no bottom in sight. The cliff-holders
of Skyfoot herded the ledges under Mera Skyfoot, who read the thermals by
the wind's voice. Thermal, a harpy grown vast on welded wind-sails, stooped
out of the cloud and rode back up out of reach. The ridge-lore was chalked
on a shrine wall, and it held.

**53 · The Ember Dunes.** A marching desert run backward — sand cool
through the sunless day, smouldering by night when the ember-moths came
down. The
nomads of Duneshade crossed by star-lore with the stars gone, under Sira
Duneshade, dune-mother. Emberback, a sand-swimming salamander in an ember
shell, struck from below the crests. Every so often the dunes moved off the
Sunk Caravanserai and gave the road back.

**54 · The Wreckfields.** A high plain scattered with an entire sky-fleet,
downed in one night — keels upturned like whale-ribs, drakes nesting in the
holds. The salvagers of Hullside stripped the wrecks they had once flown,
under Weld Hullside, who dived the deepest hulls alone. Keelhaul, a
hull-borer wearing the flagship's keel as a shell, dragged climbers into the
splintered holds. One salved skiff flew again.

**55 · The Troll Bridges.** Snow-bridges arcing over crevasses with no
visible bottom, built and held by the mountain-trolls, whom the fever had
made jealous of their own spans. The keepers of Bridgeward sounded every
crossing under Orla Bridgeward. Spanbreak, a bridge-troll with ice-block
fists, collapsed the snow under crossers. In the Groaning Chasm's blue ice a
caravan hung frozen, still carrying its cable.

**56 · The Rookeries.** A sea-cliff miles high, every ledge packed with a
numberless rookery stirred to permanent riot; the mob was what killed you.
The keepers of Rookwall worked the ledges on rope under Cole Rookwall, who
climbed where no one else would. Shrikewind, a matron harpy spurred like a
shrike's larder-thorn, drove the whole cliff down on climbers. The
gatherers' rope-town hung there still — a village with no ground.

**57 · The Shade Walks.** The lamplit boulevards of an elf wood-city, where
the lamps failed one by one and shadows peeled loose from their casters,
hungry. The lamp-keepers of Candlerow guarded the last flames under Illa
Candlerow, a lantern always in hand. Palewick, a living dark welded to the
city's greatest snuffed lamp, pooled the black. When the Great Lamp was
relit, every shadow on the Walks snapped home.

**58 · The Giant Steadings.** Freehold farm-country at a giant's scale —
barns like cathedrals, hay-ricks like hills, a climber wading a lawn to the
knee. The freeholders of Thanesrest were penned by their own closing fences,
under Dara Thanesrest, who meant to hold her steading free or not at all.
Fencewright, a plough-bull walled in fence-stone and living hedge, penned
climbers in closing lanes. The charter came out of the root-cellar with the
seed-corn.

**59 · The Trapdoor Fields.** A flat grey cavern-plain, deceptively level
and riddled edge to edge with the hinged lids of trapdoor-burrows; almost
nothing was safe ground. The field-delvers of Latchlight re-marked the safe
lines from the Watch-Mound — the one thing that was not a lid — under Wend
Latchlight, who had scars for her wrong steps. Latchjaw, a spider whose
shell was its own great lid, dropped the floor out from under the unwary.

**60 · The Frosthall.** The mead-hall of a giant jarldom, warm light
spilling into the snow and an endless war-feast within, thanes dying and
rising to drink again. Bruna Hearthold kept the Mead of Memory hidden and
the true songs alive. The warden was **Jarl Hrimgar** — the best of them,
his open hand frozen to a fist — freed, not slaughtered, when the Mead woke
him. The feast became a welcome again.

## Floors 61–80 · The Scattered Sky

**61 · The Anchor Chains.** A sky of vast mooring-chains slung tower to
tower over cloud and a fall with no bottom — links walked like roads, the
isles they once held long drifted away. The chain-keepers of Chainrest
sounded every link by its hum, under Mera Chainrest. Shacklewise, a drake
welded into the great windlass, snapped the spans to shake walkers off. One
near isle was hauled home to its mooring.

**62 · The Reactor Scar.** A crater of fused colorless glass where one of
the old world's reactors melted down, burning still with a flame no eye
could see — felt only as the sickness deepening. The survivors of Scarwatch
lived at the rim on pale water, under Grint Scarwatch, who glowed faintly
himself. Palescorch, a salamander welded round a shard of the broken core,
fought invisible in the shimmer. The Glassed Town stood fused mid-street,
clear as ice.

**63 · The Updrafts.** Wind-gorge passes where thermal columns boomed strong
enough to lift a kite and its pilot; the crossings were flown, or not made.
The kite-pilots of Kitegate read every column's note under Cole Kitegate.
Galecrest, a dueling harpy on wind-cutting vanes, owned the columns and cut
lines mid-air. On the gorge-bottom lay the Wrecking-Floor, a drift of every
kite that ever fell.

**64 · The Ice Quarries.** Open quarries sawn from a glacier of flawless
clear ice, gantries swinging house-sized blocks that no one guided. The
quarriers of Hewnstone worked under loads they had not locked themselves,
under Hune Hewnstone, who read a block's flaws by the light through it.
Hewnheart, a troll welded into the great gantry-crane, crushed with its own
cargo. The finest master-lens ever cut came up from the black sump.

**65 · The Mast Forest.** A sky-fleet that lost its lift and settled
upright, hull-deep in the mountain — masts in ranks like a forest, rigging
for a canopy. The mast-folk of Sparside lived aloft in the crowsnests under
Weld Sparside and never trusted the wreck-choked floor. Mastwrack, a drake
welded to the tallest main-mast, whipped the shroud-lines to snare and
fling. One mast of the dead forest flew again.

**66 · The Kennel Courts.** The stone kennel-yards where the elf-realm bred
and gentled every beast of its hunt; the fever ungentled all of it at once.
The keepers of Stillhorn whispered the old names through the bars under Nael
Stillhorn, hoping. Leashbone, a pack-matron harnessed by iron leashes to
every run on the floor, hauled whole packs as a weapon. When the master horn
sounded true, every beast heeled to its name.

**67 · The Coolant Falls.** The deep-forge's outflow, a mile-high waterfall
flash-frozen in the act of falling, milk-lit caverns behind the curtain. The
falls-folk of Milkwater kept the source-shrine in the Cataract Cave under
Bruna Milkwater, listening for the roar. Whitefall, a troll welded into the
cataract itself, calved blocks of the curtain onto climbers. Pilgrims stood
frozen mid-climb in the ice, and were thawed at last.

**68 · The Silk Vaults.** The Deep's cold storerooms, aisle on aisle of neat
white bundles; the fever turned the keeping cruel, and the vault-spiders
wrapped the living for storage. The crews of Spoolgate cut free the newly
wrapped under Wend Spoolgate, who knew which bundles held treasure and which
held someone still breathing. Spoolhide, a keeper-spider welded into the
master reel, spun binding faster than any blade could cut.

**69 · The Lightning Fields.** A plain bristling with copper poles under a
storm-ceiling that never rained and never stopped flashing; the fever taught
the sky to aim. The field-folk of Copperpole crossed by the safe lanes under
Mera Copperpole, who read the next strike by the crawl on her skin.
Groundstrike, a drake crowned in captured lightning atop the Master Pole,
called the bolts down. The Glass Garden recorded every strike in fulgurite.

**70 · The Tempest Court.** A sky-palace on the highest crag, wreathed in a
permanent roaring tempest of unbottled gales; a whole court clung to its
perches, tied to the stone. Cole Galesend kept the bottling-lore alive among
the captives. The warden was **Zephyra, the Storm Queen** — a good queen
crowned into a tyrant of tempest, freed, not killed, when her storm-crown
broke in the eye. The gales fell to a breeze.

**71 · The Char Forest.** A woodland burned so hot it petrified — trunks of
black glassy charcoal, some still warm at the heart a thousand years on. The
char-burners of Stumpwell worked the dead wood under Grint Stumpwell, who
knew which snags held a live coal. Charspine, a salamander in a petrified
husk glowing at every crack, reignited the trunks around it. The Great Snag
was banked into the tier's one safe hearth.

**72 · The Drake Aeries.** A cliff of tiered eyries and hoard-caves, every
ledge heaped with drake-gold; Men had lived here by an old truce, and the
fever broke it. The keepers of Scalewatch dodged the squabbling drakes under
Weld Scalewatch, who climbed to the tyrant's perch alone. Stoopfall, the old
deposed tyrant-drake in a welded stoop-harness, beat climbers off the cliff.
The truce was re-struck at the carved stone.

**73 · The Wind Walls.** Colossal ice-and-iron ramparts the giants raised to
break the killing gales, their measuring shutters jammed open so the wind
chose, and chose to kill. The wall-folk of Shuttergate braced in the
casemates under Bruna Shuttergate, who read a coming gust by its note in the
walls. Galebone, a troll welded into the master shutter, blasted climbers
off the crown. The highest wall had been tuned by ages of gale into an
organ.

**74 · The Snare Woods.** A hunting-grove seeded root to crown with wire,
tripline, and deadfall — quiet, green, and wholly lethal. The snare-folk of
Brambleside disarmed what they could under Illa Brambleside, who read a
cocked wood by the tension in the air. Springtrap, a hound welded into a
walking snare-engine, sprang the wood's traps in cascades. In the Gibbet
Glade the caught were cut down at last and given graves.

**75 · The Jarl's Road.** The jarldom's processional way, dead-straight
across the uplands — and marched by a spectral column bound for a feast a
thousand years fallen. The road-folk of Horncall pulled the caught out of
the column under Hune Horncall, road-warden. Hallmarch, a lamplighter troll
made a marching-engine, drove the ghost-muster on. The true horn, sounded
from the Milestone Cairn, called the march home to its feast.

**76 · The Quarry Runs.** The elf-hunt's coursing-runs, which remembered the
chase; set foot on a lane and the whole wood decided you were quarry. The
quarry-folk of Bayhollow learned to double back on the hunt under Nael
Bayhollow. Foxglove, the runs' apex quarry made their master hunter, drove
the living to a killing-ground strewn with a thousand hunts' antlers.
Broken, the great fox-wolf was quarry once more.

**77 · The Egg Galleries.** The Deep's hatcheries — pale caverns racked
with glowing egg-sacs in their tens of thousands, the fever quickening the
hatch
to a flood. The watchers of Palewatch chilled sacs to slow it, under Wend
Palewatch, who knew to the hour when a gallery would burst. Husklight, a
sentinel spider welded round a cluster of mother-sacs, birthed broods as it
fought. The Incubatory was stilled at the source.

**78 · The Eye Wall.** The dead-calm eye of the storm-queen's tempest —
soft gold light, suspended rain, and a peace that killed by kindness; those
who
sat to rest never rose. The eye-dwellers of Lullside stayed awake by the
eye-wall's roar, under Mera Lullside, who shook the drowsing awake.
Stillmark, the killing calm given a body, welcomed climbers to lie down. In
the Sitting Garden, folk seated eighty years stood up and walked.

**79 · The Wyrmroad.** An ancient migration-canyon drifted spear-deep in
shed wyrm-scale, older than the tower and answering no crown. The road-folk
of Moltgate sorted molt under Grint Moltgate, who knew which hollows held a
sleeping wyrmling. Moltcrown, a young wyrm crowned in ages of welded molt,
was cured rather than killed, and remembered it. The Bonefall — the skeleton
of a wyrm dead before the tower rose — spanned the canyon.

**80 · The Pale Court.** The bone-white hall of the Master of the Hunt, hung
with a thousand years of trophies and lit moon-pale; from it the Grey Rides,
the Kennel Courts, and the Quarry Runs all took their law. Nael Hornsend,
once the Master's own horn-caller, kept the calloff-lore. The warden was
**the Pale Huntsman** — the gentlest hand in the wood, hunted longest and
furthest gone of all the freed lords. He woke, ended every hunt in the
realm, and laid the horn down for good.

## Floors 81–100 · The Made

*The Made were built, not stolen, and their trays alone keep no
country's outline: perfect circles, rim to rim — a line no people ever
drew.*

**81 · The First Outwork.** The first floor of the Made — no soil, no
green, only slag-fill and riveted plate, a siege-rampart the size of a
border-country raised by slave-sappers whose bones went into the fill. Dara
Sappersrest of the labor-camp kept the demolition-lore and the tally of the
dead in the walls. Rivetgrim, a section of rampart risen and walking,
dragged climbers into its plate. The outwork came down from below.

**82 · The Chain Yards.** Forging-floors slung over quench-pits, a country
with a single product: chains — worked by the bound, who hammered their own
shackles and hated every link. Wend Linkside filed at his shackle in the
dark and kept the master-drain's secret. Anchorwrath, the yard's drag-lines
welded into a thrashing anchor-serpent, hauled climbers to the pits. A
hidden file cut collars cold, with no spell at all.

**83 · The Furnace Roads.** A lattice of iron-grating causeways over open
furnace-pits, glowing red from beneath — a griddle-country crossed grate by
grate. The road-crews of Stokegate lost one of their own through a grate
every shift; Grint Stokegate read which bars would give by their glow.
Cindergrate, the fused ash and bone of a hundred fallen crews, dropped the
gratings under climbers. The master damper banked it all cold.

**84 · The Welding Halls.** The horror-factory of the Made, where captured
souls were welded seam by seam into imps and hellknights; some on the
cooling-racks still screamed inside their seams. Sef Seamside split free
every half-made soldier the overseers left unwatched. Seamwright, the master
welding-engine itself grown a will, welded climbers where they stood.
Reversed, the halls ran backward — un-making soldiers into people.

**85 · The Imp Warrens.** A smoke-choked barracks-slum of iron cell-blocks
where the tower's soldiery denned between shifts, and press-gangs hauled the
next batch through to the racks. Wend Sootside ran the whisper-network of
the disaffected. The warden Fluegrim was no construct but a Pressed
hellknight, his collar old and cracked; freed rather than broken, he turned
his whole warren — the first great defection of the King's own army.

**86 · The Bastion Line.** An endless drill-fortress — ramparts and
parade-grounds out of sight, ranks wheeling and presenting forever to drum
and horn, discipline made a landscape. Dara Wallrest kept the stolen
drill-orders and counter-signs. Bastionheart, a drill-master welded into
command-plate, was Pressed, not made; his collar broke, the endless drill
halted for the first time in an age, and his army formed up behind the
rising.

**87 · The Siege Gardens.** The King's parody of the green world — terraced
beds where siege-engines were grown from iron-seed, oozing black oil for
sap, tended by thralls with pruning-torches. Nael Ramside kept the
blight-lore, souring beds before they fruited into war. Rootram, a
half-grown assault-ram fused to the garden's root-cables, flailed on
part-grown limbs. Blighted, the crop rotted in its rows and the seed-vault
went cold.

**88 · The Iron Chantries.** War-chapels of black iron where the bound were
made to chant the King's creed until some half-believed — the floor that
worked on the mind, not the body. Sef Chantside whispered the counter-creed
to those slipping under. Vespergrim, the accreted false faith risen as a
pillar of iron-smoke and scripture, hazed the naves with devotion. The
belfry was rung against the liturgy, and the compulsion cracked.

**89 · The Herald's Road.** The ceremonial approach to Malgrim's gate —
proclamation-horns the size of houses blaring the King's titles without
pause, a shout that was itself a weapon. Nael Clarionfall kept the knack of
turning a horn against its own fanfare. Trumpetsteel, a Pressed honor-knight
welded into a horn-bodied herald, was freed rather than felled — and blared
the King's decree backward, throwing the muster into confusion.

**90 · The Herald's Gate.** The muster-gate of the Made, iron yards under a
colossal gatehouse where drums beat men into soldiers and clerks tallied the
conscript-rolls. Dara Lastmuster kept the knack of striking the rolls. The
warden was **Malgrim, Herald of the King** — freed once far below, re-taken,
and promoted until he loved the King who unmade him. Offered every mercy the
climb had learned, he refused; he fell unfree, the hardest victory of the
war.

**91 · The Obsidian Stair.** A grand staircase cut from one sweep of black
mirror-glass, climbing through a lightless hall; the reflections did not
always copy, and some stepped out. Wend of Firststep taught the held to look
away before the glass learned their faces. Firstriser, the first reflection
the stair ever freed, fought exactly as its climber did, only colder. It had
been up there longer, and it had learned things.

**92 · The Mirror Galleries.** Endless silvered halls where the King hoarded
the faces of the taken, worn and swapped by the things in the glass. Illa of
Sheenrest returned stolen likenesses to the blank-featured one mirror at a
time. Backglass, a hundred stolen faces welded into one figure, dueled
behind borrowed masks with its victims' own skill. The master mirror was
shattered, and a thousand faces flew home.

**93 · The Reactor Galleries.** The tower's living core — the
colorless-flame engine from which the Yoke, the fever, and every collar and
weld poured out. The reactor-thralls of Coreside fed the heart of their own
bondage; Ferro kept the scram-lore, hoarded a lifetime. Halflight, the
core's own flame given a body, could not be killed while it fed, and was
starved instead. The core was scrammed, and the whole tower went quiet at
once.

**94 · The Antechambers.** A honeycomb of waiting-rooms before the throne,
where the taken held numbered tokens for audiences that never came — bondage
without a chain, refined to pure patience. Nib of Doorward kept the hard
knack of standing up and walking out of the line. Anteroom, a grey
chamberlain accreted from a lifetime of recalled tokens, kept everyone
forever almost admitted. The queues simply walked out.

**95 · The Trophy Vaults.** The King's museum of his own conquest — the
crowns of drowned kingdoms, the arms of fallen liberators, and the gear of
every climber who came before, labelled. Vess of Hoardside knew whose every
relic was. The Curator, a construct of welded crowns and swords, turned the
fallen heroes' own famous weapons on the climb. On the Climbers' Shelf a
space had been kept blank at the end.

**96 · The Night Gardens.** The King's one vanity of beauty — a walled
pleasance of black-flowering trees, terraced and fountained, grown from the
taken, who were planted in the beds and coaxed to flower. Every bloom had a
face if you looked. Elys of Bloomside dug the planted free before they
flowered past saving. Nightbloom, a vast night-flower whose every petal was
a face, breathed a perfume that would have lulled an army into the beds.

**97 · The Kingsway.** A mile of white monumental marble climbing the last
tiers — a thousand statues of Vharuk, arches, and reliefs retelling the
Theft as glory. The captive masons of Wayside smuggled the true history into
the plinths where the King never stooped to read. Waymarshal, a colossal
marble Vharuk stepped off its plinth, guarded the approach with a god's
arrogance. The idols came down the whole length of the avenue.

**98 · The Court.** The gilded audience-tiers of the willing — courtiers
never taken and never collared, who chose the King and prospered by him; the
only captives were the servants in the walls. Bly of Courtside kept their
long ledger of the court's crimes. The warden was the Grand Vizier, a free
man who laughed at mercy and was judged rather than freed. The ledger was
read aloud over the gilded ruin.

**99 · The Last Door.** One immense threshold-hall and the Door itself —
black adamant and welded sorrow, a mosaic of fallen liberators sealed where
they fell. Hollen of Lastward kept the belief that the hero inside still
lived. The warden was the Champion, the greatest liberator ever to climb,
welded living into the Door as its lock. Given back her name and her sword,
she stepped out of the metal alive, and climbed the last floor beside the
host.

**100 · The Throne.** The summit — a single hall of cold iron and raw
aether open to a black sky and its stars, built by a King who cut the
world into a hundred pieces
so it could never stand in one room. No town stood here, and no keeper; only
**Vharuk, the Demon King**, a usurper of nothing, offered no mercy because
he was the source. When he fell, the stolen aether went home, every collar
opened, and the hundred floors stood together on his throne-floor — the one
thing the tower was built to prevent. Then the host walked down.
