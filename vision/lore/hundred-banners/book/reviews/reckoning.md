# The Reckoning

*Editor-in-chief's closing audit of the cycle: the virgin draft
(`backups/book-backup001/` and `backups/book-backup001-appendices/`)
against the shipping text (`chapters/`, `appendices/`), after five
editorial iterations (ledgers `changes-001` through `changes-005`) and
the plan-011 world-construction re-grounding mid-cycle. Date:
2026-08-04. After this document: restorations applied, the mechanical
sweep run, and the final edition cut.*

## The numbers

- Chapters: 145,024 words (virgin) → 149,064 (shipping, pre-reckoning);
  54 files both sides. Net +4,040 — the book grew by earning scenes, not
  by padding: the largest single gains are `35-heralds-gate` (+441, the
  Serle Danner hinge and Ede's command voice), `03-hedge-and-hollow`
  (+340, the gate-rule plant), `19a` (+326), `41-epilogue` (+265, the
  gold-ladder panorama), `28-the-coursing` (+149).
- Appendices: 13,712 → 14,148; the whole of the growth is
  `06-on-the-yoke` (+396), rebuilt end to end by the plan-011
  re-grounding.
- Diffs read in full for this audit: `00-prologue`, `09-the-quiet`
  (breather), `35-heralds-gate` (battle), `38-scram`, `41-epilogue`,
  appendix `06-on-the-yoke` — plus `11`, `13`, `17`, `22`, `24`, `25`,
  `26a`, `40`, `40a`, and a word-level removed-phrase sweep across all
  60 files.

## 1. What actually got better — the five biggest wins

**1. The tower's physics became one story (plan 011, ratified
iteration 3).** The virgin draft's tower stood "by power" — grav-engines
at every weld, the throne "the winch-house." The shipping text stands it
on the Strand, and every consequence follows: why the lifts turned the
morning after the scram, why the scram could kill every cruelty and not
one ounce of weight, why the war could be *read off the tower's face*.

> Virgin (app. 06): "It stands by power… the throne is the winch-house;
> hold the core, and the whole Ascent answers you like a bellows-crew."
>
> Shipping: "It stands by the Strand… the Strand is an oath, said once,
> whole, at the cutting; it does not ask leave of the core, or of the
> King, or of anyone living; the throne was never the winch-house of the
> standing — only of the voice; hold the core and you hold every cruelty
> in the tower, and not one ounce of its weight."

And the image the re-grounding bought, nowhere in the virgin draft
(epilogue): "the tower stood at the town's back the way a ladder stands
against a barn… And it was gold, the whole visible height of it but one
rung — a single grey hairline at the fortieth, where a queen kept her
own dark by contract." The virgin epilogue had a cliff; the shipping
epilogue has the entire war, legible from a fishing quay.

**2. The climax argues true (`38-scram`, iterations 2–5).** The virgin
scram scene had Ulfar keeping "the grav-engines fed" on a tenth of the
core — physics the book itself contradicted at `18`'s load-curves — and
Sliver claiming to be "the last light of the shardkind" while 212
census-shards burned in the host's pockets. Both wounds are closed, and
the repairs are better prose than what they replaced:

> Virgin: "Damp the coils to a tenth… And with the tenth I keep the
> grav-engines fed, and I set the floors down."
>
> Shipping: "Throttle the broadcast to a tenth — the King's voice, the
> King's veils, the coils that carry them, and nothing else… And the
> engines keep the core's full breath — the broadcast never fed them,
> and the Strand needs no feeding — and I set the floors down."
>
> Virgin: "I am the last light of the shardkind."
>
> Shipping: "The carried ones burn on — the census's two hundred and
> twelve, warm in pockets and palms the length of this host — but the
> roads between us are gone. The choir is what died. Each of us is a
> lamp now in a country without roads."

**3. The finale got its witness back (Wat Kello, iterations 1, 3, 4).**
The virgin finale renamed the welded banner-bearer of `33`/`34`/`36`
"Marn" — colliding with Marn Deepfurrow, dead since `21a` — and the
dramatis personae carried two ghost entries for one man. The shipping
text gives him one name through five chapters and an appendix, and
iteration 4 gave the name a body: "Shieldwall's grey went before the
biers all evening; Wat Kello had taken the pole that morning, and no
one asked for it back" (`35`) — so when he raises the first banner over
Roothollow in the epilogue, the reader has watched him earn the pole.

**4. The big battle became a commanded battle (`35-heralds-gate`,
iterations 2–4).** The virgin Herald's Gate was a panorama the
protagonist watched. The shipping chapter has a hinge with a name on it
and a general's voice in it — both absent from the virgin draft:

> "I stood at a fire once and told her she sold us. Today I hold her
> hinge." (Serle Danner, paying the `08` arc at term)
>
> "*Forge-Court to the hinge — close the gap walking; you are a wall,
> and walls do not run.*" (Ede's one command beat, iteration 3)

The same rounds fixed the sixty-thousand arithmetic ("the Bastion
Line's wheeling-over alone had doubled the rolls"), replaced the false
treaty with Zephyra's true debt ("promised in her own eye the day she
scattered the host — wind, when the wind profits"), and killed the
metafictional wink ("One chapter late" → "A battle late").

**5. The tics died and their replacements grew from the scenes.** The
virgin draft spent "like a held breath let go" seven times and
"untended" as a formula; the shipping text licenses two of each and
minted local coin for the rest — each replacement in the register of
its own floor: "like a loom when the last thread is cut" (`09`, the
silk-floor); "the way dust turns in a shaft of lantern-light" (`26a`,
the cellars); "for the time an ingot takes to lose its color" (`38`,
the Coalbrows); "for the time a drawn breath takes to turn to frost"
(`17`, the Frozen Sea). This is the difference between de-duplication
and revision: the book's simile engine now runs on the scenes.

Behind all five sit the unglamorous audits: the eighty-year register
enforced ("a tempest a thousand years old" → "eighty years old"), the
banner-ladder reconciled to 40 → 58 → 70 → 100, the burglary given one
address, the Warcamp one distance, and the summit its stars.

## 2. What was lost

Looked hard, per the mandate. Method: full diffs of sixteen files plus
a word-level sweep of every removed phrase ≥45 characters across all 54
chapters and 6 appendices (42 hits, every one traced against the
ledgers). Most deaths were correct. Three were not.

**Real losses, restored (see §3):**

1. **Hobb's tricolon** (`41-epilogue`; cut by iteration 3, change 12,
   as "redundant"). Virgin: "'They match,' said Hobb Fennick, to the
   gate-post, to the valley, **to every climber he had ever counted
   into the hedges and never counted out.**" The cut clause is not
   redundant with "Everyone who went in has come out" — the amen states
   the fact; the clause names the *audience*: eighty years of ghosts.
   And the truncated pair ("to the gate-post, to the valley.") is a
   limp two-beat where the virgin built a tricolon to the book's best
   cadence. The trim saved 14 words in a chapter already granted its
   overage everywhere else. Restored.

2. **"The willing signed nothing"** (`40a-the-trials`; lost in
   iteration 2's Annis Cray trim). Virgin: "Nobody had informed on her;
   **the willing signed nothing, that was the whole craft of the
   collar,** and she could have stood among the freed forever." The
   shipping sentence kept the fact and dropped the *why* — and the
   dropped clause is a 12-word statement of the book's central theme
   (the collar's craft is that it leaves no evidence but the wearer),
   the same theme the Herald's inside-fastened collar and Berrin's
   confession carry. Restored.

3. **The old digger** (`40a-the-trials`; same trim). Virgin: "**An old
   digger stood up and said what the whole hall needed a first case to
   say:** that the tower had built a world…" The shipping text rendered
   the book's first free verdict as narrator's summary. In this book
   verdicts are spoken by people — that is the entire design of the
   trials chapter. Restored as the speaker frame around the (kept)
   condensed verdict; the case stays trimmed.

**Honorable deaths — quoted for the record, deliberately not
restored:**

- Sela Steamgate's aphorism, `13-silk-terms`: "timing the ground's
  white jets by their whistles — *read the whistle, not the steam, she
  said; the steam has already cooked you* — and the army crossed a
  breathing yellow plain single file, on the clock of one keeper's
  ear." The best line in the eight-floor catalog, and it died with the
  catalog (D1, endorsed by two panels; the condensed "the rest the
  histories may keep" list is the round's best piece of craft).
  Reinserting it would re-open the tour the cut exists to close. It
  belongs in a gazetteer entry someday, not back in the march.
- The quarry-master of the Ice Quarries, `24-anchor-chains`: "reading
  each block's flaws by the light through it before he would let a
  company pass beneath" — same cut, same reasoning.
- "one of them, at a whistle from a hedge, turned its head" (`40`) —
  cut by iteration 4 so the whistle belongs to floor one and the last
  page; the epilogue's dog pays it. Correct.
- "mid-harvest, mid-flood, without spilling a hay-rick, because the
  thief wanted his takings whole" (app. 06) — a beautiful clause about
  engines that no longer exist. Plan-011 casualty; correctly dead, like
  every virgin line about the black iron bearing the tower's weight.
- Hale's gate-rule explainer (`22`) is not a loss: it moved to the
  fighting-pits of `03`, where the rule is now spoken ("lose your
  sword, lose your teeth, but keep your gate") so the death scene rings
  instead of lectures. Verified in place.

## 3. Restorations made (on the record, this date)

1. `41-epilogue.md` — restored "to every climber he had ever counted
   into the hedges and never counted out" to Hobb's amen (+14 words;
   chapter 3,206 → 3,220).
2. `40a-the-trials.md` — restored "the willing signed nothing, that was
   the whole craft of the collar" to the Annis Cray paragraph.
3. `40a-the-trials.md` — restored the old digger as speaker of the
   first verdict ("An old digger stood up and said what the whole hall
   needed a first case to say: that…"), keeping iteration 2's condensed
   verdict wording (chapter 3,122 → 3,154 across both edits).

Each was checked against all five ledgers: none reintroduces a fixed
defect (no century leak, no tic, no plan-011 dead canon, no keystone
touched); the two `40a` restorations sit inside iteration 2's trim
without undoing its length (the case remains ~a third shorter than the
virgin draft's).

## 4. The verdict

The shipping text is better than the virgin draft, and not narrowly:
the virgin book was a beautiful ruin whose physics contradicted its
climax, whose finale starred a dead man's name, and whose best images
wore out from reuse, while the shipping book argues true from the
Strand to the scram and spends its coin once. What the five iterations
could not manufacture is compression — the epilogue still runs past its
spec even after its trim, the mid-book march chapters (`13`, `24`)
remain catalogs even condensed, and the panels' instinct to summarize
where the book's own method is testimony cost it three of its truest
lines, which this reckoning has put back. The ambition was a hundred
floors that each feel like a country and a ledger that always balances;
the shipping text delivers the ledger everywhere and the country most
of the time, and where it falls short it now falls short honestly, on
the record, with nothing left in the margins that anyone was afraid to
read.
