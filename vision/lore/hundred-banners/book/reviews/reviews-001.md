## 1. The Genre Faithful

**Verdict: LOVED.**

I would press this on a friend tonight, with the caveat "clear a week."
The awe is real, the fellowship is earned in specific, physical scenes
(a banner-hem held taut, letter-punches for kobold hands, a rota-board
chalked in warren-script), and the payoffs are planted so far back and
paid so cleanly that the reread will be a second book. The great scenes
are GREAT: Gnarl refusing the Break-collar in `04-king-of-nothing.md`
("I'll not be pardoned out of my own punchline"); Dun's muster-roll sung
across the fire in `08-what-the-freed-forgive.md`; the out-mourning in
`12-out-mourned-king.md`; both doors of the feast in `22-the-feast.md`;
"Bread," said Wat Kello in `33-the-welded.md` — the single best line-beat
in the book; Calyne dying with both hands on his collar-band in
`34-viziers-floor.md`; the bell in `35-heralds-gate.md`; the knock
descending a hundred floors in `40-the-throne.md`. The Champion chain —
A. BRIGHT worn thumb-flat on the Stone in `01-trainer.md`, the crooked
star in `08a-first-banner.md`, "she was my fourth" in `39-last-door.md` —
is as good as foreshadowing gets in this genre.

Notes, ranked:

1. **The iron-jawed banner-bearer changes name and it breaks the book's
   best payoff.** He is Wat Kello in `33-the-welded.md` ("'Bread,' said
   Wat Kello") and at the trial in `36-malgrim.md`; then in
   `38a-stolen-years.md` ("furled on Marn's shoulder"),
   `39-last-door.md` ("Marn of the thirty carried it now, the man freed
   at the Welding Halls, iron from the jaw down"), `40-the-throne.md`
   ("Marn, standing under his banner on the summit"), and
   `41-epilogue.md` ("Marn's one uncovered hand") he is Marn — while a
   DIFFERENT Marn (Deepfurrow, the wagon-matron) died and was buried in
   `21a-the-wintering.md`. The reader who wept at "Bread" arrives at the
   Aldis salute holding the wrong name. Fix: restore Wat Kello in 38a,
   39, 40, and 41. Surgical, four files, no prose lost.

2. **The Warden-death beat has one simile and it is wearing out.** "Like
   a held breath let go/out" appears eight times, and five of them sit
   on the book's signature payoff moment — the seam splitting, the lift
   opening, the silk going slack: `03-hedge-and-hollow.md` ("like a held
   breath let out"), `04-king-of-nothing.md` ("split its seam with a
   sound like a held breath let go"), `07-red-warcamp.md`,
   `09-the-quiet.md`, `20-long-stair.md`, plus `12-out-mourned-king.md`,
   `21a-the-wintering.md`, `38a-stolen-years.md`. By floor sixty the
   awe-moment reads recycled. Fix: keep the first (`03`) and one
   deliberate late echo (`38a`, where "like held breath let go" is doing
   new work on the Years); rewrite the middle five fresh. This is the
   cheapest awe-repair on offer.

3. **`35-heralds-gate.md` sells its own morning short.** The steel hour
   before the bell — "the battle the histories would remember for its
   gentleness opened as ugly as anything since the Red Warcamp" — is
   summarized at arm's length (Vega "would remember very little of that
   walk"). The bell's noon is transcendent precisely in proportion to
   how bad the morning was; give the morning one more close-quarters
   beat (a named soldier of the thirty in the hinge, twenty lines, no
   more). This is the one battle where I wanted a half-page I didn't
   get.

4. Small: Sliver's "I have carried forty-one climbers" refrain (seven
   uses) pays off perfectly in `39-last-door.md` — keep every one; but
   Sliver's *"Forty-one climbers I have carried"* variant in
   `04-king-of-nothing.md` lands on the same page as its use in `02`,
   and one of the two can go.

**Do not touch:** the Berrin engine — the prologue's unsigned childhood,
the warm scenes that reread as horror ("Bring me the name that signed
it. I'll bake for the hanging," `02-third-refusal.md`), Malgrim's
ledger interludes ("A ledger entry does not retire"), and the sentence
of labor in `36-malgrim.md`. This is the book's spine and its market
differentiator. No softening, no earlier tipping of the hand, no
execution "for closure."

## 3. The Structural Editor

**Verdict: LIKED — architecture sound, two clumps and a tour to fix.**

Act balance is genuinely good: the feast (`22-the-feast.md`) lands at
~55% of the build, the unmasking (`34-viziers-floor.md`) at ~80%, and
the two are far enough apart that the middle carries its own weight
between them. The interludes are perfectly placed pressure-valves.
Chapter lengths are disciplined; `30-what-ede-carries.md` is
intentionally short and correctly so.

**On the eight insertions, the direct question first: they sit
seamlessly — with two exceptions of degree, not kind.** `08a` (the
banner tale), `19a` (Roothollow from below), `21a` (the wintering),
`26a` (the bottled court), and `38a` (the stolen years) are not
detectable as insertions; they are the book's best "time inside people"
and the reason the middle reads LIVED-IN rather than TOURED. `21a` is
the load-bearing wall of the feast — Hale's winter-long transit up the
benches ("cold iron coming back to temper") is what makes the south
door pay. `12a` is structurally necessary (Voss's turn happens on-page
instead of by rumor). The two that show seams: `31a-wakes-price.md`
replays the ultimatum-structure of `26-gathering-the-blown.md` (the
Wake presents a bill at the worst hour; someone defies it) with a long
down-queue travelogue before Calyne's audit; and `40a-the-trials.md` is
the only chapter a cold reader would guess was added — a procedural
sitting after the climax, before a second denouement (`41-epilogue.md`).
Both should be tightened, neither cut (see notes).

Notes, ranked:

1. **POV clump: four Rhovan chapters in five files.** `11-banner-war.md`,
   `12a-toll-burning.md`, `13-silk-terms.md`, `14-hostage.md` are all
   Rhovan, with only `12-out-mourned-king.md` (Ede) between. The spine
   POV goes quiet for the exact stretch where the Vyx contract — the
   book's biggest gamble of Part II — is signed. Fix: give
   `13-silk-terms.md` to Ede (she is present for the whole negotiation
   and the "we will come back for them" promise is hers); keep `14` in
   Rhovan, where the isolation is the point. One re-POV, and the clump
   dissolves.

2. **The TOURED passages are localized and cuttable.** The middle is
   lived-in *except* where the book audits floors in list-paragraphs:
   `13-silk-terms.md` (Drifts / Delvings / Rig Graves / Vent Country /
   Processional Way / Broken Doors / Peat Cuts / Echo Glades — eight
   floors, one block) and `24-anchor-chains.md`, which tours twice in
   one chapter ("Three countries lay between the chains and the fleet"
   AND "The bridge floors went by like beads told on a string"). Fix:
   in `13`, keep three floors (Drifts, Broken Doors, Peat Cuts) and cut
   the rest to a sentence; in `24`, halve the second catalog. Each keeper
   named in a list is a keeper the reader learns to skip.

3. **`31a-wakes-price.md`: cut the travelogue, keep the audit.** Lines
   describing the two-day ride down and the queue (roughly the first
   third of section two) repeat information the chapter re-delivers
   through Calyne's three papers. The chapter is Calyne's public
   spending of her seat — her last scene alive-and-winning — and it
   should open closer to the trestle table.

4. **`40a-the-trials.md`: one test case, not two.** Annis Cray and
   Corven Dale make the same structural move twice (case called, jury
   of the wronged, sentence in the Berrin form). Keep Corven Dale — he
   cites the Berrin precedent into law, which is the chapter's reason
   to exist — and compress Annis Cray to a told paragraph ("the court's
   first entry taught it the line between the willing and the starved").
   Cuts a third of the chapter and the post-climax sag with it.

5. **Two winterings, five files apart.** `16-the-molt.md` (chasm-mouth
   camps become "a town in a market-week") and `21-frosthall-parliament.md`
   / `21a` (Frosthall winter) both run the army-becomes-a-town texture.
   The two-year timeline makes two winters legitimate; the *texture*
   need not repeat. Fix is small: in `16`, trim the camp-domesticity
   paragraphs and keep the census-wall (which is that chapter's true
   subject); leave all domesticity to `21a`, which does it better.

6. For the record, ruled IN and to be defended in future iterations:
   `19a-what-roothollow-sends.md`. It is the one chapter that shows what
   the rising *is* below the army ("Not a supply-train. A country,
   walking"), it seeds the too-lucky supply trains ("never once had a
   train been taken... Good paper is a kind of armor") that pay off at
   the unmasking, and its length is earned.

**Do not touch:** the insertion set `08a`/`19a`/`21a`/`26a`/`38a` as a
system. They are why the middle of a 147k-word climb reads like a lived
war and not an itinerary; any future "tighten the middle" note that
proposes cutting one of these should be refused with prejudice.

## 9. The Bored Teenager

**Verdict: LIKED. I don't even like books this thick.**

Where I looked up from the page (the good list): Ede killing the
kneeling kobold in `03-hedge-and-hollow.md` — chapter three and the hero
does THAT; Gnarl's whole death; the runner girl saying "come and see
what your word is worth" in `10-hales-lesson.md`; walking TOWARD the
horns in `28-the-coursing.md` (best chapter in the book, fight me);
"Bread." in `33-the-welded.md` — I got actual chills; Calyne pulling his
shirt open WHILE DYING in `34-viziers-floor.md`; Nix riding the bell's
striker in `35-heralds-gate.md`; the knock in `40-the-throne.md` and
somebody thumping a fence-post a hundred floors down.

Where I looked at my phone (the honest list):

1. **`18-brother-and-sister.md` is a math homework chapter.** Two giants
   at a light-table checking couplings, then seats, then feed — "She
   tried the couplings first... She tried the seats... She tried the
   feed" — I skimmed all three probes and missed nothing, because the
   chapter tells me the answer anyway ("Three ways she went at it, and
   three ways it held"). Keep the fight, cut two of the three probes.
   The payoff in `38-scram.md` ("Who did you write in the crew-table?")
   only needs the survey to exist, not to be audited on-page.

2. **Cut entire: `40a-the-trials.md`.** The book ends on the throne
   floor — the light goes home, the chair gets scrapped for parts,
   perfect — and then there's a courtroom episode. I get what it's for
   (the Berrin sentence becomes the law of the world, fine, that's
   actually cool) but I read it at a skim and I was right to. If the
   panel won't cut it, cut half of it. The grandmother case and the
   levy-master case are the same scene twice.

3. **Every keeper is the same guy.** Meet at the gate, say one flinty
   rule, hand over the floor-trick: "Walk between the rails" / "The
   pack lies" / "Don't touch the floor" / "The gratings lie. I know
   which ones." By Part V I stopped reading keeper names because they're
   all Somethingside or Somethinggate — Sparside, Coreside, Seamside,
   Stokegate, Steamgate, Kitegate, Galesend, Hornsend, Lullside,
   Bloomside, Hoardside. I know the density is "a feature." I'm not
   asking for fewer floors; I'm asking for two or three keepers in
   `32-siege-of-outworks.md`–`33-the-welded.md` to get their rule some
   other way than the gate-greeting beat, because I could sing the beat
   by then.

4. The floor-list paragraphs in `13-silk-terms.md` and
   `24-anchor-chains.md`: skimmed, both, completely. The structural
   editor has the fix; I'm just confirming the crime scene.

5. Nobody asked, but: Nix's cord of knots in `20-long-stair.md`, "counting
   was how he wept" — that chapter is why I'd hand this to a friend who
   thinks fantasy is boring. The tower killing people with a rope and a
   sandbank and NOTHING TO HIT was scarier than any Warden.

**Do not touch:** Nix. Not one line. The census line "Goblins: one"
(`16-the-molt.md`), the soup-buckets battle-order, "I'd rather die a
nothing of somebody's" (`37-obsidian-stair.md`), holding Berrin's leash
at the end. If an iteration ever trims Nix for pace I will burn the
building down.

## 10. The Acquiring Editor

**Verdict: LOVED — I'd take this to acquisitions with the first 30
pages unchanged and a comp deck I actually believe.**

Title-to-hook time: two pages. The prologue's stolen valley — lamplit
windows rising among the stars, the dog that never stopped barking
before somebody came — is a first-five-pages that sells the book by
itself, and its last line ("the ledger decides who eats") is the whole
novel in nine words. By page ~8 (`01-trainer.md`) the cut ledger-page is
on the table and the hook is commercial as well as literary: *not a
defeat — a delivery.* The sample ends (`02-third-refusal.md` /
`03-hedge-and-hollow.md`) on two of the strongest beats available: the
shard that re-chooses in public, and the heroine executing a surrendered
prisoner from muscle memory while a spy logs it in silence.

The pitch sentence: **"The company she lost is alive in cages twenty
floors up — and the oldest friend who sold them will feed her army all
the way to the top."**

Comps, honestly meant: *Senlin Ascends* (tower-as-world, but this has
armies and grief Bancroft doesn't attempt) × the Red Wedding / beloved-
traitor engine of *A Storm of Swords*, with the fellowship warmth of
*The Lord of the Rings* carried by a goblin who reads like Watership
Down grew a tally-stick. The mercy taxonomy (cure / eviction / a death
that should not have been needed) is the fresh mechanic the genre
conversation will latch onto.

Notes, ranked:

1. **Commercially brave, keep at all costs:** the kobold killing in
   `03-hedge-and-hollow.md` on ~page 28. Marketing will flinch; it is
   the thesis of the book performed in one stroke, and the Rhovan
   pen-scratch that follows ("see that she is never absolved cheaply")
   is the best character-establishing beat in the sample. Any iteration
   that softens it kills the acquisition case.

2. **One trim in the sample:** `01-trainer.md` opens with two full drill
   exchanges (Brackjaw counts) before Rhovan enters. The first exchange
   is charming and load-bearing (it pays off when Brackjaw dies by the
   counts in `03`); the second is warm-up. Cut or halve the second and
   the grey man at the yard's edge arrives a page sooner.

3. **Commercially risky, priced in:** ~147k words and forty-plus named
   keepers. The density is the audience's pleasure — but it makes the
   front-matter plan (the spine-tall cross-section plate, the Roothollow
   map) a *requirement*, not a decoration. Flag for production now.

4. **The interludes are a selling point — say so on the jacket.**
   Malgrim's ledger voice ("Grief is a bond that matures; every cage she
   opens, she carries. Let her portage." — `08x-interlude-1.md`) is
   quotable villainy of the kind that gets screenshotted. Excerpt one in
   the marketing copy.

5. Minor: the sample's only stumble for a cold reader is the unexplained
   glow-vocabulary in `01` (Stone of the Climb, north face) resolving a
   page later than it's used. One clause earlier — it already exists in
   the text ("where nothing glows") — could move up. Polish-level; do
   not spend an edit slot on it unless one is free.

**Do not touch:** the prologue. It is the best opening I have read in a
submission in years, and the fact that its child is never named — and
that the reader will not know whose eyes those were for forty chapters —
is the engine of the reread market. No name, no hint, no italic
"somewhere, a boy remembered."

## Critic 2 — The Literary Critic

**Verdict: LIKED.**

The prose is far above the genre's waterline — concrete, elegiac, funny in
the right mouths, and capable of real terror without adjectives (the
prologue; the Welding Halls; Malgrim's ledger interludes). What keeps it
from LOVED is not weakness but *habit*: a handful of pet constructions
fired often enough to become audible machinery, and one mechanical
inconsistency no reader should ever have to forgive.

### Notes

**2.1 — The dialogue punctuation changes convention three times.**
Most of the book speaks in double quotes. Then five consecutive files —
`24-anchor-chains.md`, `25-storm-queens-price.md`,
`26-gathering-the-blown.md`, `26a-bottled-court.md`, `27-the-eye.md` —
switch wholesale to Continental dashes:

> — This one is sound, — said the chain-keeper beside her.
> (24-anchor-chains.md)

> — We're not asking the wind, — Ede said. (25-storm-queens-price.md)

Then the book reverts to double quotes, and then the final stretch —
`39-last-door.md`, `40-the-throne.md`, `41-epilogue.md` — switches again,
to single quotes:

> 'That is my banner,' she said. (39-last-door.md)
> 'Well, hostage,' said Vyx. (41-epilogue.md)

Three conventions in one novel reads as three drafts stitched together.
**Fix: normalize every chapter to double quotes.** Purely mechanical, zero
risk, highest value-per-keystroke of anything in this review.

**2.2 — The "held breath" is the book's most over-drawn breath.**
Ten occurrences of the held-breath/breath-let-go image, including the same
stair-lift sentence used verbatim twice:

> "the stair-lift's stone split its seam with a sound like a held breath
> let go" (04-king-of-nothing.md)
> "the seam of the stair-lift split behind the shield-wall with a sound
> like a held breath let go" (07-red-warcamp.md)

Plus 03-hedge-and-hollow.md ("like a held breath let out"),
09-the-quiet.md, 20-long-stair.md (twice — "quiet as a held breath" AND
"like a held breath let go"), 21a-the-wintering.md, 26a-bottled-court.md,
38a-stolen-years.md. The single best use is the Kingsbarrow folding shut —
"a sound like a breath let go after a thousand years of holding"
(12-out-mourned-king.md) — and every repetition before and after taxes it.
**Fix: keep 12 and one early lift-opening; vary or cut the rest.**

**2.3 — "The time a lamp takes to gutter" gutters.**
Six near-identical uses (12a-toll-burning.md, 17-frozen-sea.md,
25-storm-queens-price.md, 27-the-eye.md, 38-scram.md, 38a-stolen-years.md).
The no-minutes diction rule is right, and the book already owns the good
variant — "the time a lamp takes to burn a finger's width"
(05-ledger-of-gates.md). The one keeper is 38a, where Nix is literally
watching a guttering lamp. **Fix: vary the others against the world's own
clock-cupboard (a kettle coming to boil, a ladle of iron cooling, a
whetstone's pass).**

**2.4 — Metafiction in Rhovan's mouth.**
> "One chapter late," he said, to nobody, an elf's reckoning of a debt
> (35-heralds-gate.md)

A "chapter" is not an in-world unit of time; this is the outline winking
through the page at the reader ("her gales arrive one chapter late"). It
is the only place in 147,000 words where I heard an author instead of an
elf. **Fix: "A battle late," or "Half a day late." One word.**

**2.5 — Nix's counting refrain double-fires.**
"Counting was how he wept" appears verbatim in 04-king-of-nothing.md and
again in 07-red-warcamp.md; and 20-long-stair.md runs the same
construction twice inside one chapter — "counting was how he held the
world still enough to look at" and, 150 lines later, "counting was how you
held a thing still enough to see what it really was." The refrain is one
of the book's best inventions; over-firing teaches the reader to skim it.
**Fix: keep 04's "counting was how he wept: one" (unimprovable), cut or
rephrase 07's repeat and one of 20's pair.**

**2.6 — Sliver's calling-card is dealt from too thick a deck.**
"I have carried forty-one climbers" fires in 03, 04 (variant), 09, 15, 19
(variant), 21, and pays beautifully at 39-last-door.md ("She was my
fourth. She was the first of them stubborn enough to matter"). At
15-calyne.md and 21-frosthall-parliament.md the formula is furniture — the
sentence works without it. **Fix: cut the two decorative uses so the
payoff at 39 lands on a rested ear.**

**2.7 — One simile, two owners.**
"Mild as milk" belongs to Rhovan in 02-third-refusal.md and to Weck the
toll-captain in 05-ledger-of-gates.md, three chapters apart. A simile that
distinctive is a fingerprint; two men can't share it. **Fix: keep
Rhovan's, replace Weck's.**

**2.8 — (Advisory, no edit demanded this round.)** Nearly every chapter
closes on a burnished epigram — "The counts were about to change."
(02-third-refusal.md); "The rest was owed, and morning would come
collecting." (07-red-warcamp.md); "He was, for once in his fractional
life, entirely right." (05-ledger-of-gates.md). Each is good; forty-eight
in a row is a mannerism. In a later iteration, let two or three quiet
chapters end plain.

**Do not touch:** `00-prologue.md`. "It rained soil for a while after.
Clods, and slates off a shaved roof, and one bruised windfall apple,
which he picked up and held in both hands and could not eat." The best
opening pages in modern epic fantasy that I have read, and the ending —
the loaves still in the cold oven — is the whole book in a kitchen. Not
one word.

## Critic 5 — The First-Time Reader

**Verdict: LIKED.**

I came to this knowing nothing and was never bored across 147,000 words —
the keeper-of-the-floor structure teaches you how to read it by chapter
three, and the scale, which I feared would be the confusing part, is the
clear part. I was genuinely lost exactly once (the winter), and I stumbled
on names more than a book this disciplined should allow.

### Notes

**5.1 — The winter broke my timeline, and I flipped back three times.**
`14-hostage.md` ends with Rhovan released **at the thaw**: "The thaw came
to the tier above; the trains moved; and... the clauses let him go." Then
`15-calyne.md` opens at the **start** of that same winter — "the first
true cold of the wintering in her teeth" — with Rhovan already back
("He had come up from the Broodthrone a fortnight past"), and closes "on
the last night before the freeze closed the high road." Then
`16-the-molt.md` re-narrates the Broodthrone passage I'd already watched
in 13-silk-terms.md, and runs the wintering and census after 15 already
ordered them. I honestly checked whether there were two winters. There
aren't. **Fix (smallest surgery I can see): end the hostage clause at
first-freeze — the rear-guard has cleared and the trains have stopped, so
Vyx discharges the warranty when the road shuts, not at the thaw — and
give 16-the-molt one signpost sentence marking it as the fuller telling of
the passage.**

**5.2 — Berrin's age doesn't reach the Theft.**
The eighty-years-since-the-Theft frame is otherwise perfectly graspable —
the style guide's math held every time I checked it — except on the one
character it matters most. The prologue boy is seven on the night of the
Theft, and at his trial "the collar had been put on him young, on the
night of the Theft" (36-malgrim.md). But he is "a stout man of sixty"
(12a-toll-burning.md) and "sixty years old, flour on his cuff"
(34-viziers-floor.md) — twenty years short of possible. The prologue
compounds it: the clerk's expression is one "he would spend the next
fifty years learning to make himself" (00-prologue.md) — fifty puts the
feast thirty years early. When I re-read the prologue after the unmasking
(the book earns that re-read; it's wonderful), the arithmetic snapped the
spell. **Fix: delete the stated ages ("an old man" carries every scene)
and make the prologue's "fifty years" something unnumbered — "the rest of
his life."**

**5.3 — The keeper name-pool recycles, and I built wrong pictures.**
Six Wends (Silkstead in 09/10, Threadneedle in 13, Latchlight in 20,
Linkside in 32, Sootside in 34, of Firststep in 37), five Brunas, four
Daras, three Meras, three Naels, three Grints, two Mags. Two failures
were real, not cosmetic:
- `10-hales-lesson.md`: "the van to hold the Smelterworks under **Bruna**
  and the banner" — the first Bruna in the entire book, bare, no surname,
  never seen again. I assumed I'd forgotten someone and went hunting.
- `13-silk-terms.md`: bare "Wend" reading threads at Vyx's court, one
  tier-arc after a different bare "Wend" barred her vault against the
  rising at the Broodwarrens (10-hales-lesson.md) — two silk floors, two
  Wends, and for half a chapter I thought Silkstead had forgiven us.
**Fix: rename the incidental colliders (the Smelterworks Bruna above all;
one or two Wends), and attach the surname at every re-entry of the rest.
Do not thin the keepers themselves — the density is the world.**

**5.4 — Wat Kello becomes "Marn" and I thought I'd skipped a chapter.**
`33-the-welded.md` gives the unsealed soldier a name I'd waited five
floors for — Wat Kello, "iron from the jaw down," who says "Bread." Then
`39-last-door.md` has the banner "carried... by **Marn of the thirty**,
the man freed at the Welding Halls, iron from the jaw down," and
`41-epilogue.md` raises the banner with "Marn's one uncovered hand" —
while a *different* Marn (Marn Deepfurrow, the wagon-matron) died and was
buried in 21a-the-wintering.md. If Marn-of-the-thirty is Wat, one name is
wrong; if he's a second welded man, he cannot wear Wat's exact
description. **Fix: unify on Wat Kello.**

**5.5 — Praise, filed so nobody "fixes" it: the scale works.**
I understood the tower's size before I needed it: the lift rising into "a
flat white glare that was no sun's" with hedges to the horizon
(02-third-refusal.md); the far ceiling as "a faint dark grain far
overhead... at the top of miles of sky" (03-hedge-and-hollow.md); and
then the mid-book masterstroke — Nix going home and finding "there was no
tower. From the yards of Roothollow there was a wall, and the wall was
the sky" (19a-what-roothollow-sends.md). Not one floor is crossed in an
afternoon. Whatever else changes, this stays.

**5.6 — (Minor.) One breadcrumb short on the banner-count.**
Forty-one banners (15-calyne.md), sixty-one (21-frosthall-parliament.md),
seventy (30-what-ede-carries.md), a hundred (40-the-throne.md) — the
ladder is trackable, but at `25-storm-queens-price.md` ("Fifty-eight
banners; by the charter each spoke once") the number arrives after the
longest gap and I paused to re-derive it. One clause — "fifty-eight now,
the wintering's musters counted in" — would have carried me over.

**Do not touch:** `19a-what-roothollow-sends.md`. The down-and-back
chapter is the single most orienting thing in the book — the supply
queue, the moot voting the seed-corn, the mason's little gates cut beside
the thirty names. If any pass ever proposes cutting it for pace, refuse.

## Critic 8 — The Thematic Reader

**Verdict: LOVED.**

The mercy engine is *argued*, not declaimed, and the argument is honest
because the book lets mercy fail: Gnarl refuses the Break-collar and dies
on his own punchline (04-king-of-nothing.md); Skarn dies believing the
collar a rank (07-red-warcamp.md); Malgrim holds the halves of his broken
chain and "nothing in his eyes changed at all" (36-malgrim.md). Three
willing collars, three refusals, each costlier and better reasoned — that
ladder is the book's spine, and the philosophy never once leaves a
character's mouth. The keystone audit below finds four clean, two
migrated-and-improved, and two genuinely broken plants that need this
iteration's knife.

### Keystone audit

- **K1 (judgment)** — PLANTED AND PAID. Verbatim at the fire in
  03-hedge-and-hollow.md ("Spend your certainty slowly. It is the only
  coin in this tower that does not come back"); paid at 36-malgrim.md
  ("she had learned — it had taken her a tower — to spend her certainty
  slowly") in the very sentence where she hands Berrin's judgment to the
  thirty. The echo is narration rather than speech — acceptable; her
  *act* is the speech.
- **K2 (the wanting)** — PLANTED AND PAID, slightly over-glossed (note
  8.6). Pol Fairstead, 09-the-quiet.md: "The tower is only the stone...
  Now break the wanting." Paid at 40-the-throne.md: the knock of a
  hundred banners refusing the throne.
- **K3 (the stair)** — CLEAN. Hrimgar at the signing
  (21-frosthall-parliament.md); echoed at the muster,
  35-heralds-gate.md: "not a tower. A stair."
- **K4 (the chosen collar)** — REWRITTEN AND IMPROVED. The spec lines are
  gone (correctly — anti-copy rule) and what stands in
  36-malgrim.md is stronger: "Yours freed you. Mine fed me. You will
  forgive a poor man for not seeing the difference from inside," answered
  by "What loves you lets you set it down." That answer is the line
  readers will carry out of this book. He refuses; the tragedy holds.
- **K5 (the forge-oath)** — **BROKEN PLANT** (note 8.2).
- **K6 (the wyrm's pity)** — **BROKEN PLANT** (note 8.1).
- **K7 (steps)** — PERFECT. 20-long-stair.md ("Big folk count floors.
  Little folk count steps") → 41-epilogue.md ("Turns out you climb a
  tower the same way you leave one. One true step, then the next").
- **K8 (the banner)** — MIGRATED, AND BETTER. The keystone left Dun's
  handover (08-what-the-freed-forgive.md) and lives in Aldis's words in
  08a-first-banner.md: "a banner doesn't march facing forward. A banner
  is read from *behind*... So sew what they need to read. A wall." The
  epilogue's last image — the banner leaning on the horse trough —
  closes it exactly as designed.

### Notes

**8.1 — K6 is paid but never planted. One sentence fixes it.**
`38a-stolen-years.md` quotes it as established memory: "The Wyrm had said
it of him, a hundred floors ago: *he built the one tower tall enough to
see the whole world from, and he has never once looked.*" But Cindermaw
never says it — her speech in `19-wyrms-pity.md` is the (excellent)
prisoner-and-cell passage, and the "never once looked" line appears
nowhere in the book before its own echo. A first-rate payoff citing a
nonexistent plant is the exact failure this pass exists to catch. **Fix:
give Cindermaw the line in 19-wyrms-pity.md — it slots naturally after
"he is the only one who can never leave."**

**8.2 — K5's oath arrives unplanted, a rabbit from a hat.**
The oath that decides the tower's fate — "Light no fire I cannot quench.
Keep no fire past its work. And answer for every hour it burns"
(38-scram.md) — is heard nowhere before the moment Vega demands Ulfar
recite it. The spec's "We do not own what we make. We owe it" appears
nowhere at all; its nearest cousin is 24-anchor-chains.md's "Craft like
this is a debt." The scram scene *works*, but it works as theater;
planted, it would work as memory keeping a promise. **Fix: half-quote one
clause early, in Vega's own mouth — 06-the-adit.md, beside "I make things
that hold" ("my mother made us answer for every hour a fire burned"), or
at the light-table in 18-brother-and-sister.md where the mother's forge
is already invoked.**

**8.3 — The hunt-rhyme is banked and never spent by the person who
banked it.** The plant is exact: the fair's children skipping rope to
"run from the horn and the horn runs after; walk to the horn and the horn
falls silent," and Ede hearing it "the way you bank a coal against a
colder year" (09-the-quiet.md). But at the covert in `28-the-coursing.md`
she stands and walks on Rhovan's kennel-law alone; the coal never comes
back to her. **Fix: one line as she gets her feet under her — the
skipping-rhyme surfacing in her own memory — so the bravest walk in the
book belongs to her banked coal, not his lore.**

**8.4 — Hale's gate-rule plants itself inside its own payoff.**
`22-the-feast.md`: "He had a rule from the fighting-pits... lose your
sword, lose your teeth, but keep your gate. Ede had heard it at seventeen
and carried it thirty years." All of that is told to us in the paragraph
where he dies keeping the gate — assertion standing in for a plant. Hale
is on the page from 03-hedge-and-hollow.md and never says it. **Fix: put
the rule in Hale's mouth at 03 (he is pricing her recruits; it is exactly
what he'd say), and cut the explanatory sentence from 22. The south door
then collects a debt the reader has held for nineteen chapters.**

**8.5 — The trials: three-quarters court, one-quarter pageant.**
The true test of the mercy engine already happened, and it is
magnificent: at `36-malgrim.md` rope gets six named votes inside
Shieldwall, the parole is invented by the wronged, and Ede confesses the
engine's cost — "she would have hanged him... the thirty had taken the
one thing Ede Stonhal had wanted for herself... and given it to *him.*"
That is mercy tested to the bone. `40a-the-trials.md`, though, lets every
jury reach the right answer within a page — Annis Cray reclassified,
Corven Dale sentenced-and-bettered, the two refusers let walk — and then
*concedes in narration* what it declines to dramatize: "the court would
misplace it a hundred times in the years ahead." **Fix: dramatize one
misplacement — one jury goes too hard (or too easy), and Ede seals the
verdict anyway, because the court's independence from her IS the
sentence. Half a page, and the chapter is a court.**

**8.6 — K2's payoff over-explains itself.**
`40-the-throne.md`, after the knock: "And standing in that stillness Ede
understood at last what was being refused... the prison that outlives
every stone it was learned in... The knock was the wanting breaking, a
hundred floors at once, one more collar, the last one." The plant (Pol,
09-the-quiet.md) and Ede's two-word answer ("You've heard them") have
already done this work; the exegesis is the book's one moment of
declaiming its thesis. **Fix: cut or halve the explanatory sentences;
keep "the knock was the wanting breaking" if one clause must stay.**

**8.7 — (Optional, low rank.) Sliver's re-choosing plays as pure
triumph.** The plan's ashamed beat — a shard that should not be able to
want — is absent from 02-third-refusal.md, so the confession at
30-what-ede-carries.md ("I did not tell you, because you would look at me
the way you are trying very hard not to look at me now") has a little
less floor under it than it deserves. One dry, uneasy clause at the gate
would do it. Only if a light hand is available.

**Do not touch:** Malgrim entire — the ledger interludes, the parley at
36-malgrim.md, and the sentence of Berrin Or. "Kind, reasonable, wrong"
is the best-executed dark mirror in the genre since the great ones, and
the parole — "You don't get to die until it balances, and I count slow" —
is the book's thesis wearing boots. No hand touches the parole.

## 4. THE CONTINUITY WARDEN

**Verdict: MIXED** — the book's spine is astonishingly sound for a draft this
size (the traitor's paper trail reconciles end to end; Dun is one-armed from
`07` on without a slip; Calyne enters in `15` and dies in `34` exactly as
chartered; the Break-collar cost ladder pays at every rung), but the draft is
carrying one renamed character, one broken company-arithmetic, and a
systematic inflation of tower-era time-spans that violates the Theft-age hard
canon in at least ten places. Everything below is fixable with line surgery.
Nothing below requires a rewrite.

### W1. Wat Kello is renamed "Marn" from `38a-stolen-years.md` onward — the draft's worst error

The welded Shieldwall soldier — the witness whose one word convicts Berrin —
is **Wat Kello** in the three chapters that create him:

- `33-the-welded.md`: "Wat Kello. Five years on the Stone of the Climb in
  Roothollow… 'Bread,' said Wat Kello."
- `34-viziers-floor.md`: "Say your word, Wat."
- `36-malgrim.md`: "Then it came around the fire to Wat Kello, who had been
  welded… 'Rope's a wage,' Wat said."

Then, with no death and no explanation, he becomes **Marn**:

- `38a-stolen-years.md`: "furled on Marn's shoulder, the oldest banner in the
  world went up the stair…"
- `39-last-door.md`: "Marn of the thirty carried it now, the man freed at the
  Welding Halls, iron from the jaw down, who had carried it since the
  Herald's Gate…" — unambiguously the same man.
- `40-the-throne.md`: "Marn, standing under his banner on the summit… told
  Nix it was the first hour in twenty years that the iron had weighed
  nothing at all."
- `41-epilogue.md`: "raised over the drill-yard by Marn's one uncovered hand
  and Tam's two."

Worse: **Marn is already a beloved corpse.** `21a-the-wintering.md` spends a
full scene burying "Marn Deepfurrow… died in the second month, in her sleep."
A reader who wept at that grave meets "Marn" carrying the banner three parts
later and reasonably concludes the dead woman got up. FIX: he is Wat Kello in
all four late files (and note `40`'s "twenty years" must be "five" — see W3).

### W2. The Shieldwall company is thirty-one people in Part I and one hundred and twelve from `08` on

- `01-trainer.md`: "She had led thirty-one people into a mine and it was
  never a battle at all." The bill of sale reads "*persons, thirty and
  some.*"
- `02-third-refusal.md`: "I led thirty-one people up that stair once and I
  came down alone."
- `08-what-the-freed-forgive.md`: "A hundred and twelve had marched behind
  her into this floor… Thirty names she had cut… The cages had given back
  sixty-three. That left nineteen… *sent above*." (30 + 63 + 19 = 112 —
  internally perfect.)
- `08a-first-banner.md`: "Hundred and twelve names in the dark."

The 112-arithmetic is the better and more load-bearing one (Dun's roll-song,
the nineteen "sent above" who become the Welded of `33`). FIX in Part I: let
the bill undersell ("one company, standards, stores, and persons, a hundred
and some" — or keep "thirty and some" as the *sworn shields* and have Ede
correct it bitterly: thirty-one swords, and eighty-one souls behind them).
Whichever way, `01`, `02` and the bill must agree with `08`.

A related knot: if she believed all 112 dead, why did she cut exactly the
thirty names that later prove to be the actual dead (`07`: "Ede Stonhal had
cut thirty names into the Stone of the Climb with her own hand")? One
half-line in `01` — the thirty were the *confirmed* dead, the bodies the
rout brought out; the rest she mourned unmarked because she could not know —
closes it.

### W3. The Warcamp is five years before chapter one (hard canon) — and the draft says nine, two, and twenty

The five-year count holds in most places (`07` opening, `08`, `33`, `34`:
"sold five years since"). Violations, each needing one word changed:

- `01-trainer.md`: "**Nine years** she had mourned her thirty as dead" —
  should be five.
- `07-red-warcamp.md`: "**Two years**, and the cages, and he knew her at a
  word" — Skarn last saw her five years ago.
- `28-the-coursing.md`: "what **twenty years** in them had made of her
  thirty" — five years in the cages; and thirty lines later "an old habit
  from a company **twenty years** dead" — seven at most (five + the climb).
- `40-the-throne.md`: "the first hour in **twenty years** that the iron had
  weighed nothing" — Wat was welded five years.
- `41-epilogue.md`: "a farm girl… **born the year of the Warcamp**, who
  would be tall enough for the wall in a season or two" — that child is
  seven. Either she was born the year the *climb began*, or she is simply
  young; as written she is a toddler drilling with a banner.

### W4. Berrin's chronology cannot currently be drawn on one line

The prologue boy IS Berrin (the counting, the bread, the clerk — beautifully
done). But:

- Theft = eighty years before the rising (hard canon; `K3`, Cindermaw, Pell
  all say eighty). The prologue boy is seven at the Theft → Berrin is
  **~87** at chapter one.
- `11-banner-war.md`: "a stout man of **sixty** with flour on his sleeves";
  `34-viziers-floor.md`: "Berrin Or stood up slowly at the charter-table,
  **sixty years old**."
- `00-prologue.md`: "the expression he would spend the next **fifty years**
  learning to make" — fifty lands him at 57, not 87.
- `36-malgrim.md` (the trial): "the collar had been put on him young, **on
  the night of the Theft**" — but the prologue shows a fed child and a
  written name, no collar; `34` shows a collar "**twenty years** polished by
  the skin"; `14-hostage.md` dates his one climb-and-return to "twenty years
  gone"; `23x-interlude-3.md` says he "knelt" on a particular night, and
  `33` says he had been "selling for twenty years."

The twenty-year collar is the version the plot runs on (Vyx's web, the
private road, the sale). FIX: make Berrin old — near ninety and vigorous is
*better* for the character ("nobody in the low world was better to his
neighbors" for two generations) — change "sixty" in `11`/`34`, "fifty years"
in the prologue, and rewrite the trial line in `36` so the *lesson* is from
the night of the Theft and the *kneeling* is twenty years gone: the clerk
fed him at seven; he went up the tower at seventy to ask for the hand that
fed. That aligns prologue, `14`, `23x`, `33`, `34`, and `36` at a stroke.

### W5. Tower-era spans keep leaking into centuries (Theft-age hard canon)

The style guide is explicit: tower-era spans read in decades and lifetimes,
never centuries. The stolen realms' own histories may be ancient — and the
draft mostly walks that line well (the Kingsfield's thousand years of
musters, the barrows' old dead: all fine). These are not fine:

- `13-silk-terms.md`: Vyx — "The Wake has been declining my correspondence
  for **three centuries**." Rhovan founds the Wake "in the first winter
  after the Theft" *on the same page*, and `15-calyne.md` confirms
  "**Fourscore years** of silence."
- `31a-wakes-price.md`: "For **a thousand years** the Wake had been a rumor
  with a purse" and Calyne's "I was the Wake's eyes for **three hundred
  years**" — both impossible; both should be fourscore.
- `31-breaking-the-hunt.md`: "ten thousand years of law holding against
  **ten years** of weld" — the same file says the Master wakes "from
  **fourscore years**." The weld is eighty years old, not ten.
- `31-breaking-the-hunt.md`: Vega "had shod horses for **four hundred
  years**" — Vega is in "her fortieth year at the anvil" (`06-the-adit.md`).
- `25-storm-queens-price.md`: "a tempest **a thousand years** old," "worn by
  a thousand years of crawling courtiers"; `26a-bottled-court.md`: "a crag
  where the wind had not rested in **a thousand years**." The tempest is the
  tower's welding — Zephyra "kept a sky-realm well for forty years before
  the tower made me weather" — so the storm is eighty years old. (The
  *cellars* being a thousand years of the realm's own husbandry is fine;
  the storm is not.)
- `39-last-door.md`: "the failed climbers of **five ages**";
  `40-the-throne.md`: "the fallen of **five ages** folded into its grain."
  Cindermaw counts **three** armies in eighty years (`19-wyrms-pity.md`).
- `14-hostage.md`: the wrapped knight "Taken in the Men's wars, **four tiers
  up, ninety years gone**" — ninety predates the Theft, and "four tiers up"
  is tower grammar. Make it "fourscore years gone, in the tower's first
  fury" or the like.
- Rhovan's own age wobbles: "three hundred years" of trade (`11`, `12a`,
  `31`) vs "for the second time in **fourscore years**, the entire truth"
  (`37-obsidian-stair.md`). An elf may be three hundred years *old*; his
  *Wake service* is bounded by eighty and his service under Calyne is
  "twenty years" (`23`). Pick the register once and sweep.

### W6. Sliver is "the last light of the shardkind" — but the census counted 212 living shards in this host

- `16-the-molt.md`: "the shardminds asked to be counted… Souls of crystal:
  **two hundred and twelve**."
- `38-scram.md`: "*I am lit, alone. I am the last light of the shardkind.*"

Either 211 chosen companions died in the scram without one line of grief —
unthinkable in a book that buries everyone — or the claim is wrong. Two
clean fixes: (a) in `38`, one paragraph — the host's carried shards gutter
and *hold*, because chosen shards burn on their bearers, and what dies is
the unchosen network; Sliver's line becomes "the last that remembers the
network," which is truer to `30` anyway; or (b) cut the census line. I
recommend (a): the image of two hundred splinters flickering at two hundred
throats in the same breath is free and devastating.

### W7. The banner ladder wobbles against itself and against the anchors

Anchor: forty at the census, seventy at the muster below the Made, a hundred
at the Herald's Gate. In draft:

- `11-banner-war.md`: "**Forty-one** banners went up the freed stair" (floor
  30 — already past the census target ten floors early).
- `14-hostage.md` ("the count is 41 banners") and `15-calyne.md` ("Nine
  peoples and **forty-one** banners") vs `16-the-molt.md` census: "Banners:
  **forty**" (and `16x` echoes forty). Forty-one and forty cannot both stand
  a week apart.
- `20-long-stair.md`: "**A hundred banners** furled and carried flat" at
  floor 59 — figure of speech, but it detonates the ladder one file before
  `21-frosthall-parliament.md` counts "**Sixty-one** banners hung from the
  rafters."
- `25-storm-queens-price.md`: "**Fifty-eight** banners" vote at floor 70 —
  three fewer than the Frosthall with no loss accounted (if three banners
  burned at the feast or dissolved in the scattering, one clause says so;
  the vote arithmetic 27+27+4 = 58 is otherwise lovely).
- `30-what-ede-carries.md` "Seventy banners" and `31`/`34`/`35` "hundred" —
  correct per anchor.

FIX: make the census line 40 or 41 everywhere (pick one), replace `20`'s
"hundred banners" with "the banners, all of them, furled," and give `25` a
half-line for the missing three.

### W8. Smaller contradictions, each one line

- **Ulfar's plan**: "**Thirty-one years**" three times in
  `18-brother-and-sister.md`; "**Sixty years**, Vega" and "sixty years long"

## 6. THE CHARACTER SURGEON

**Verdict: LOVED** — every turn on the charter list runs on visible rails,
no change of heart is bought off-page, and the women are the book's spine
rather than its scenery. My notes are about three seams, not about surgery.

### S1. Every named turn is earned on-page — verify stands

- **Hale**: annexes the party by writ (`03`), massacres the dig-crew and is
  cast out (`06`), carries out his own dead and asks the one question ("How
  does the goblin do it?" — `10-hales-lesson.md`), names the warren's dead
  at the mound with Nix feeding him the names (`12-out-mourned-king.md`),
  and — the masterstroke — migrates bench by bench across a winter:
  "Then one week he was mending them six benches in. Then four."
  (`21a-the-wintering.md`). By the time he keeps his gate at the south door
  (`22`), the reader has watched every inch of the road. The Boromir seat,
  filled correctly.
- **Ulfar**: arrives with wrong reasons stated aloud ("We have come to
  inherit it," `17`), loses the mathematics argument *without losing the
  mathematics* (`18` — Vega concedes "The figures are sound"), banks the
  quarrel through a parliament clause (`21`), votes to fly (`25`), and is
  turned at the scram not by ethics but by the Fuel Pens and his mother's
  oath — "She saw a carried digit fall" (`38-scram.md`) is the best
  single-sentence character beat in the book. Nothing off-page.
- **Voss**: the toll-chain (`05`), the drumhead peace (`11`), and the
  conversion scene that states its own limits — "I am not converted…
  I am *invested*" (`12a-toll-burning.md`) — then never once backslides
  into virtue: GOODWILL as a ledger title (`41`) keeps him greedy to the
  last page. Exactly what the outline ordered: used, never caught.
- **Calyne**: suspected for three parts and never once softened to buy
  sympathy — she pays her seat for the grain queue in `31a` ("It was
  arithmetic, Rhovan, not sentiment") and dies mid-proof hauling Berrin's
  shirt open (`34`). The Snape seat, played without one wink of secret
  warmth. Her posthumous presence (the blood-margined cipher-book Ede will
  not wipe) is perfect.
- **Zephyra**: the refusal (`25`) is a real vote with real arithmetic
  (27–27–4, the general casting), her price is stated in her own mouth, her
  vengeance is contractually exact ("She kept the two parts… She is a queen
  of her word"), and `26a-bottled-court.md` retro-earns her whole reign —
  the bottled court is the single best addition the inserted chapters make
  to any character. Her late gales (`35`) land as a kept, worthless word,
  as chartered.
- **Berrin**: see the Warden's reread. From this chair I add: the feast
  arrow (`22`) survives the reread *because* the text never decides whether
  he saved Ede from his masters' arrow or performed a scripted rescue —
  and `23x` ("nothing in it is false but the sum") licenses the ambiguity.
  Keep it unresolved forever.
- **Nix and Vega** hold their rails so well they are load-bearing for
  everyone else's (Nix's parole of Berrin is the sentence of the whole
  book; Vega's "some things are not broken. Some things are *made wrong*"
  in `37` is her arc completing in one line).

### S2. The one unearned note: Rhovan's "loved her" arrives from nowhere

`37-obsidian-stair.md`: "I blackmailed her at Roothollow on the first
morning and **loved her before the Broodthrone**." Whatever the register —
comrade-love or something quieter and older — the page record between `05`
and `13` shows only professional fencing: shares divided, fractions fed.
The claim reaches back across a stretch where the reader saw nothing
tender. This is the book's only off-page change of heart. Two fixes, either
cheap: (a) one quiet beat in Part II — `12a` already sends him down the
burned toll-road alone with her trust; let him notice *that* trust land,
one sentence of interior; or (b) soften the mirror-line to "and was hers
before the Broodthrone," which the fealty record fully earns. I prefer (a).

### S3. The trio evolves between beats — except in the wintering, where the book's best quiet chapter forgets two of them

The brief asks where one earned quiet beat would fix a jump. Here:
`21a-the-wintering.md` is the finest "quiet ligament" chapter in the draft —
and it spends its whole warmth on Dun, Hale, Vega, Nix, and Ulfar. Ede
appears only as the presiding general; Rhovan does not appear at all.
Consequences downstream: when Vega says "the arm I would give to stop you
is the bad one" (`28`) and when Ede reads Rhovan "all of it, no fractions"
(`31`), the intimacy is real but was mostly welded in battle. One scene in
`21a` — Ede at the smithy failing to thread a needle, or Rhovan at the
settle being taught the lock-stitch — would earn both later beats *and*
patch S2, at a cost of half a page in a chapter built to absorb it.

### S4. Berrin's trial confession contradicts his own origin — a motive note, not just a continuity one

`36-malgrim.md`: "the collar had been put on him young, on the night of the
Theft… not an excuse but an inventory." The prologue's actual devastation
is that *nothing* was put on him — he was fed, counted, and taught that
"the ledger decides who eats," and he chose the collar himself, as an
adult, twenty years ago (`14`, `23x`, `34`). The confession as drafted
trades the book's most original villainy (chosen, in daylight, for the
being-necessary — Malgrim: "it purchases what he asked for on the night he
knelt") for standard-issue childhood determinism. Rewrite the confession to
match the prologue: the lesson at seven, the kneeling at seventy. It is
crueler and truer.

### S5. The women read as people — with one flag on distribution

Ede, Vega, Calyne, Aldis, Mereth Wakesend, Bruna Hearthold, Orla Floeside,
Wend Latchlight, Dara, Marta Reed, Edda Trench, Pell of the Years — the
keeper-women are the book's connective tissue and not one of them is a
role. Note in passing: the *antagonist* women (Vyx, Zephyra) get richer
interiority than some allied men (Ulfar aside, the Frosthall thanes blur) —
that is a strength, not a fault; log it so nobody "fixes" it. Aldis Bright
is handled with exactly the right restraint after `39`: she argues footwork
in the epilogue instead of delivering wisdom. Keep her small.

### S6. Ede's arc closes on the correct wound

The flaw chartered in the outline — "she would rather die making amends
than live being followed" — is paid in the one place it costs most:
"the thirty had taken the one thing Ede Stonhal had wanted for herself
since the Red Warcamp, and they had given it to *him*" (`36-malgrim.md`).
That paragraph is the novel's moral engine finishing its stroke. My only
note is protective: `40a-the-trials.md` restates the lesson ("Mercy was not
the wage of the strong. It was the discipline of the tired") — one
restatement is right; do not let later iterations add a third.

**Do not touch:** the trial of Berrin Or (`36-malgrim.md`) — the roll-call
with the dead answering *held*, Wat's "Rope's a wage. Paid once. Done,"
Nix's parole speech, and Ede sealing a sentence that steals her own
absolution. It is the book's thesis executed as drama, and every line of it
is earned by something planted earlier.

## 7. THE BATTLE CHOREOGRAPHER

**Verdict: LIKED** — five of the seven set-pieces can be drawn from the text
alone, which is a rarer thing than authors know. The feast is a model of
legible catastrophe. My notes are two mid-battle camera problems, one
plan/execution mismatch, and one scale seam at the Gate.

### C1. `07-red-warcamp.md` — drawable, but the climax retreats to hearsay twice

The ground is excellent: three companies with three doors ("the Muster at
the Broadgate, loud on purpose… the Wrights at the shaft-mouth… the Van's
one thin lane running up the Paylane") and the reader always knows where
Ede is. But at the two peaks the narration steps out of the battle into
retrospect:

> "Afterward, the histories made the locks the quiet part, and the
> histories were wrong."

> "It was Nix who silenced it. **Later she pieced it together from a dozen
> tellings**: the goblin at the third drum-tower…"

The drum-silencing is the emotional crest of the assault and it is
delivered secondhand, mid-action, inside Ede's POV. She can *see* the third
drum-tower from the Paylane — the chapter says the towers stand over the
tent-lines. Give her three live sentences (the small figure on the frame,
the drums dying one by one) and keep "counting, lips moving" as the
close-up she genuinely can't see; save the "histories" frame for the
chapter's last page, where it already works.

### C2. `11`/`12` (Tithe Barrows and Kingsbarrow) — geography holds

Ridge, lych-gate, crescent camp, moated mound: I could draw all of it, and
the wight-payment mechanic ("Every mound an account. Every account
payable") turns a melee into a legible system. The out-mourning at the
Kingsbarrow is not a fight and correctly refuses to become one. No notes —
except praise for Berrin's wagon entering the killing-ground "at a walk,
like weather," which is choreography doing characterization's job.

### C3. `22-the-feast.md` — the model

Two doors named and *placed* before the first knife ("the great north door
to the yard and the south door by the kitchens"), fire given a direction
(the wind bending flames toward the north door), the fight given a shape in
one sentence ("fire along the center-beam… knives at the signers' benches…
and at either end of the long house the two great doors, which were the
whole battle, because a burning hall is not a trap until it shuts"). Dun's
and Hale's deaths are geographically inevitable a page before they happen.
Do not let anyone tighten it.

### C4. `25-storm-queens-price.md` — one grounding sentence missing

The embassy, the eye, and the vote are all placeable. But when the queen
spends the cellars, the army we watch destroyed has never been placed:

> "down the long slopes, where the host lay camped by companies along the
> wind-shadows of the tier — down where the army was — the loosed gales
> fell upon the rising like hawks into a poultry-yard."

That's the first we hear of where the host is relative to the crag. One
sentence during the climb up ("below, the host strung its camps down the
lee of the tier, company by company in the wind-shadows") converts the
scattering from spectacle into witnessed geography. The scattering itself —
"like seed… exactly like a sower flinging seed" — is the right image and
survives at country-scale.

### C5. `32-siege-of-outworks.md` — the stated plan and the executed battle disagree

The plan is briefed as three steps: "Get the work-gangs clear. Fire the
gas. Drop the King's first wall into the King's own hole." What happens:
*Unrivet* strips the rampart's face, the collar-breakers assault *into* the
breach, Rivetgrim counterattacks, and only then is the trench cleared and
the gas fired. The breach-assault — the whole middle of the chapter — is
not in the plan the reader was given, so it reads as either improvisation
or error, and the text never says which. One line in the briefing ("first
the seams come off and the breakers go in, so the wall's garrison dies
freed instead of buried — *then* the trench does its work") makes the
battle the executed plan it clearly wants to be. Otherwise legible:
sap-head, Furnace-Trench, breach, and the count running through it.

### C6. `35-heralds-gate.md` — two seams in an otherwise superbly drawn Pelennor

The soldiers' place-names (the Anvil, the Bell-Road, the Alley) are exactly
how you make a shire-sized field walkable, and the bell's ring — "a ring of
stillness widening… every rank inside it seated and every rank beyond it
still charging" — is the best-choreographed magical effect in the draft.
Two seams:

1. **The general vanishes.** Ede's last action is at dawn ("went to her
   place under the hundred banners"); she is next seen receiving the
   Marshal's sword in late afternoon. In the book's Pelennor, the commander
   has no on-page beat between the first charge and the surrender. One
   paragraph at the Anvil hinge — Ede reading the line, refusing to commit
   the reserve, *choosing* to let the bell's timetable spend her people —
   would also sharpen the chapter's bitter arithmetic, which is currently
   carried by Vega alone.
2. **Scale seam.** "the yards themselves ran beyond any seeing — wall to
   far wall a hard day's march" (correct per Scale canon) sits against "the
   shield-line bowed across the yards like a drawn bow." A single line
   cannot span a shire. Localize it: the battle fills the gatehouse
   approach, one corner of a country-sized square — which also explains how
   the choir can walk the Bell-Road from mid-morning to noon and arrive.

### C7. `40-the-throne.md` — brief by design, and it works

"Forty heartbeats," the Last Field and the Steps named by the vanguard, the
shield-arc planted, one moving knot walking up the middle. The refusal of a
long final battle is the right choreography for a book whose weapon is the
bell, and the banner-knock descending a hundred floors is blocking, not
spectacle — the reader knows exactly where every one of ten thousand people
is standing. My only note is the Warden's (host size), not mine.

**Do not touch:** the coursing and the walk toward the horns
(`28-the-coursing.md`). The three-night pursuit is the most spatially
disciplined action writing in the draft — hounds that pace instead of
closing, the horn always "one glade behind," the foxglove drift pulling Ede
downhill mid-paragraph — and the payoff (the pale line parting "like water
around a post") is staged so the geometry itself delivers the theme.

handful of genuine defects, and iteration 1 spends its whole budget on them:
continuity and keystone repairs first, because a masterpiece that contradicts
itself is not one. The structural notes (13's POV run, 40a's trials, 35's

refused — to iteration 2, where structural surgery is still permitted.

Ranked, the notes that serve our reader (accepted this round — see

3. K6 paid in 38a but never planted in Cindermaw's mouth in 19.
4. Dialogue punctuation switches conventions twice mid-book.
5. Berrin's stated ages cannot reach the Theft.
6. Century-leak: tower-era spans still reading in centuries against canon.
7. Warcamp distances contradict the five-year canon.
