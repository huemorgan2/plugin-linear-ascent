## Critic 1 — The Genre Faithful

**Verdict: LOVED.**

The re-grounded world is a straight upgrade for our reader. The tower as a ladder of paper-thin countries is the most original piece of megastructure fantasy I've read since the genre started borrowing from engineering textbooks, and the book now *cashes* it: Nix coming out of the Root Gate and failing to find "the tower" because the tower is the sky (`19a-what-roothollow-sends.md`) is an awe-shot worthy of the first sight of Minas Tirith, and the war-read-as-color — grey rungs going gold — gives the whole epic a second, silent narrator. And liberation has a face now: floor after floor, killing the Warden buys the sky back, and the freed *stare up*. That is what the veil canon was for, and it works.

1. **The book plants a star and never pays it.** `08a-first-banner.md`: Nix — born under a lid — asks "What's a star?", and Dun answers, "The ground keeps thousands; you'll see them when we've done the work — that's in the terms, far as I'm concerned." That is a promise, made over the book's holiest object, to the book's most beloved character. Then the summit (`40-the-throne.md`) is "Black sky, starless," and the promise dies unpaid. Nix never sees his star on-page. Give the summit its true sky (see critic 4's canon case — the fix is one word and one beat) and let the goblin count stars. This is the cheapest great moment remaining in the manuscript. **MUST-FIX.**
2. **Gnarl's banners never fly.** `04-king-of-nothing.md`: "In the dead air they did not fly" — a hundred stolen banners hanging limp is the chapter's ruling image, and Gnarl's death should break the dead air. It doesn't; the field stays windless and grey to the last line. One paragraph — the gloom letting go, real evening wind coming in off the rim, a meadow of dead colors lifting all together over the field where the muster finishes itself — pays the image AND the missing veil-lift in one stroke. **MUST-FIX** (jointly with critic 4).
3. **The grey rung deserves its curtain call.** `19a-what-roothollow-sends.md` gives us "one grey rung held among them, where a queen kept her own dark by contract" — a chill and wonderful detail — and then `41-epilogue.md` paints the ladder "gold, the whole visible height of it" and forgets her. Whichever way canon rules (critic 4 has the docket), the epilogue is poorer without one sentence about the fortieth rung. A tower all gold but one, and Roothollow telling strangers why, is a better final image than uniform gold. **SHOULD.**
4. **The Years is the best chapter in the book and nobody prepared me to expect it.** No note. Entered for the record so nobody trims it by accident.
5. **The great scenes are great.** The out-mourned king, the feast with its two doors, the walk to the horns, the bell, the knock descending a hundred floors, "You've heard them." I would press this book on a friend and stand there while they read chapter 26. The insertions did not dilute one of these; several (the Kindling touching Ede's face before she walks at the horns, `28-the-coursing.md`) improved them.

**Do not touch:** `38a-stolen-years.md` — the garths, the bees, the barley mowed by its own last light. It is the reason the ending lands, because it makes the world worth the walk down.

## Critic 2 — The Literary Critic

**Verdict: LIKED, a hand's width from LOVED.**

The question this round: do the ~40 plan-011 insertions read like the book or like patches? Verdict: roughly thirty read native, and the best read better than native. Exhibits for the defense, quoted because credit must be as precise as blame: "the grey came off the sky through that afternoon, scaling away like rust under a wire brush" and "the gloom drained out of the air the way bilge goes out of a pumped hull, and by evening the wreck-fields stood in level amber light... a fleet's worth of shadows, all sailing east together" (`24-anchor-chains.md` — floor-grown, twice, in one chapter); "The edge above the Pale Court ran in one clean unbroken curve... geometry, perfect as a coin... No people had ever drawn that line" (`32-siege-of-outworks.md` — the Made-are-round beat delivered in Nix's own arithmetic); the whole ladder view of `19a`. Where the insertions fail, they fail the same way, which makes the repair cheap:

1. **The "untended" heptad.** Seven veil-lifts share one syntactic skeleton: "the floor's gloom, untended now, was coming apart the way it came apart on every freed floor" (`11-banner-war.md`); "the grey overhead loosened and came apart, untended" (`12-out-mourned-king.md`); "untended gloom going the way it went on every freed floor" (`13-silk-terms.md`); "came apart in a day, untended" AND "it unravelled, untended" (`17-frozen-sea.md` — twice in one chapter); "the veil went — untended, the grey false day thinning and tearing the way the freed had seen it go above every floor whose Warden they had broken" (`33-the-welded.md`); "the ninetieth's veil going, untended" (`36-malgrim.md`). Plus `06-on-the-yoke.md`. This is the patch showing. Note that the strong veil-lifts (`20`, `21`, `24`, `27`, `31`) never use the word at all — they let the floor supply the image. Remedy: keep "untended" twice at most (12 and 33 earn it); rewrite 11, 13, and both of 17's from their floors' own stock (17 already owns "the iron sky went the way the shell had gone" — there the fix is deleting one word). **MUST-FIX** as a bundle.
2. **The motif explains itself three times.** "The way it came apart on every freed floor" / "going the way it went on every freed floor" / "the way the freed had seen it go above every floor" (`11`, `13`, `33`). Once is orientation; thrice is the author elbowing the reader. Keep one. **SHOULD.**
3. **The two-doors lecture is delivered four times.** `05-ledger-of-gates.md` ("The tower had set its two doors a country apart, so there was no such thing as passing a floor. You crossed it, or you stayed."), `11-banner-war.md` ("Every floor had two doors — the arch you came up out of at one rim, the stair you left by at another..."), `20-long-stair.md` (Nix: "the whole Ascent had been laid out by somebody paid by the mile"), `24-anchor-chains.md` (a clause). Keep 05 (first, load-bearing) and 20 (the joke); reduce 11's to a subordinate clause — it arrives six files after 05 taught the same lesson in nearly the same grammar. **SHOULD.**
4. **Canon-doc diction leaking into narration.** `14-hostage.md`: "the wind that squeezed howling through the slot between the floors and died ten steps inland" — that is `world-construction.md`'s own sentence ("Ten steps inland from any lip, the Shear dies to a whisper") wearing a thin coat. It's concrete and it works, but a reader of both will hear the seam; loosen the phrasing. **NICE.**
5. **"Still warm" sentimentality overriding the world.** `32-siege-of-outworks.md`: "the loaves still warm from Berrin Or's own ovens at the bottom of the world" — eighty floors below, in a book whose own arithmetic says a sack of barley "spent a season getting to the war" (`19a`). The warmth is doing emotional work the physics can't carry, and it dulls the dramatic irony (Berrin is standing in the camp). Cut "still warm" or re-source the loaves to a gate-town oven he taught. **SHOULD** (shared with critic 4).
6. **A new tic is seeding.** "An age and a half" appears at `36-malgrim.md` ("the muster-drums... had beaten for an age and a half") and `38-scram.md` ("the first nothing in an age and a half"). Twice is a coincidence a copy-editor kills before it becomes a colony. Keep one. **NICE.**
7. **Double attribution.** `37-obsidian-stair.md`: "'There it is,' Ede said..." immediately followed by a new paragraph beginning "'You never wept for Malgrim,' Ede said" — two consecutive said-tags for the same speaker with no beat between. Merge or give the reflection a half-line. **NICE.**

**Do not touch:** Malgrim's ledger voice, interludes and appendix both — "Grief is a bond that matures; every cage she opens, she carries. Let her portage" is the best villain prose in the book, and the near-verbatim doubling between the interludes and `05-malgrims-ledger.md` reads as the recovered-complete conceit, not as padding.

## Critic 3 — The Structural Editor

**Verdict: LIKED.**

Structurally the plan-011 work was performed with a scalpel: I diffed against `backups/book-backup003/` expectations and found no chapter whose architecture the insertions altered — the enrichments sit in aftermaths, keeper speeches, and transit paragraphs, exactly where a book absorbs new matter without changing its gait. Three pattern-level notes and two ledger items:

1. **The veil-lift is colonizing the coda slot.** Of the twelve full veil-lift beats, nine sit in the final page of their chapter, four as the literal closing image (`03`, `11`, `12`, `21`). The beat itself is right — liberation should be visible — but the placement is becoming a stanza the reader can predict. `33-the-welded.md` models the alternative (the veil goes mid-scene, "then the word was said again, and the iron went on opening, in daylight" — the work continuing *inside* the new light). When the prose fixes from critic 2 are made to 11/13/17, move at least one of those beats off the final paragraph. **SHOULD.**
2. **Chapter 35 has quietly broken the battle cap.** The style-guide holds battle chapters to 3,000–3,500 words; `35-heralds-gate.md` stands at ~4,300 after the iteration-2 additions (Serle Danner hinge, Ede's command beat — both earned). Nothing in it reads slack; the Anvil/Bell-Road/Alley geography and the ring-of-stillness set-piece are the best-organized battle in the book. Recommendation: amend the style-guide's cap for this one chapter rather than cut — but make it a decision on the record, not a drift. **SHOULD** (paperwork, not prose).
3. **The letter-chapters carry the new canon best.** It is worth noting for the final-edition pass that the three biggest world-insertions (ladder view, bead-town, knot-garden in `19a`; the shelf-crossing in `38a`; the healing reports in `40a`) all landed in the "breather" chapters, and all three breathers are now stronger than some mainline chapters. The rhythm of battle → breather is doing exactly what it was built for. No action; entered as a defense against future trimming.
4. **The Vyx-rung repair has a structural home.** Whatever canon ruling critic 4 wins, the epilogue's Broodthrone visit (`41-epilogue.md`) is the natural single point of repair — the scene already exists, the queen is on-page, and one sentence there ripples backward through `38` and `06-on-the-yoke.md` with two matching clauses. Do not solve this with a new scene. **Advisory.**
5. **Header/file skew.** File numbers and chapter headers have diverged by up to five (`09-the-quiet.md` is "Chapter 10"; `32-siege-of-outworks.md` is "Chapter 38"). Harmless in the working draft, but the final edition cut will need a renumbering pass, and every panel citation so far uses file names. Flag for the final-edition checklist, not for this round. **NICE.**

**Do not touch:** the descent structure of the epilogue — floor by floor, debt by debt, ending at the trough. The temptation at final-edition time will be to compress the descent "because the war is over." The descent is the argument.

## Critic 5 — The First-Time Reader

**Verdict: LOVED.**

I came to this book knowing nothing, and the re-grounded world is the most learnable epic geography I've encountered: every rule is taught by a keeper with dirt on their hands, exactly when I need it, exactly once — the lens (`03`'s "tilting gently away... down and inland"), the lid (`02`'s "the day was paint on a lid"), the two doors (`05`), the Kindling (`09`, from Pol, in grief), the ladder itself (`19a`, through Nix's eyes, at the perfect moment — late enough that I'd earned it, early enough to reframe the whole back half). For the record, against future trimming: the "repetitions" critic 2 flags in items 2–3 never *confused* me — they carried me — so cut with care; it's the samey phrasing, not the reminding, that shows.

1. **Where I was lost: the two silk Wends.** Wend Silkstead keeps the Broodwarrens (`09`, `10`); Wend Threadneedle keeps the Broodthrone (`13`) — two keepers named Wend, on the book's two spider floors, four files apart. I conflated them for a full chapter and had to page back. I know the panel has ruled the keeper-name pool deliberate and closed the general docket; I re-raise only this single worst pair, because the floors rhyme as hard as the names. Surname on every re-entry at minimum; one rename ideally. **SHOULD.**
2. **Where I stumbled: the peoples count.** "Peoples: nine" is a set-piece I cried at (`16`). When `31` said "thirty-one peoples" I flipped back believing I'd misread the census. One half-sentence acknowledging the recount (see critic 4, item 15a) turns a stumble into a payoff — the census growing is the *point*. **SHOULD.**
3. **The question the book taught me to ask and didn't answer: the grey rung.** `19a` made me love that one rung stayed grey by contract — it was the moment I understood this book keeps its rules even against its own big finish. So at the epilogue's "gold, the whole visible height of it," I did exactly what the book trained me to do: I asked *what happened to hers?* — and got silence. Same fix as critics 1 and 4; I'm the witness that a newcomer notices. **MUST-FIX** (by adoption).
4. **Two words nearly collided: Kindle and the Kindling.** `17` teaches me "Kindle, the warmth that a body could wear like a coat"; the rim-gold event (named the Kindling in the lore, though the chapters wisely say "the rim-gold" / "the gold") arrives in `09` and `28`. As printed the chapters never actually collide the terms — good — but if any future edit inserts the word "Kindling" into a chapter, know that a first-time reader will reach for the warmth-charm. Guard, don't change. **NICE.**
5. **What I needed one sentence earlier: nothing fatal — one small thing.** In `28`, "the Hunt kept its own ways between its floors" made me briefly believe I'd misunderstood the whole one-stair-per-gap rule the book had spent fifty floors teaching me (I'd been *proud* of knowing it). Critic 4's one-sentence fence would have saved me the wobble. **SHOULD** (by adoption).
6. **What made me look up from the page, in the good way:** Hobb's "Every floor up there is owed an evening, captain. Go collect" (`03`); the ladder (`19a`); "nobody musters alone" (`27`); the mowing (`38a`); the knock (`40`); the fence-post. I was never bored, and I never skimmed — the density of names the charter protects is a feature for a newcomer too, because the book always pays the freight of a name before it spends it.
