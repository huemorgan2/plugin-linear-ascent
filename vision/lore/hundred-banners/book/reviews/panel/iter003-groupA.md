## PART A — Self-audit of iteration 2's surgeries (cold eyes)

We diffed every audited chapter against `backups/book-backup002/` and
then re-read each in full context. Rolling back a bad change is a
victory; we went looking for one and did not find it. Five verdicts:

### 1. The Garet Hode wrong verdict — `40a-the-trials.md` — KEEP

It seams cleanly and it serves the chapter; it does not steal the
climax. The case is 27 lines, third in a sequence that was building a
graduated scale (Annis Cray struck to the wronged; Corven Dale's
bettered sentence), and Hode is the missing rung: the court reads a
frightened informer as willing, and Ede — who "had weighed him while
they were out... and the beam had come down otherwise" — seals it
anyway. The application note's key sentence survived intact and is
the best line in the chapter: "a court she could overrule would only
have been her own arm in a longer sleeve." The cost lands where it
should, on the sealing hand — "She pressed the seal down, slow, and
her hand did not shake" pays the steadiness motif planted in
`30-what-ede-carries.md` ("the steadiness, worn like a shield on the
wrong arm"). And crucially the chapter's true close — the under-herald
reading the first petition to the waiting-folk — is untouched and
still lands last. The Annis Cray trim (a third, per the diff) reads
tighter, not thinner; the jury's answer as free indirect speech
("the crime was the famine, and the famine had an owner, and the
owner was dead") is better than the old digger's speech it replaced.
Verdict: KEEP, unmodified.

### 2. The Serle Danner hinge + Ede command paragraph — `35-heralds-gate.md` — KEEP

Does it slow the bell? No — and we timed it. The Danner beat (25
lines) sits in the pre-dawn walk-the-line movement, before the drums
quicken; the bell's tide (choir walk, noon brake, the swing) is
untouched from "It began with steel" onward. The hinge pays the `08`
arc at exact term: "I stood at a fire once and told her she sold us.
Today I hold her hinge" — the man who asked the book's first hard
question now holds the ground the answer stands on. The craft detail
(shields "lapped like hedge-stakes... hedge-fighter's craft, learned
in the lanes at the bottom of the world") stitches floor one to floor
ninety in one image, and "Vega left him her second-best maul, which
between smiths and serjeants is a whole speech" is the right exit.
The Ede command paragraph fixes a real defect — the general had gone
inaudible in her own greatest battle — and does it in muster-yard
diction that carries her voice without stealing Vega's POV ("you are
a wall, and walls do not run"). The localization clause ("one stitch
of iron in a square that ran a day's march") repairs the scale canon
without a lecture. This is the round's best surgery. Verdict: KEEP.

### 3. The live drum-tower beat — `07-red-warcamp.md` — KEEP

The old text gave Nix's finest hour entirely to the histories
("Later she pieced it together..."). The new text gives Ede six lines
of live witness from the Paylane — "a small shape going up its lashed
frame quick as a rat up rigging... that tower's drums fell out of the
storm, and the storm faltered around the hole they left" — and then
hands off to the retained frame ("The how of it she pieced together
later"). The seam between live and retrospective is honest: she saw
the what, learned the how. The hearth-wall image ("one picture
outlived the rest") still closes the movement. Verdict: KEEP — with
one adjacent fresh note (see Bored Teenager, note 3): the
*pre-existing* forward-telling two sentences later ("would be
assembling the strength to speak it at scale for thirty floors to
come") now reads even more like a brochure inside a battle, because
the live beat raised the passage's pulse around it. That is a new
trim, not a rollback.

### 4. The floor-catalog cuts — `13-silk-terms.md` and `24-anchor-chains.md` — KEEP

We hunted for scar tissue and found none. In `13`, the eight-floor
catalog is now three kept floors (Drifts, Broken Doors, Peat Cuts)
and one graceful discharge — "The rest the histories may keep: the
Troll Delvings' three whispered days, the Rig Graves' rusting meres,
the Vent Country's whistling crust, the Processional Way's
passing-bells" — which preserves every name a later chapter leans on
(the Echo Glades still host Rhovan's last dispatch). No orphaned
keepers: Sela Steamgate, Hune Delverest, and Weld Rustwake appear
nowhere downstream. In `24`, the halved first tour and the
bead-string second ("The bridge floors went by like beads told on a
string, and every bead was a country") keep exactly the clauses that
pay later — Kennel Courts (the freed hounds at `26`/`28`/`36`), Silk
Vaults (the Welding Halls' "sold on" logic), and the Lightning
Fields' storm-copper, which the navy paragraph spends four lines
later. Transitions read grown, not stitched. Verdict: KEEP, both.

### 5. The Rhovan interior plant — `12a-toll-burning.md` — KEEP

One sentence, and it carries the whole downstream payload: "forty
years the Wake had bought his eyes and never once trusted his word,
and this woman had just handed him both, across a plain table, with
a crown in sacking between them — and he filed the feeling, as he
filed everything dangerous, under a name he declined to read."
When `37-obsidian-stair.md` arrives at "I blackmailed Ede Stonhal at
Roothollow on the first morning and loved her before the
Broodthrone," the root is now on-page, in the right chapter (the
Broodthrone is two files later), in Rhovan's own filing-cabinet
idiom. It neither names the feeling nor sentimentalizes it, which is
the only way this elf could carry it. Verdict: KEEP.

**Audit summary: five KEEPs, zero rollbacks, one adjacent trim
promoted to the fresh-finds list.** The panel notes for the record
that iteration 2's application discipline (grepping cut names
forward, checking the held-breath count — still exactly two, `12` and
`38a`) is why there is no scar tissue to find. We also swept for
collateral damage from the change-15 pronoun fix in `37` and found a
seam — logged below as fresh find, because the double tag it left is
new defect, not old.

## Critic 1 — The Genre Faithful

**Verdict: LOVED.**

I have read this book twice now across two iterations and I would
press it on a friend tonight, with the standard warning that the
feast chapter will do to them what it did to me. The awe is real
(the prologue's rising valley; the Frozen Sea; the Years), the
fellowship is earned in ink (Dun's muster-roll; Nix's census-line;
"nobody musters alone"), and the payoffs land planted: the crooked
star sewn at `08a` is read by its own sewer at `39`; the fair-
children's skipping rhyme at `09` becomes the walk to the horns at
`28`; the clerk's heel of bread in the prologue becomes the trial's
answer at `36`. That is the genre's whole covenant, kept.

Notes:

1. **The Storm Queen's treaty is sworn off-page.** `35-heralds-gate.md`
   has "the Storm Queen's gales, promised by embassy and sworn by
   treaty-debt" — but no embassy after the scattering ever appears.
   The plant exists (`25-storm-queens-price.md`: "the Court does not
   refuse custom. When the wind profits, you shall have wind," and
   `26a`'s unclosable entry), so this is a half-step, not a hole:
   give the purchase half a sentence at the muster in
   `31-breaking-the-hunt.md` — envoys down from the crag, wind for
   the gate-battles, priced to the letter — so "treaty-sworn" at `35`
   has a receipt. This book runs on receipts; it should not skip
   this one.
2. **The King never costs the vanguard anything at the summit.** In
   `40-the-throne.md` the forty-heartbeat fight wounds no one we can
   name; Vharuk's menace is all arsenal. One priced beat — Aldis
   turning his guard "an age late" taking something for it — would
   make the last blade-work weigh what everything else in this book
   weighs. I raise it knowing the design argues back ("he had spent
   an age making certain he need never fight the world"); the
   synthesis may refuse me with that reason and I will accept it.
3. **Pib, first of her line** (`10-hales-lesson.md`) gets the best
   naming scene of the low floors and is never seen again, though
   the ninety-one recur collectively for the whole book. One clause
   at Chalk's fire (`16-the-molt.md`) or in Sesk's asking-after
   (`19a`) would close a small warm loop. Low rank; density budget
   may be better spent above.
4. Praise, for the record, so iterations 4–10 protect it: the
   chain of "small folk first" climaxes — Nix at the drum-tower
   (`07`), at the Nursery (`09`), at the bell-gantry (`35`), on the
   bollard calling the hundred (`40`) — is the best-built escalation
   in modern epic fantasy and must not be evened out by any future
   line pass.

**Do not touch:** the epilogue's last paragraph — the banner "leaning
against the horse trough." The whole hundred floors land on that
trough, and it was in chapter 1, skimmed with dust.

## Critic 3 — The Structural Editor

**Verdict: LIKED.**

With continuity noise now low, the architecture reads clean: three
tiers of act (root to Warcamp, Warcamp to feast, feast to throne),
the interludes correctly spaced as the ledger-spine, the mid-book
sag solved by the wintering/feast one-two, and the double climax
(Gate at `35`, throne at `40`) correctly separated by the traitor's
unmasking and the Years. The lettered chapters (`08a`, `12a`, `19a`,
`21a`, `26a`, `31a`, `38a`, `40a`) all justify their insertions.
Two seams remain, both fixable at clause scale — which is exactly
what this round is for.

Notes:

1. **The `27`→`28` horn seam.** `27-the-eye.md` ends with the
   coursing-horns counting "Four notes" through the eye-wall while
   the four sleep in camp and Nix says "Wake the general."
   `28-the-coursing.md` then opens: "The horns took them at moonset,
   on the third night in the eye... they were four on the ridge
   above the eye-wall when the calm ended," and narrates "the first
   horn" as if unheard before. Same night, two incompatible framings
   — the cliffhanger's horns have vanished. When the book restarts a
   timeline elsewhere it signposts ("a count is read from its first
   mark, so the tale walks back here," `16-the-molt.md`); `28` needs
   either that signpost or, better, one bridging clause: the horns
   had counted four into the eye at middle watch; by moonset the
   four were on the ridge because the road had to be walked before
   the army walked it. One sentence. Top of my list.
2. **Double dialogue tag in `37-obsidian-stair.md`** — likely scar
   tissue from change 15's pronoun repair. Two consecutive
   paragraphs, no interruption: '"There it is," Ede said. "That's
   the flaw in the casting." / "You never wept for Malgrim," Ede
   said.' Merge the paragraphs or retag the second; as printed it
   reads like a dropped reflection line.
3. **`23-reckoning-of-names.md` opens abstract** ("truth keeps to
   pantries, stair-feet, the space between a question and its
   answer") directly after the feast's fire. I flag it and withdraw
   it in the same breath: after re-reading in sequence, the cool
   opening is the survivors' pulse-rate, and the annex-ash scene
   arrives within a page. No change.
4. **`40a` as penultimate chapter** was a risk (a court docket
   before the epilogue) and it clears: the docket modulates through
   Aldis's "this is the craft that doesn't come back on its own"
   into the petition-reading, which is the correct threshold to the
   descent. Keep the order; do not let any later iteration swap
   `40a` and `41`'s weighting.

**Do not touch:** the interlude spine — five ledger entries, each
under 450 words, each placed at a tier boundary. It is the book's
metronome and its dramatic irony engine, and its restraint (Malgrim
never once appears on-page between the prologue and `36`) is why
`36` lands.

## Critic 7 — The Battle Choreographer

**Verdict: LIKED.**

I can draw every major set-piece from the text, which was not true
two iterations ago. The Warcamp's four-point geometry ("three doors
and a thread") is teachable. The feast reads as chaos with a spine —
fire at the center-beam, knives at the signers' benches, two doors —
and both door-deaths are staged on ground the reader already holds.
The new localization clause at `35` ("one stitch of iron in a square
that ran a day's march") finally squares the day's-march yards with
a holdable line, and Ede's command paragraph gives the battle its
missing chain of command. Two legibility gaps remain, both one
clause wide.

Notes:

1. **Nix teleports to the bell-gantry.** `35-heralds-gate.md`: at
   mid-morning he is deep in the enemy mass — "She could not see
   Nix. Nobody could see Nix; that had always been the point of
   Nix" — and at noon he is "riding the gantry with a hammer-cord in
   both fists" with no road between. The square's north face opens
   when the carriage brakes; give him one clause coming back down
   the Bell-Road through it (a mole's run in reverse) and the
   floor's whole geometry closes. As printed, the one soldier whose
   position the chapter taught us to track by inference arrives by
   authorial hand.
2. **The smoke-hole gallery at the feast is never resolved.**
   `22-the-feast.md`: "the first bowstring spoke" from the gallery,
   Rhovan is "on a table with his bow, loosing up into the gallery"
   — and the thread ends there; the fire and the doors take the
   narrative and the dozen bows above the hall simply stop existing.
   One clause — the gallery going quiet under his shafts, or a
   Herald falling among the banners — retires the overhead threat
   the scene itself armed. Optional; the chaos-from-Ede's-POV
   defense is real, but she is reading the fight's shape for us in
   that very paragraph, so the shape should close.
3. Confirmations for the record: the change-15 host-placement
   sentence in `25` ("camps strung down the lee of the tier by
   companies") makes the scattering fully legible — we know where
   the army is before the sky takes it. And the briefed breach in
   `32` ("the plan — Vega's plan, and Dara's... Speak the rampart's
   own seams open... and send the collar-breakers sweeping through
   it first") reads as doctrine now, not improvisation. Both hold.

**Do not touch:** the two lintels at `41-epilogue.md` — "DUN HARROW
HELD HERE" / "HALE HELD HERE." That is battlefield geometry turned
into grief-architecture, and it is the payoff of the most legible
fight in the book.

## Critic 9 — The Bored Teenager

**Verdict: LIKED.**

Full disclosure: I came in ready to skim and the back half didn't
let me. Files `32` through `38a` — outworks, chain yards, the
Welding Halls, the unmasking, the bell, the mirror fights, Malgrim,
the Years — is the best two hundred pages I've read in this genre,
and I include the published competition. I looked up from the page
(good) at the horn counting *four*, at "Open your shirt," at Nix
dropping the cup on purpose, and at "You've heard them." I looked at
my phone zero times after the feast. Before the feast, twice, and I
know exactly where, and one of them got adjudicated last round and
I'm not re-raising it (the way-loft ledger stuff in `19a`; the
seed-corn vote pays it off; fine; I said fine).

Notes:

1. **Cut the brochure sentence in the Warcamp battle.**
   `07-red-warcamp.md`, right after the drum-tower beat that now
   actually goes hard: "It was learned. The rising owned it, and
   would be assembling the strength to speak it at scale for thirty
   floors to come." Sir. The drums are literally still falling
   silent. Nobody wants the sequel pitch mid-battle. Cut from "It
   was learned" through "floors to come," keep "The drums stopped."
   The book already proves the at-scale thing — at the bell, where
   it's the whole climax. Trust it.
2. **The giants-finish-their-sentences bit is a great joke told
   sixteen times.** I counted (Nix taught me): sixteen-ish
   instances. It's perfect the first five times — Brom building "the
   sentence first in his head, stone by stone" (`02`) is genuinely
   great — but by `18-brother-and-sister.md` line "finished his
   sentence, because he always finished his sentences," the narration
   is doing the joke about itself, and by `28` ("finishing the
   thought the way giants do") I groaned out loud. Keep the front
   half's instances and the two that are load-bearing (`38`'s "the
   only sentence Vega ever heard her brother leave unfinished" NEEDS
   the tic established — that's the best use of it in the book).
   Thin three or four of the middle repetitions. Line-level, cheap,
   makes the `38` payoff hit harder.
3. **Don't add anything back.** I read the backup of `13` out of
   audit duty and the old eight-floor catalog was where I'd have
   checked my phone. The cut version I read without noticing a cut,
   which is the whole test. Whoever's running iteration 4: the
   correct amount of new floor-tour material is zero.

**Do not touch:** Nix. All of Nix, but specifically "counting was
how he wept" and every payoff of it (`04`, `07`, `27`, `40` — "Nix's
voice broke clean in half calling the hundred"). If any future
critic suggests Nix is comic relief and needs trimming, throw them
off the panel.

## Critic 10 — The Acquiring Editor

**Verdict: LOVED.**

I did the assignment twice: the first 30 pages fresh, then the last
30 fresh, then checked whether the ending is the thing the opening
sells. It is — to a degree I'd put in the auction letter.

Notes:

1. **The first 30 pages buy the book.** The prologue is a complete,
   devastating short story (a valley stolen mid-bake; "The tower
   feeds what it counts"; "Nothing is what everything costs, at
   first") that plants the villain's creed, the bread motif, and —
   we only see it on the re-read — the traitor himself: "The clerk
   watched him eat with an expression he would spend a lifetime
   learning to make himself — patient, kindly, keeping count."
   Chapter 1 then delivers the hook mechanism (the cut ledger-page)
   inside twelve pages and the thesis line inside twenty ("Who sold
   us... Not a question. A requirement."). Title-to-hook time is
   excellent. Pitch sentence, for the record: *The Count of Monte
   Cristo staged as a hundred-floor siege — a disgraced captain
   climbs a tower of stolen countries to find the name that sold
   her company, and accidentally builds a nation on the way up.*
   Comps: The Wall's atmosphere, Martin's ledger-cold cruelty,
   Pratchett's small-folk heart in the Nix line.
2. **The last 30 pages land the opening's exact promises.** Cold
   loaves in a dead oven (prologue) → "the smell of bread that meant
   only bread now" (epilogue). The trainer of other people's
   recruits at a dusty trough (ch. 1) → the same woman at the same
   trough, trade made honest, "she would climb with them now...
   every time." The clerk's heel of bread (prologue) → Berrin's
   trial answer, "word for word as it had been said to him over a
   heel of bread when he was seven" (`36`). The ring closes without
   a single authorial nudge. This is what "planted and paid" looks
   like at acquisition grade.
3. **The epilogue is the right length.** 2,945 words against a
   2,500–3,000 spec, and every station pays a standing debt: Vyx's
   story-clause becomes the book's own frame ("told her the book —
   this one, all of it"); Voss's copper pays the toll-chain of `05`;
   Hobb's "The counts match" pays the first keeper's first line;
   Berrin's silent delivery pays the sentence. Nothing to cut,
   nothing to add. Specifically protect the farm girl's final beat
   — the banner set down "in the one place in the square where a
   thing may lean and not fall" — from any well-meaning iteration-7
   line pass; its plainness is the point.
4. **Commercially brave, and keep it:** the beloved-traitor reveal
   with no cackle (Berrin's warmth was never a mask — the Malgrim
   interludes say so in advance, "nothing in it is false but the
   sum"); justice-as-labor instead of a gallows; killing Calyne in
   the exact second of her vindication. **Commercially exposed, and
   manage it:** the density of names is a feature per charter, but
   the final edition should ship the existing appendices (dramatis
   personae, gazetteer) up front where the trade paperback reader
   can find them. That is a production note, not an edit.
5. One structural confirmation the market will care about: the
   book's two biggest chapters (`22`, `35`) both end on forward
   motion rather than exhaustion ("Bring the thirty. I have
   something of theirs to return"). Series-agnostic but
   sequel-capable. Do not close those doors in later polish.

**Do not touch:** the prologue. Not one word. It is the best twenty
minutes a browser will spend standing in a bookshop this decade, and
it is also, invisibly, the solution to the mystery — which is the
kind of thing that sells the re-read, which is the kind of thing
that sells the hardcover.

## Closing synthesis — ranked recommendations (panel budget: 6 of 5–12)

The audit produced zero rollbacks and zero repairs. The whole budget
goes to fresh finds, ranked:

1. **Bridge the `27`→`28` horn seam** (Structural #1). One clause at
   the top of `28-the-coursing.md` acknowledging the eye's counted
   horns and the decision to go forward. The book's only true
   continuity seam left at chapter scale.
2. **Fix the double "Ede said"** in `37-obsidian-stair.md` (Structural
   #2). Merge or retag; two lines; likely iteration-2 scar tissue —
   catching it is this audit's one repair.
3. **Return Nix to the gantry on-page** in `35-heralds-gate.md`
   (Choreographer #1). One clause down the Bell-Road when the
   square's north face opens.
4. **Root the Storm Queen's treaty** (Genre Faithful #1). Half a
   sentence at the muster in `31-breaking-the-hunt.md`: the Court's
   custom bought, priced to the letter — so `35`'s "treaty-sworn"
   has its receipt on-page.
5. **Cut the "at scale for thirty floors to come" forward-telling**
   in `07-red-warcamp.md` (Teenager #1). One sentence; keeps the
   drum-silence beat clean; the bell proves the point where it
   matters.
6. **Thin the giants-finish-sentences tic** (Teenager #2). Cut or
   vary three to four of the ~16 instances in the book's middle
   (candidates: `18`'s self-narrating instance, `28`, one of `12`'s
   two), preserving `01`/`02` (establishment) and `38` (the payoff
   that requires the tic).

Refused, with reasons:

- **A priced wound at the throne** (`40`, Genre Faithful #2):
  refused. The summit's argument is that Vharuk built the tower so
  he would never have to fight the world in person; a costly duel
  would re-litigate the book's thesis at the moment of its proof.
  The cost was correctly paid at the Gate.
- **The feast gallery clause** (`22`, Choreographer #2): deferred to
  the editor's discretion beneath the budget line. Defensible both
  ways; if taken, it is one clause and must not slow the run to the
  north door.
- **A Pib echo** (Genre Faithful #3): refused for budget. The
  ninety-one recur collectively; the loop is warm but not open.
