## Critic 2 — The Literary Critic

**Verdict: LIKED.**

Iteration 1's line surgery was performed with a real ear. The
dedup­lication of "held breath let go" produced replacements better than
the original tic — `04-king-of-nothing.md`'s stair-lift now splits "with
a sound like a stopper drawn from a year-sealed jug," which is both
fresh and *Gnarl's* (a drinking image at a drunkard-king's death is
writing, not patching); `09-the-quiet.md`'s fever lifts "like heat off
a griddle" on a fairground floor; `20-long-stair.md`'s plain got "quiet
as an unstruck drum" on the floor where drumming kills. The dialogue
normalization is total — I grepped every chapter for Continental
dashes and single-quote speech and found none. And "a battle late"
(`35-heralds-gate.md`) removes the book's only authorial wink at the
price of nothing. What keeps the verdict at LIKED is that iteration 1's
ledger silently dropped four of my notes — neither accepted nor refused,
which the charter forbids ("a refusal without a reason is a blind
spot") — and the dropped tics are all still firing.

### Notes

**2.1 — One "held breath" survived the sweep, at the exact beat the
sweep existed for.** `07-red-warcamp.md`: "the seam of the stair-lift
split behind the shield-wall with a sound like a held breath let go" —
this was one of the two *verbatim* stair-lift twins my original note
led with. Its twin in `04` was rewritten; this one was missed (it
wraps across two lines, which is probably how it escaped the knife).
Fix: replace with a scene-grown image — the Warcamp is drums, smoke,
and cages; the floor's own register offers plenty ("like a drum-skin
splitting," "like a cage-door leaving its hinge").

**2.2 — "The time a lamp takes to gutter" still gutters, six times.**
`12a-toll-burning.md` ("stood very still for the time a lamp takes to
gutter once"), `17-frozen-sea.md`, `25-storm-queens-price.md`,
`27-the-eye.md`, `38-scram.md`, `38a-stolen-years.md`. The keeper is
`38a`, where Nix is literally watching the warrens' last lamp gutter —
that one is now doing double work and must stay. Vary the other five
against the world's own clock-cupboard: a kettle coming to boil, a
ladle of iron cooling, a whetstone's pass. This note was filed in
iteration 1 (my 2.3) and fell through the ledger unanswered.

**2.3 — "Counting was how he wept" still double-fires, and `20` still
runs its pair.** Keep `04-king-of-nothing.md`'s "counting was how he
wept: one" (unimprovable) and its later "counting was how he sang"
(true variation). Cut the verbatim repeat in `07-red-warcamp.md`
("counting the freed out loud because counting was how he wept") — the
scene survives on the image alone. And collapse one of
`20-long-stair.md`'s pair ("counting was how he held the world still
enough to look at" / "counting was how you held a thing still enough
to see what it really was" — 145 lines apart). Dropped note, renewed.

**2.4 — Sliver's calling-card is still dealt from a thick deck.** "I
have carried forty-one climbers" fires in `02`, `03`, `04`, `09`,
`15`, `21`, `36`, and pays at `39-last-door.md` ("She was my fourth").
The uses in `15-calyne.md` ("I have carried forty-one climbers, and I
will tell you the only thing four ages taught me…") and
`21-frosthall-parliament.md` ("I have carried forty-one climbers and
not one of them was asked for that before") are furniture — both
sentences work better without the badge. Cut those two; the payoff
lands on a rested ear. Dropped note, renewed.

**2.5 — Two women share one pronoun in the book's best confession.**
`37-obsidian-stair.md`: the reflection's lure is "*Calyne lives*, where
I am… She *lives,* Rhovan" — and Rhovan's answering whole-truth begins
"I blackmailed her at Roothollow on the first morning and loved her
before the Broodthrone," where "her" is suddenly *Ede*, then swings
back to Calyne inside the same speech ("I grieve her and I am not
sorry"). I know who was blackmailed at Roothollow; a reader at
midnight, primed by three "she lives" beats, will hand the love to the
wrong woman for a paragraph. Fix: "I blackmailed *Ede Stonhal* at
Roothollow on the first morning…" Two words, and the sentence gains
rather than loses — a man telling the entire truth would use the name.

**2.6 — "Mild as milk" still has two owners.** Rhovan in
`02-third-refusal.md` ("'That's one,' he said again, mild as milk"),
Weck in `05-ledger-of-gates.md` ("'The bread pays,' Weck observed, mild
as milk"). A simile that distinctive is a fingerprint. Keep Rhovan's;
give the toll-captain something with coin in it ("comfortable as
rent-day"). Dropped note, renewed — one word of budget.

**2.7 — (Advisory, no edit this round.)** The chapter-final epigram
habit stands ("The counts were about to change"; "The rest was owed,
and morning would come collecting"). Forty-eight burnished last lines
remain a mannerism. Hold for the line-polish iterations; when they
come, let two or three quiet chapters end plain.

**Do not touch:** `00-prologue.md`, still, and now with a second
reason: iteration 1's one edit inside it ("an expression he would spend
a lifetime learning to make") is invisible — the page reads as if it
had always been so. That is what surgery should look like. Not one
further word.

**D11 (Berrin's confession) — ENDORSE change 6 as sufficient on the

**D12 (Nix's collar duration) — RULED: NINE YEARS IS CANON.** The
three passages, read in place:
- `16-the-molt.md` carries the number *structurally*, twice — the scar

- `06-the-adit.md`'s "I wore one twenty years, it's on the floor of

  to amend and the least load-bearing. Fix: "nine years."
- `27-the-eye.md`'s "everything in him that had lived four years under
  a collar" is a bare slip. Fix: "nine years."
Consistency notes: `04`'s "He had held this post six years" is
*compatible* (six of the nine at Gnarl's throne) — leave it. And
`37-obsidian-stair.md`'s "Twenty years of floors and you're still —"
(Nix to his reflection) echoes the wrong number; recommend the
concrete Nix-flavored "Ninety floors and you're still —".

**D13 (`34`'s "a hundred banners") — ENDORSE THE NUMBER; buy it with
half a clause.** "A hundred banners on the poles the King's standards

staged at the completed muster in sight of the gate, and `35` opens on
the same hundred the next dawn. But the ladder's last rung — 70 at the
muster below the Made (`30`/`31`) to 100 at the gate — is never shown
being climbed, and the reader who has been faithfully counting will
pause. One clause in `34` pays it honestly: *the freed floors of the
Made had sewn the last thirty in nineteen days.* The material is
already on page in `31` (the pale court's banner "sewn in three nights
by the trophy-keepers") and `32`–`34` (warrens, yards, chantries
mustering); it only wants saying once with a number.

**D14 (`14`'s champion knight "ninety years gone") — ENDORSE FIX.**
"Taken in the Men's wars, four tiers up, ninety years gone" is tower
grammar ("four tiers up") and therefore tower-era; ninety predates the
Theft by a decade, against hard canon. Rule: "fourscore years gone, in
the tower's first fury" — which also quietly enriches Vyx's floor with
a relic of the first climbs. One phrase.

## Critic 6 — The Character Surgeon

**Verdict: LOVED.**

The brief asked me to re-examine whether Ede, Rhovan, and Vega evolve
*between* beats now. They do, and I can cite the ligaments: Rhovan's
turn runs `26` (the token broken on-page, unplanned, "like stepping

("some things are *made wrong*") → `38` (the hours). Between-beats is
no longer this book's weakness. Two seams remain, both cheap.

### Notes

**S-1 — D10 ruled on: the site is wrong, the need is real.** The claim
to be planted is `37-obsidian-stair.md`'s "loved her *before the
Broodthrone*" — floor forty, the `05`–`13` stretch where the page
record shows only professional fencing. The wintering (`21a`, floor
sixty) is twenty floors too late to plant a floor-forty feeling;
worse, adding an Ede beat to `21a` would dilute that chapter's
deliberate design (it is Vega's ledger of the yard, and Ede's absence
from it is itself characterization — the general has no household).
The correct site was on iteration 1's table all along: `12a-toll-burning.md`,
the watch-house, where Ede slides him the singed bill and says "Then
you'll tell me what you read, and I'll act on it." One sentence of
Rhovan interior — the professional noticing, with surprise, that her
trust has *landed* somewhere it was never supposed to reach — and the
`37` confession collects a debt planted twenty-five floors before the
Broodthrone's silk. Half a line. I will not ask again; I will simply
re-file this every iteration until it exists.

**S-2 — D11 sharpened: give the confession the clerk's own sentence.**
`36-malgrim.md`'s recast holds ("a grown man… knelt for the collar with
his eyes open… not an excuse but an inventory") — chosen-in-daylight is
the book's most original villainy and it is now stated cleanly at
trial. What's missing is the anchor to the bread-lesson: the prologue's
creed is spoken by the clerk — "**The tower feeds what it counts**" —
and the boy "was fed that night" and never wholly unlearned it. Let
Berrin, asked why, answer with the sentence itself: *"The tower feeds
what it counts. I never once found it false."* No name, no night, no
wink — the cold reader hears a proverb; the rereader hears the exact
words said over a heel of bread at the rim of a pit, and the prologue
closes like a trap. One line. (Co-filed with critic 8.)

**S-3 — Wat Kello's banner: one clause would buy the finale's image.**
`33-the-welded.md` establishes the pole "went by turns among the freed
of Shieldwall since Dun Harrow fell"; `39-last-door.md` tells us Wat
"had carried it since the Herald's Gate and let no one spell him." The
moment the turns *stopped* — the freed of Shieldwall giving the banner
to the man the tower made iron — happens off-page. One clause at the
end of `35-heralds-gate.md` (the banners over the graves at dusk: *Wat
Kello had taken the pole that morning, and no one asked for it back*)
and the image that closes the book is earned instead of asserted.
Low rank; spend it only if the budget clears.

**S-4 — Verified, so nobody adds redundant beats:** Ulfar's turn needs
nothing. `38-scram.md`'s "She saw a carried digit fall" at the Fuel
Pens, the oath recited to bedrock, the one unfinished sentence of his
life, and the survey laid on the dead controls "for whoever keeps the
museums of lost futures" — the arc is complete and exactly the right
size. Likewise Ede's refusal of lightness between `22` and `41` is
correct; the epilogue's two counted laughs pay it. Log both as sound;
future panels should treat proposals to "warm Ede up mid-book" as
broadening moves and refuse them.

**S-5 — Protective renewal:** the mercy lesson is stated once by Ede's
acts (`36`) and once in narration (`40a`: "Mercy was not the wage of
the strong. It was the discipline of the tired"). Two is the ceiling.
No third restatement, ever.

**Do not touch:** the scram scene (`38-scram.md`), Ulfar's whole turn —
the argument won by a crew-table and a dead mother's third clause,
"whoever cannot answer, quenches." It is the book's thesis argued in
figures instead of feelings, by the character built to lose any other
way, and it is perfect.

slightly over-glossed, note below. **K3** clean (`21` → `35`'s "not a
tower. A stair"). **K4** clean and unimprovable (`36`: "Yours freed
you. Mine fed me." / "What loves you lets you set it down"). **K5**
NOW PLANTED — `06-the-adit.md`'s half-oath is the best repair of the
iteration: Vega breaks off at "and answer for — " and *does not
finish*, so that when Ulfar finishes it in the lead pulpit
(`38-scram.md`) the reader has been waiting thirty-two chapters for
the third clause, which is the clause the tower dies of. **K6** NOW
PLANTED (`19-wyrms-pity.md`) and paid as memory (`38a`). **K7** clean.
**K8** clean, closed by the trough. Eight for eight. The hunt-rhyme
(`28`: "the old counting-rhyme surfaced in her: walk to the horn and
the horn falls silent") now gives Ede's bravest walk to her own banked
coal, exactly as asked. This book now argues its theme with planted
evidence at every hinge.

### Notes

**8.1 — The shard-line as applied wounds the theme, not just the
fact.** The rising's deepest rule is Dun's: *nobody musters alone* —
sung at the graves in `27`, kept eleven-thousandfold. But
`38-scram.md`'s repair of the census contradiction now reads: "the
census's two hundred and twelve sleep bottled yet, and I cannot hear
them" — which quietly puts two hundred and eleven sleeping kin *in the
camp, at their bearers' throats*, with no one sitting up with them, in
the very hour the book grieves a dying network by name
("Kel-of-the-sixtieth, gone…"). The original panel fix was better and
is still free: the carried shards gutter *and hold*, because chosen
shards burn on their bearers — two hundred splinters flickering at two
hundred throats in one breath — and what dies is the unchosen network.
Sliver's close ("the first, in an age, that burns on nothing but its
own choosing") survives intact. Re-apply; details in the self-audit.

**8.2 — Berrin's why should quote the clerk (D11, thematic case).**
The book's dark first lesson is spoken in the prologue by the enemy —
"The tower feeds what it counts… Nothing is what everything costs, at
first" — and currently pays only through Malgrim's ledger voice. The
mercy engine's rebuttal (`36`, `40`, `41`) is fully in character
