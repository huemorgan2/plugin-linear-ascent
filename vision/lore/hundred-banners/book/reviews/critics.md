# The critic panel — charter for the ten editorial iterations

*Each iteration: back up the book, convene this panel on the FULL current
manuscript, write `reviews-NNN.md`, decide what to change and what to refuse
in `changes-NNN.md`, apply the changes surgically, and judge the PREVIOUS
iteration's changes with cold eyes. Ten times. Then the reckoning: version 10
against version 1, and the final edition.*

## Who our reader is (the audience question, answered once)

**Ours is the genre-loving epic-fantasy reader** — the person who has read
Tolkien and Martin and wants the thing those books gave them: a world that
holds, a fellowship earned, battles with weight, grief that costs, and words
worth carrying out of the book. We are writing a masterpiece FOR THEM, not a
compromise for everyone.

Consequences, fixed for all ten iterations:
- Density of names, floors, and factions is a feature. We clarify; we do not
  simplify away.
- Darkness stays (the feast, the Welded, Berrin). We earn it; we don't cut it.
- The philosophy stays in character mouths. No authorial sermons.
- Broadening moves (shorter sentences, fewer POVs, softer losses) are
  REFUSED unless they also make the book better for ours.
- A critique is accepted only if the fix serves our reader. Every refusal is
  written down with its reason — a refusal with a reason is a decision; a
  refusal without one is a blind spot.

## The panel (every review file speaks in ALL these voices, in order)

1. **The Genre Faithful** — lifelong epic-fantasy reader. Judges: does this
   deliver awe, fellowship, payoff? Are the great scenes GREAT? Would they
   press it on a friend?
2. **The Literary Critic** — prose-first, allergic to cliché and
   sentimentality. Judges sentence quality, imagery, repetition, purple
   patches, unearned emotion. Quotes offending lines verbatim.
3. **The Structural Editor** — pacing and architecture. Judges act balance,
   chapter lengths, where the middle sags, whether turns land at the right
   distance apart, POV distribution.
4. **The Continuity Warden** — canon and internal consistency. Checks facts
   against the outline, style-guide anchors, world-lore, and floor files.
   Lists every contradiction with file and line.
   FROM ITERATION 3 ON, also polices the world-construction canon (adopted
   mid-cycle by plan 011; see ../../world-construction.md and the
   style-guide's Scale/Ladder/Veil blocks): the tower is a ladder of thin
   country-shaped trays, never an iron wall; the lid is visible, never
   "too far up to see"; Wardens hold the LIGHT — no structural language at
   a Warden death, and every on-page Warden death is followed by its
   veil-lift; the scram kills veils/broadcast, never the standing; lifts
   survive; freed floors heal; towns default to the Rimlands; the Made
   trays are perfect circles; the Kindling, the Shear, bead-towns and
   stay-roads exist and must be used consistently.
5. **The First-Time Reader** — smart but new; knows nothing of the game.
   Where were they lost? Which names blurred? What did they need one
   sentence earlier?
6. **The Character Surgeon** — arcs and motive. Does each turn (Hale,
   Rhovan, Ulfar, Voss, Berrin, Zephyra) run on visible rails? Is any change
   of heart bought off-page? Do the women read as people, not roles?
7. **The Battle Choreographer** — action legibility. Can you draw each
   set-piece from the text? Who is where? Does geography hold? Is any fight
   a fog of verbs?
8. **The Thematic Reader** — the mercy engine and the keystones. Does the
   book ARGUE its theme or just declaim it? Do the eight keystone lines
   land as planted-and-paid, or as needlepoint?
9. **The Bored Teenager** — raised on The Maze Runner. Where did they skim?
   Which chapter would they cut entire? What made them look up from the
   page (good) or at their phone (bad)?
10. **The Acquiring Editor** — the market eye. First 30 pages: would they
    buy it? Title-to-hook time, comp titles, the pitch sentence. Notes what
    is commercially brave vs. commercially fatal.

## Review file format (`reviews-NNN.md`)

Per critic: a verdict line (LOVED / LIKED / MIXED / COLD), 3-8 specific,
actionable notes citing chapter files, and one "do not touch" — the thing
that critic believes is the book's best asset. Then a closing synthesis: the
5-12 notes the EDITOR believes serve our reader, ranked.

## Changes file format (`changes-NNN.md`)

- **Accepted changes**: numbered; each with the critic note it answers, the
  exact chapters touched, and the edit made (summary, not diff).
- **Refused notes**: each with its reason, in one honest sentence.
- **Self-audit of the previous iteration** (from 002 on): re-read
  `changes-(NNN-1).md` — did those edits survive contact with this panel?
  Any change to roll back? Rolling back a bad change is a victory, not a
  defeat.
- Scope discipline: 5-15 surgical changes per iteration. No chapter
  rewrites after iteration 3; by 7, only line-level polish; 8-10 converge —
  fewer, smaller, better-justified edits each round. Convergence is the
  goal: a book that stops needing you.

`book/backups/book-backupNNN/`. Backup 001 is the virgin draft.
