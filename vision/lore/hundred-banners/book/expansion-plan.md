# Expansion plan v2 — growing the book without padding it

*Rewritten after the draft completed (~119k words across 42 chapters and
4 interludes ≈ 420–480 typeset pages) and after two corrective passes
changed the ground rules: the originality sweep (no borrowed lines,
scenes, or imagery from famous works, in prose or plates) and the
tower-scale canon (each floor a Scotland-sized country under kilometers
of its own sky; the tower held by aether, not masonry; never seen
whole). The page-count fear is already answered — 119k sits inside the
100–120k epic standard. What follows is NOT page-filling. It is the
mass the book has EARNED but not yet staged: promises the outline made
that currently happen off-page, plus the appendix apparatus that gives
an epic its felt weight. Every addition must pass one test: if the book
were already 600 pages, would we still want this scene? If no, it
doesn't go in.*

## Rule of thumb

Padding stretches what exists; growth stages what was promised. We add
scenes only where a reader could today ask "wait, when did THAT
happen?" — and the honest answer is "between chapters."

## Ground rules carried in from the fixes (bind every addition)

1. **Scale is a gift to these chapters, not a constraint.** New scenes
   happen on country-sized floors with weather, horizons, and marches
   measured in days. The supply-train chapter is a journey, not an
   errand; the memorial garden is crossed over days, not an afternoon;
   sightlines go to horizons, never to a floor's far wall. The tower's
   fabric is iron anchors and aether-seams — never brick.
2. **Originality discipline.** The old plan cited famous-novel "seats"
   (Shadow of the Past, the Red Wedding). Those stay as *cautionary*
   references only: the writer must know them to steer AWAY from their
   distinctive furniture. No borrowed lines, no borrowed scene
   choreography, no named-thing echoes. Same rule for the new plates'
   prompts (strictly medieval, no modern gear, entirely original
   compositions).
3. **Same style guide, same voice.** `style-guide.md` (including the
   Scale hard-canon anchor) governs every new word. Keystone and
   foreshadow lines from `editorial-pass.md` are load-bearing; new
   chapters may echo them but never contradict them.
4. **No new POV characters, no new subplots, no second traitor, no
   resurrections, no extra Warden fights.** Additions face the same
   critics as everything else in the editorial iterations.

## A. New chapters (8) — the off-page promises, staged

File names slot into the existing lexicographic build order. Each gets
a plate (`plate-NNa.png`, prompt added to `plates.tsv`, same graphite
anchor). Target ~2,600 words each.

**A1 · `08a-first-banner.md` — "The Tale of the First Banner"** —
Part I, between ch. 8 and interlude 1. POV Ede. A fireside chapter: Dun
Harrow tells the founding of the Shieldwall — Aldis Bright, the first
climb, why the banner is sewn with a wall and not a sword. Earns: Dun
deepened before his death at the feast; the ch. 39 Champion reveal
converted from surprise to destiny; the Aldis-Bright stone-name hint
given its story. *Caution: history-told-by-firelight is a beloved
shape; keep the telling interrupted, argued with, and paid for — Dun is
a bad storyteller corrected by his listeners, not a sage.*

**A2 · `12a-toll-burning.md` — "The Toll-Burning"** — Part II, after
ch. 12. POV Rhovan. The cast list promises Voss's turn ("enemy of the
purse → quartermaster, after the Heralds burn his tolls at floor 30").
Stage it: the Heralds fire his gate-houses to starve the rising's
supply line; Voss arrives at the camp with his strong-boxes on his back
and his ledgers singed — not converted, invested. The rising takes his
coin with open eyes. Earns: the book's most cynical alliance, on the
page.

**A3 · `19a-what-roothollow-sends.md` — "What Roothollow Sends"** —
Part III, before ch. 20. POV Nix, riding a supply-train down and back —
weeks of travel through freed countries, gate-roads, and lift-queues
(the scale canon makes this chapter: the rising's supply line is a
season-long artery, not a corridor). Freed low floors trading,
quarreling, sending sons and grain up; the Stone of the Climb with new
names; Roothollow's council managing a war it cannot see under a sky
that is a wall. Dramatic irony runs silent underneath: the supply
network the reader admires is Berrin's — never said, devastating on
re-read. Earns: the epic's width; the rising as a civilization, not a
column.

**A4 · `21a-the-wintering.md` — "The Wintering"** — Part III, between
ch. 21 and ch. 22. POV Vega. The army winters at the Frosthall:
forge-weddings, a giant's funeral for an old campaigner, Nix teaching
freed kobolds their letters, Dun mending the banner one-handed by the
hearth, Hale sitting nearer the fire each night. No plot advances;
every death in ch. 22 lands twice as hard. *Caution: warmth-before-
slaughter is a known shape; the chapter must be loved for itself — its
business is the army becoming a town, not the reader being set up.*

**A5 · `26a-bottled-court.md` — "The Bottled Court"** — Part IV,
between ch. 26 and ch. 27. POV Rhovan. The cast list promises Zephyra's
arc ("enemy → grudging debtor after the party frees her bottled
court"). The party, hunting scattered regiments through the Tempest
Court's cellars, finds her courtiers bottled in storm-glass by Vharuk's
wardens as sureties, and frees them. No thanks given; a debt incurred.
Earns: her one-chapter-late gales at the Herald's Gate become a debt
paid on a queen's schedule, not a plot convenience.

**A6 · `31a-wakes-price.md` — "The Wake's Price"** — Part IV, after
ch. 31 (before interlude 4). POV Rhovan. The Wake's answer to Rhovan's
defection: they call in the elf-woods debt, blockade a freed gate with
hired bows, and Calyne — of all people — breaks the blockade from
inside the Wake's own councils. Earns: Rhovan's faction arc closed
on-page; Calyne's competence shown (not told) before her death; the
"factions fight over everything but the goal" thesis given its last
proof before the Made.

**A7 · `38a-night-gardens.md` — "The Night Gardens"** — Part V, in the
ch. 39 span (floors 94–98 currently compress into one montage page).
POV Ede. One hushed chapter: the tower's memorial garden of the stolen
years — the taken world's seasons, bottled and flowering in the dark, a
country of them, crossed in days of silence. No battle; one
keystone-weight image; the last breath drawn before the throne. Earns:
Part V is currently all iron and drums — this is its one green chapter,
and the reader needs it exactly here.

**A8 · `40a-the-trials.md` — "The Trials"** — Part V, between ch. 40
and the epilogue. POV Ede. The morning after the fall: what the rising
does with Vharuk's willing — the collared-by-choice, the Heralds taken
alive, the floor-lords who profited. The mercy engine's final
examination, in a real court, with the freed as jury. Berrin's sentence
(ch. 36) becomes precedent, cited. Earns: the book's thesis tried in
daylight instead of asserted; the descent epilogue freed to be purely a
homecoming.

Eight chapters × ~2,600 words ≈ **+21k words**, plus eight plates
(plate-08a, -12a, -19a, -21a, -26a, -31a, -38a, -40a).

## B. Deepenings (in-place, no new chapters)

- **B1.** Battle chapters 7 / 22 / 35 / 40: one pass each for geography
  and breath — named ground, a map-clear middle movement, one
  held-frame image per battle, horizons and weather doing work
  (+~300 words each).
- **B2.** Floor-file scenery: chs. 5, 13, 24, 34 (the compressed
  marches) each get one paragraph per named floor drawn from its lore
  file — the land as the army actually crosses it, at the land's true
  size (+~800 total).
- **B3.** Verify the keystone/foreshadow lines from `editorial-pass.md`
  are all present and unbroken after the sweeps; place any still
  missing.

## C. The apparatus (~30–40 pages, zero risk to the novel)

New folder `book/appendices/` (build.py extended to append them after
the epilogue behind an "APPENDICES" part page):

- **C1. Dramatis personae** — the banners, by faction, one dry line
  each (the dry line is where the jokes hide).
- **C2. A gazetteer of the hundred floors** — one entry per floor
  distilled from `vision/lore/floors/` (what it was, who held it, what
  freed it). Players of the game will read this appendix first.
- **C3. The Charter of the Frosthall** — the document as signed, with
  the marks of signatories who could not write.
- **C4. Vyx's contract** — full clauses, including the story-clause and
  its annual schedule. Funnier and colder in legal language.
- **C5. Malgrim's ledger, complete** — the four interludes plus the
  entries the book never showed, ending mid-sentence on the day of the
  Gate.
- **C6. On the Yoke, the shards, and the Break-collar** — a
  half-scholarly note "compiled at Roothollow after the fall," giving
  the magic system its appendix without ever lecturing in-story. Must
  fold in the scale canon: why the tower can only be held by power, and
  what the scram and the Warden-breakings did to that power.

## Arithmetic after expansion

~119k (complete draft) + ~21k (A) + ~2k (B) ≈ **142k novel words** —
squarely epic — plus apparatus ≈ **500–560 pages** with 53 plates. If
the editorial iterations judge the middle heavy, we cut from B before
A, and from A only whole chapters — never by thinning prose.
