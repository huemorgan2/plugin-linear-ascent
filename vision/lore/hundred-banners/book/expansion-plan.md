# Expansion plan — growing the book without padding it

*Written when the draft stood at ~122k words with six chapters still landing
(projected ~138k complete ≈ 460–550 typeset pages). So the arithmetic fear —
"300 pages is short for epic fantasy" — is already answered: the draft
overshot its outline and sits inside the 100–120k+ epic standard. What
follows is therefore NOT page-filling. It is the list of mass the book has
EARNED but not yet staged: promises the outline made that currently happen
off-page, plus the appendix apparatus that gives an epic its felt weight.
Every addition below must pass one test: if the book were already 600 pages,
would we still want this scene? If no, it doesn't go in.*

## Rule of thumb

Padding stretches what exists; growth stages what was promised. We add
scenes only where a reader could today ask "wait, when did THAT happen?" —
and the honest answer is "between chapters."

## A. New chapters (8) — the off-page promises, staged

**A1 · "The Tale of the First Banner"** — Part I, between ch. 8 and the
interlude. POV Ede. A fireside chapter: Dun Harrow tells the founding of the
Shieldwall — Aldis Bright, the first climb, why the banner is sewn with a
wall and not a sword. Earns: Dun deepened before his death at the feast; the
ch. 39 Champion reveal converted from surprise to destiny; the Aldis-Bright
stone-name hint given its story. (LotR seat: the Shadow of the Past chapter —
history told by firelight.)

**A2 · "The Toll-Burning"** — Part II, after ch. 12. POV Rhovan. The cast
list promises Voss's turn ("enemy of the purse → quartermaster, after the
Heralds burn his tolls at floor 30") — it currently happens between
chapters. Stage it: the Heralds fire his gate-houses to starve the rising's
supply line; Voss arrives at the camp with his strong-boxes on his back and
his ledgers singed, not converted — invested. The rising takes his coin with
open eyes. Earns: the book's most cynical alliance, on the page.

**A3 · "What Roothollow Sends"** — Part III, before ch. 20. POV Nix (riding
a supply-train down and back). The world below, revisited at midpoint: freed
low floors trading, quarreling, sending sons and grain up the gate roads;
the Stone of the Climb with new names; Roothollow's council managing a war
it can't see. Dramatic irony runs silent underneath: the supply network the
reader admires is Berrin's — never said, devastating on re-read. Earns: the
epic's width; the rising as a civilization, not a column.

**A4 · "The Wintering"** — Part III, between ch. 21 and ch. 22. POV Vega.
The army winters at the Frosthall: forge-weddings, a giant's funeral for an
old campaigner, Nix teaching freed kobolds their letters, Dun mending the
banner one-handed by the hearth, Hale sitting nearer the fire each night.
No plot advances; every death in ch. 22 lands twice as hard. (GoT lesson:
the feast kills because the hall was warm first.)

**A5 · "The Bottled Court"** — Part IV, between ch. 26 and ch. 27. POV
Rhovan. The cast list promises Zephyra's arc ("enemy → grudging debtor after
the party frees her bottled court") — unstaged. The party, hunting scattered
regiments through the Tempest Court's cellars, finds her courtiers bottled
in storm-glass by Vharuk's wardens as sureties, and frees them. No thanks
given; a debt incurred. Earns: her one-chapter-late gales at the Herald's
Gate become a debt paid on a queen's schedule, not a plot convenience.

**A6 · "The Wake's Price"** — Part IV, after ch. 31. POV Rhovan. The Wake's
answer to Rhovan's defection: they call in the elf-woods debt, blockade a
freed gate with hired bows, and Calyne — of all people — breaks the
blockade from inside the Wake's own councils. Earns: Rhovan's faction arc
closed on-page; Calyne's competence shown (not told) before her death;
the "factions fight over everything but the goal" thesis given its last
proof before the Made.

**A7 · "The Night Gardens"** — Part V, inside the ch. 39 span (floors
94–98 currently compress into one montage page). POV Ede. One hushed
chapter: the tower's memorial garden of the stolen years — the taken
world's seasons, bottled and flowering in the dark. The army crosses it in
silence; no battle, one keystone-weight image; the last breath drawn before
the throne. Earns: Part V is currently all iron and drums — this is its one
green chapter, and the reader needs it exactly here.

**A8 · "The Trials"** — Part V, between ch. 40 and the epilogue. POV Ede.
The morning after the fall: what the rising does with Vharuk's willing —
the collared-by-choice, the Heralds taken alive, the floor-lords who
profited. The mercy engine's final examination, in a real court, with the
freed as jury. Berrin's sentence (ch. 36) becomes precedent, cited. Earns:
the book's thesis tried in daylight instead of asserted; the descent
epilogue freed to be purely a homecoming.

Eight chapters × ~2,600 words ≈ **+21k words**, each with a plate
(prompts to add to plates.tsv; same graphite anchor).

## B. Deepenings (in-place, no new chapters)

- **B1.** Battle chapters 7 / 22 / 35 / 40: one pass each for geography and
  breath — named ground, a map-clear middle movement, one held-frame image
  per battle. (+~300 words each.)
- **B2.** Floor-file scenery: chs. 5, 13, 24, 34 (the compressed marches)
  each get one paragraph per named floor from its lore file — the land as
  the army actually crosses it. (+~800 total.)
- **B3.** The keystone/foreshadow pass from editorial-pass.md (already
  specced) — adds little mass, multiplies felt depth.

## C. The apparatus (the Tolkien move — ~30-40 pages, zero risk to the novel)

- **C1. Dramatis personae** — the banners, by faction, with one dry line
  each (the dry line is where the jokes hide).
- **C2. A gazetteer of the hundred floors** — one entry per floor distilled
  from the floor files (what it was, who held it, what freed it). The
  players of the game will read this appendix first.
- **C3. The Charter of the Frosthall** — the document itself, as signed,
  with the marks of signatories who could not write.
- **C4. Vyx's contract** — full clauses, including the story-clause and its
  annual schedule. Funnier and colder in legal language.
- **C5. Malgrim's ledger, complete** — the four interludes plus the entries
  the book never showed, ending mid-sentence on the day of the Gate.
- **C6. On the Yoke, the shards, and the Break-collar** — a half-scholarly
  note "compiled at Roothollow after the fall," giving the magic system its
  appendix without ever having lectured in-story.

## D. What we do NOT do

- No new POV characters. No subplots that don't already have rails.
- No stretching existing scenes with weather and walking.
- No second traitor, no resurrections, no extra Warden fights.
- If any addition reads as detour on the read-through, it dies in
  editorial iteration 1 — additions face the same critics as everything
  else.

## Arithmetic after expansion

~138k (complete draft) + ~21k (A) + ~2k (B) ≈ **161k novel words** — upper
end of epic range (Game of Thrones ≈ 292k; Fellowship ≈ 187k; this sits
proudly between "big debut" and "saga") — plus apparatus ≈ **550-620 pages**
with 53 plates. If the editorial iterations judge the middle heavy, we cut
from B before A, and from A only whole chapters — never by thinning prose.

## Sequencing

1. Finish the six in-flight chapters (original draft complete).
2. Write A1–A8 (same style guide, same per-chapter writer discipline) +
   their plates.
3. B-passes.
4. C-apparatus.
5. THEN the ten editorial iterations run on the full expanded text — the
   critics judge the additions with everything else.

*Nothing in this plan executes until approved.*
