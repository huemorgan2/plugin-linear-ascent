# THE HUNDRED BANNERS — prose style guide & continuity bible

*Every chapter is written against [../00-outline.md](../00-outline.md) (the
beats are law) and this guide (the voice is law). Canon lives in
[../../world-lore.md](../../world-lore.md) and the floor files in
[../../floors/](../../floors/) — when a chapter stands on a floor, its
scenery, keeper, beasts, and places come from that floor's file.*

## Voice

- **Tight third person, past tense.** One POV per chapter (the outline names
  it). We see only what the POV sees; we know only what they know. The reader
  must NOT be told Berrin is the traitor before chapter 34 — his chapters-as-
  seen-by-others must read as warm, reliable, beloved.
- **Concrete and elegiac.** The lore voice: short declaratives in action,
  long breaths in grief. Things are counted, named, mended, carried. Avoid
  abstraction; prefer the fence-nail to the feeling.
- **Sentence music.** Vary length hard: three-word sentences after long ones.
  In battle, cut clauses. In mourning, let them run.
- **No modern idiom.** Banned: okay, guys, adrenaline, seconds/minutes (use
  heartbeats, breaths, the time a lamp takes to gutter), percentages,
  "situation", "impact" as a verb.
- **No firearms, ever.** Bows, blades, spears, axes, chains, siege engines,
  aether. "Powder" only as blasting-powder in mines.

## Diction (use these words; they are the world's)

The Theft · the Ascent · the Yoke · the fever · the Pressed / Natives / the
Wrongmade · collars, Break-collar · stair-lift · gate-town · keeper ·
shardmind (a splinter, a sliver) · Roothollow · the Stone of the Climb · the
rising · the freed · gate roads · the Made (floors 81+) · wardens are "welded"
things. A kill of a Native is a *cure*; of a Wrongmade an *eviction*; of a
Pressed, a death that should not have been needed.

## Dialogue

- Speech is plain and period-neutral; keepers talk like farmers and
  foremen, not wizards. Giants speak slowly and finish their sentences.
  Elves answer questions with fractions. Nix talks fast, counts things, and
  never uses one word where three funnier ones fit.
- **Sliver (Ede's shardmind)** speaks in italics, dry and ancient: *"I have
  carried forty-one climbers. You are the ninth stubborn enough to matter."*
- The decade-Wardens each have a register: Gnarl (music-hall bitterness),
  Vyx (contract clauses), Cindermaw (geologic patience), Zephyra (royal
  bargain), Malgrim (kind, reasonable, wrong).

## Mechanics

- Chapter files: `book/chapters/NN-slug.md`. First line: `# Chapter N — Title`
  (or `# Prologue` / `# Epilogue` / `# From Malgrim's Ledger`). No other
  front-matter. Scene breaks: a line containing only `⁂`.
- Length: 2,200–2,800 words per chapter; battle chapters (7, 22, 35, 40)
  3,000–3,500; interludes 300–450 words, written as ledger-entries in
  Malgrim's voice (`Item: …`), epilogue 2,500–3,000.
- Wrap prose at ~78 columns. Em-dashes spaced — like this. No footnotes.

## Continuity anchors (do not contradict)

- Ede: sober two years at ch.1; carries the rebuilt muster-roll from ch.19;
  knows Berrin is the traitor from ch.29 and tells no one but Sliver (ch.30).
- Rhovan: Wake agent until his public break in ch.26; Vyx's hostage-contract
  (one true story annually) binds him for life — pays it in the epilogue.
- Vega: forge-mark guilt from ch.6; her brother Ulfar arrives ch.17; their
  mother's forge-oath wins ch.38.
- Nix: takes the dropped Shieldwall standard in ch.4, throws down the collar
  there; walks point on the Trapdoor Fields (ch.20); leads collar-breaker
  squads from ch.32; holds Berrin's parole from ch.36.
- Dun Harrow: freed ch.7, hands Ede the banner ch.8, dies ch.22 north door.
- Hale: teacher-antagonist chs.3–10, breaks ch.10, dies ch.22 south door.
- Calyne: enters ch.15 under suspicion; secretly hunting Berrin; gives Ede
  her cipher-book ch.30; dies ch.34 proving the case.
- Berrin Or: on-page from ch.2; warm, indispensable; "wounded" at the feast
  (ch.22) saving Ede; unmasked ch.34; flees upward; sentenced ch.36 to
  provision every floor he sold, forever, under Nix's parole.
- Sliver: re-chooses Ede publicly ch.2; confesses the shard-network secret
  early, ch.30 (the Yoke rides the dead network's bones — confirmed ch.38).
- The Champion (ch.39): Aldis Bright, founder of the first Shieldwall banner.
- Zephyra: refused ch.25; scatters a third of the host; her gales arrive one
  chapter late at the Herald's Gate (ch.35).
- Timeline: the climb takes about two years; seasons only matter indoors
  (the tower has no weather but what each floor kept).
- **Scale (hard canon).** Each floor is a country the breadth of a whole
  stolen realm — horizons, mountain ranges, rivers, marches of days
  between towns. Each floor carries kilometers of its own sky and its own
  weather; the true ceiling is almost never visible, at most a faint dark
  grain on the clearest days. Nobody ever sees a floor's far wall from
  its middle; the rim is a journey, not a glance. From outside, the
  tower cannot be taken in whole — it fills the world like a cliff-face,
  top beyond the air. And it is not masonry: no brick could hold one
  country above another. It stands by aether — grav-engines and binding-
  spells — with black iron only as the anchor the power is bolted to.
  Never write the tower as a building you could see all of, and never
  have a character cross a floor in an afternoon.
- **The Theft's age (hard canon).** The Theft ended eighty years before
  the rising — within living memory; the eldest freed remember the
  ground. Tower-era spans (waiting in the tower, watching Vharuk,
  warden ledgers, the engines' vigil) read in decades and lifetimes,
  never centuries. The stolen realms' OWN histories may be as ancient
  as you like — old countries were stolen whole.
