# THE HUNDRED BANNERS — a novel of the Linear Ascent

*Stage 1 of the book: the full architecture. Parts, chapters, plot turns, the
cast and who turns, the battles, the drawings. Built on
[world-lore.md](../world-lore.md), [story-ideas.md](../story-ideas.md)
(storyline 2, expanded), and the hundred floor files in [floors/](../floors/).
Nothing here is prose yet — this is the skeleton to argue with before a word
of chapter text is written.*

**The book in one sentence.** A disgraced captain rebuilds the company she
lost, and the company becomes an army, and the army becomes the gathered
world — the one thing the tower was built to prevent — while the traitor who
sold her climbs beside her the whole way.

**Format.** ~40 chapters + prologue and epilogue, 6–9 pages each ≈ 300 pages.
Five parts, one per span of twenty floors; each part closes on a decade-
Warden. Between parts, one-page interludes — *proclamations from Malgrim's
ledger* — the enemy reading the rising in his own voice. ~45 pencil plates
(one per chapter plus maps; plan at the end).

**Point of view.** Four rotating POVs, tight third person: **Ede** (the spine,
~half the chapters), **Rhovan** (the secrets), **Vega** (the machines and the
guilt), and **Nix** (a collar-broken goblin — the war from the ground, the
book's conscience). No POV for the traitor: the reader must trust who Ede
trusts.

## The cast

### The three

- **Ede Stonhal** — woman, fighter, of Men. Led the Shieldwall Company into
  the Red Warcamp massacre (floor 20); came back down alone; her people are
  alive in Skarn's cages. Broken commander → general of the rising. Her flaw:
  she would rather die making amends than live being followed.
- **Rhovan of the Grey Rides** — elf archer, agent of the Wake. Opens the book
  blackmailing Ede; spends it learning the difference between a handler and a
  friend. His flaw: he tells the truth only in fractions.
- **Vega Coalbrow** — giant wizard, forge-matron. Her forge-mark is on the
  collar-steel of Skarn's cages. Absolution-seeker → armorer of the freed. Her
  flaw: she believes everything broken can be mended, including people who
  don't want to be.

### The dependables (fixed stars — the reader must be able to lean on them)

- **Dun Harrow** — Ede's old standard-bearer, freed from the cages at floor
  20 one-armed and unbroken. Carries the Shieldwall banner the rest of the
  book. Never wavers once. *(His steadiness is why his death at the Frosthall
  feast is the book's hardest page.)*
- **Nix** — goblin, once cup-bearer to Gnarl the Goblin King, throws down the
  collar in Part I and stays. Small, unkillable, funny, and the first Pressed
  the reader loves — the whole mercy system given a face and a POV.
- **Hobb Fennick, Bruna Milkwater, and the keepers** — the floor files'
  keepers cameo throughout as the rising's fixed points: the people who held
  their floors alone for years and hand the army their hard-won maps.
- **The shardmind, Sliver** — Ede's splinter-voice: dry, ancient, loyal, and
  carrying one secret it is ashamed of (Part V).

### The turners (everyone below changes sides, direction, or nature)

- **Serjeant Hale** — leader of the Cullers (kill-all doctrine), the man who
  taught Ede to fight. Antagonist in Part I–II → broken by his own doctrine at
  the Broodwarrens → dies at the Frosthall feast holding the door he once
  would have barred from the outside. *(The Boromir seat: the ally whose fall
  and redemption define the moral stakes.)*
- **Thane Ulfar Coalbrow** — Vega's brother, voice of the Forge-Court: *keep*
  the tower, seize its engines, lower the floors gently over a generation.
  Ally → rival → tempted catastrophically at the Reactor Galleries (93) →
  saved by his sister, at a price.
- **Gilder Voss** — Tollmaster-General, profiteer of freed gates. Enemy of
  the rising's purse → its quartermaster, after the Heralds burn his tolls at
  floor 30. Never becomes *good*; becomes *invested* — the book's proof that
  a cause can use a man's greed without catching it.
- **Mistress Calyne** — the Wake's spymaster, Rhovan's handler. Suspected of
  being the traitor for three whole parts — cold, secretive, always one floor
  ahead. The suspicion is wrong: she has been hunting the traitor herself
  since page one, and dies exposing him. *(The Snape seat: the hateful ally
  who was loyal all along.)*
- **Quartermaster Berrin Or** — Roothollow's provisioner, Ede's oldest friend,
  the man who kept her fed through her drunk years, beloved by the reader from
  chapter one. **He sold Shieldwall.** A Herald's willing collar sits under
  his shirt for the entire book. *(The Wormtongue-Littlefinger seat, played
  as a friend.)*
- **Zephyra, the Storm Queen** (Warden, 70) — offers the rising the sky. Her
  price is Vharuk's throne. Refused, she scatters half the army across the
  wind floors — enemy → grudging debtor after the party frees her bottled
  court → absent ally at the Herald's Gate, arriving exactly one chapter after
  she'd have been decisive. *(No eagle arrives on time in this book.)*

### The speaking powers (the extraordinary encounters)

The lesser Wardens are welded beasts — mute engines. But the **decade
Wardens hold courts, and they talk.** Every twenty floors, the war stops for
a conversation the reader will not forget:

- **Gnarl, the Goblin King (10)** — a joke crowned by the tower at his own
  people's expense; knows it; rules a muster-meadow of banners with bitter
  music-hall grandeur. The first enemy who *speaks* — and the first proof the
  enemy is a prisoner.
- **Warlord Skarn (20)** — the Red Orc who believes his collar is a rank.
- **The Barrow King (30)** — a drowned king's mound given voice; speaks only
  in the funeral-forms of Men; can be out-mourned, not out-fought.
- **Matriarch Vyx (40)** — the spider-queen of the Broodthrone, silk-voiced,
  contract-minded. The rising's first *negotiation* with a monster.
- **Cindermaw the Wyrm (50)** — older than the Theft; chose its caldera; the
  only being in the tower that pities Vharuk. Speaks in geologic patience and
  drops the book's midpoint clue about the traitor: *"I smell one of you
  twice — once going up, once having already been."*
- **Jarl Hrimgar (60)** — the welded hall-lord whose freed hall becomes the
  rising's parliament.
- **Zephyra (70)**, **the Pale Huntsman (80)** — the queen who bargains and
  the hunter who doesn't speak at all, which is worse.
- **Malgrim, Herald of the King (90)** — the freed man who chose the collar;
  Ede's dark mirror; the best talker in the book.
- **Vharuk (100)** — the last conversation, and the shortest.

## The five parts

Chapters are numbered straight through. Each entry: floor(s) · what happens ·
**the turn** · the plate (pencil drawing).

### PROLOGUE — The Night of the Theft
A farm rising into the dark, seen from below by the child Berrin Or — the
traitor's own origin, unsigned; the reader won't know whose eyes these were
until Part V. *PLATE: the underside of a stolen country, roots and lamplight.*

### Part I — THE DEBT (Roothollow · floors 1–20) — 8 chapters

**1. The Trainer of Other People's Recruits.** Roothollow. Ede, sober two
years, drills recruits she refuses to climb with; the Stone of the Climb
carries thirty names she cut there herself. Rhovan arrives with a sealed
ledger-page. **Turn: the Warcamp wasn't a defeat — it was a sale.** *PLATE:
Roothollow under the tower's root, an aether-forge beside a horse trough.*

**2. The Third Refusal.** Ede says no twice; Berrin talks her out of hiding
(the traitor, launching the hunt for himself — the book's quietest irony).
The shardmind Sliver chooses her *again*, publicly — the shards don't
re-choose. **Turn: she climbs.** *PLATE: the gate-queue at dawn, one splinter
of crystal alight.*

**3. Hedge and Hollow.** Floors 1–3, fast: the Fencerows, the flooded
pasture — the low war as it really is: rat-hunts, hedge-ambushes, Hobb
Fennick's counts. Establishes the mercy system in action; Ede kills a
surrendered kobold from old habit and Rhovan logs it without a word. **Turn:
Cullers under Serjeant Hale annex the party "for escort" — Ede's old teacher
owns the low floors.** *PLATE: hedgerows under a vast shadowless false-daylight sky, a
wolf between the gaps.*

**4. The King of Nothing.** Floor 10, the Kingsfield. First battle: militia
and Cullers storm the banner-meadow. Gnarl holds court in a hundred stolen
banners; his cup-bearer Nix drops the cup and picks up a dropped Shieldwall
standard *to hide behind*. Ede takes the throne-tent — and Gnarl, offered the
Break-collar, refuses it for his crown and dies a king of nothing. Nix takes
it instead. **Turn: the first Pressed joins — and the first Warden dies
refusing to be saved.** *PLATE: Gnarl on his rifle-less crate-throne of
banner-poles, crown askew.*

**5. The Ledger of Gates.** Floors 11–16. Voss's Tollmen bar a freed lift —
against canon law and with better pay. Rhovan burgles the toll-ledger;
in it, a payment *up* the tower dated the week of the Shieldwall sale.
**Turn: the traitor's money moved through the Tollmen — and someone in
Roothollow signed.** *PLATE: a toll-chain across a stair-lift arch, coins
nailed to the post.*

**6. The Adit.** Floors 17–19, the approach mine. Vega joins at the Gear
Galleries: she has seen the cage-manifests — collar-steel bearing her own
forge-mark. Hale's Cullers massacre a surrendered kobold dig-crew; Nix stands
between the last kobold and Hale's blade, shaking. Ede chooses Nix. **Turn:
the party breaks from the Cullers — Ede against her own teacher.** *PLATE:
Vega's hand over her forge-mark stamped in a collar.*

**7. The Red Warcamp.** Floor 20. The set-piece assault: three companies,
mine-dark, drum-fire, siege-gear against a warcamp pitched inside a stolen
dwarven mine. Vega breaks the cage-locks she helped forge. Skarn dies
believing his collar was a rank. **Turn: the cages open — Shieldwall lives.**
*PLATE: full-spread battle — lantern-light, drums, the cages cracked open.*

**8. What the Freed Forgive.** Aftermath. The freed Shieldwall does not
embrace Ede — half believe *she* sold them; Dun Harrow silences the camp by
handing her the banner with his one arm. A freed prisoner names the courier
who carried their route to Skarn: a hooded man who *smelled of Roothollow
bread*. **Turn: the traitor is someone from home.** *PLATE: Dun, one-armed,
holding out the banner.*

*Interlude — from Malgrim's ledger: "Item: the woman climbs. Item: our
gate-toll investment matures. Note to the willing below: feed her the trail
slowly. A hope led is a hope steered."*

### Part II — THE BANNER WAR (floors 21–40) — 8 chapters

**9. The Quiet.** Floors 21–25. The Gallows Fair: a floor whose people *beg
the rising to pass by* — they've survived reprisal before. First meeting with
the Quiet; Ede loses the argument and it costs a village. In passing, the
Broodwarrens (24) surrender whole to Nix's parley — the rising's first floor
freed without a blow. **Turn: not everyone wants to be freed — the rising has
a second enemy: fear.** *PLATE: empty fairground cages, bunting in the wind.*

**10. Hale's Lesson.** Floor 24 revisited, then 26–28. The Cullers, holding
the rear gates, break the Broodwarrens' surrender behind the host's van —
kill-all doctrine against a warren that had already yielded to a goblin's
word. The party descends too late; brood-silk takes twenty Cullers; Hale
carries out his own dead. **Turn: Hale's doctrine breaks him — he asks
Ede, once, how the goblin does it.** *PLATE: Hale kneeling in silk-shadow
among cocooned armor.*

**11. The Banner War.** Floors 29–30 approach. The book's ugliest battle:
Cullers, Tollmen, and the Wake's hired bows fight *each other* on the Tithe
Barrows over gate-rights while the real enemy watches. Berrin arrives with
supply-trains and brokers the peace (building his cover, steering the war).
**Turn: mid-battle, the Barrow King raises the tithe-wights — the factions
stop killing each other or die separately. Forced unity.** *PLATE: three
banners tangled on a burial mound as the mounds open.*

**12. The Out-Mourned King.** Floor 30. The Barrow King cannot be beaten
with steel — the moated mound floods, the wights reform. Ede wins by
funeral-right: the freed Shieldwall dead are named and mourned at the mound's
edge until the drowned king's court concedes precedence and the Warden's
grief collapses it. **Turn: the rising learns its true weapon is not the
sword — it is what the sword means.** *PLATE: a hundred climbers kneeling at
a moat, naming their dead.*

**13. Silk Terms.** Floors 31–39, compressed march. Then the Broodthrone:
Matriarch Vyx will not fight — she *contracts*. Passage for the army in
exchange for tribute: the army's cook-fires' warmth, a hostage of rank, and
"one true story never told before, annually." Rhovan volunteers as hostage —
and uses the year's story-clause to tell Vyx a fraction of the Wake's
secrets, buying a spider-queen's goodwill with his own faction's coin.
**Turn: the rising deals with a monster — and Rhovan burns his handler to do
it.** *PLATE: Vyx on the tribute-hung throne-web, holding a teacup of warmth.*

**14. The Hostage Chapter.** Rhovan's POV alone in the Broodthrone: the
book's strangest, quietest chapter. Vyx, silk-voiced, tells *him* a story in
return — of watching Vharuk's engines pass beneath her caverns, and of the
courier who has climbed past her web **twice**. **Turn: the traitor has been
up the tower before — and come back down.** *PLATE: Rhovan and Vyx playing
some game with knots of silk.*

**15. Calyne.** The Wake's spymaster arrives furious about Rhovan's leak —
and stays, embedding in the rising's council. Everything about her reads as
the traitor: ciphers, absences, a Roothollow past. (She is hunting Berrin;
the reader must not know.) **Turn: the false suspect enters.** *PLATE:
Calyne's grey silhouette on the Grey Rides, crows on the bridleway.*

**16. The Molt.** Floor 40 falls (Vyx keeps her contract; her Warden-court
stands aside). The army winters in the Hanging City (51 is ahead — this is
the chasm-mouth camps): first census of the rising — nine peoples, forty
banners, one goblin. Nix teaches freed kobolds to say their own names again.
**Turn: the host is no longer an escort — it is a nation moving.** *PLATE:
the census-wall, names in forty scripts.*

*Interlude — Malgrim's ledger: "Item: they mourn well. Recommend against
letting them mourn at scale. Item: our friend requests extraction. Denied.
A ledger entry does not retire."*

### Part III — THE GATHERING (floors 41–60) — 7 chapters

**17. The Frozen Sea.** Floors 41–46. The ice campaign: sledge-trains,
floe-wolves, the Locked Fleet freed from the ice with Vega's thaw-craft.
Ulfar Coalbrow arrives with a Forge-Court legion — welcome muscle, wrong
reasons. **Turn: the Forge-Court joins the rising in order to inherit the
tower.** *PLATE: an army crossing pack-ice past ships locked keel-up.*

**18. Brother and Sister.** Vega's POV. Ulfar shows her the Forge-Court's
survey: the grav-engines *could* lower the floors — his plan is real, gentle,
and requires keeping the Yoke's core lit for a generation. Vega can't refute
the mathematics, only the price. **Turn: the rising's first true schism is
inside one family.** *PLATE: two giants arguing over a light-table of the
tower's cross-section.*

**19. The Wyrm's Pity.** Floor 50, the Cindermouth. Battle on the caldera
rim against the tribute-cult, then the parley inside it: Cindermaw, coiled
around its own hoard of pyre-ash, tells the rising it is the third army it
has watched climb — and gives Ede the smell-clue: *"one of you has passed me
twice."* Ede starts a quiet muster-roll of everyone who has ever climbed
before. Berrin's name is not on it, because Berrin keeps the rolls. **Turn:
the traitor-hunt turns inward — and the traitor is holding the list.**
*PLATE: Cindermaw's eye, a campfire reflected in it.*

**20. The Long Stair.** Floors 51–59 compressed: the Hanging City, the
Trapdoor Fields (a held-breath chapter — the army crossing a floor that is
one enormous trap, in silence, Nix walking point because he's lightest).
**Turn: the army takes losses no battle caused — the tower itself is the
enemy now.** *PLATE: lantern-line of an army crossing dust-funnels at night.*

**21. The Frosthall Parliament.** Floor 60 freed; Jarl Hrimgar's mead-hall
becomes the rising's first capital. The parliament of the banners: Wake,
Forge-Court, Tollmen, keepers, freed Pressed — the gathering the tower was
built to prevent, arguing like a real parliament. Voss is made quartermaster-
general (set a thief to keep thieves). **Turn: the rising signs a charter —
it has become the thing Vharuk fears.** *PLATE: the mead-hall packed, a
hundred banners hung from the rafters.*

**22. The Feast.** The Red-Wedding chapter, inverted in advance by one page:
Calyne warns Ede *that morning* that there is a Herald inside the charter's
inner circle — and is dismissed as the suspect accusing others. That night,
Heralds seeded through the serving-lines strike the parliament. Dun Harrow
dies holding the north door. Hale dies holding the south one — the Culler
who spent Part I killing prisoners spends his last hour buying time for
goblin cooks to flee. **Turn: the charter survives; the innocence doesn't.
Berrin "takes an arrow" saving Ede — cementing his cover forever.** *PLATE:
the banner-rafters aflame, two doors held.*

**23. The Reckoning of Names.** Aftermath. Ede rebuilds the muster-roll from
memory over Berrin's protests; Calyne and Rhovan privately compare ciphers
for the first time. Nix finds an unburnt serving-list in a Herald's boot —
the infiltration was ordered *by someone who knew the seating*. **Turn: the
inner circle is down to six names and the book knows it.** *PLATE: six chairs
in a burnt hall.*

*Interlude — Malgrim's ledger: "Item: the feast bill comes due and is worth
it. Item: our friend performs grief convincingly. He always did. I collared
him for it."*

### Part IV — THE SCATTERED SKY (floors 61–80) — 9 chapters

**24. The Anchor Chains.** Floors 61–65: the sky campaign. The army crosses
mooring-chains between floors, fights through the Mast Forest wreck-fields;
the freed sky-folk teach climbers to rig. **Turn: the rising gains a navy of
sorts — salvaged sky-skiffs.** *PLATE: soldiers walking a mile-long chain
above cloud.*

**25. The Storm Queen's Price.** Floor 70. Zephyra receives the rising's
embassy in the Tempest Court and offers everything: her gales to carry the
whole host to the Made in a day. Price: Vharuk's throne, hers, after. The
parliament's vote is genuinely close — Ulfar votes yes. Ede casts the no.
**Turn: refused, Zephyra opens the bottled gales — and scatters a third of
the army across the wind floors.** *PLATE: an army blown off a cloud-ridge
like seed.*

**26. Gathering the Blown.** Ede's worst hour as a general: the scattered
regiments must be recollected floor by floor while the Wake demands the
elf-woods detour *now* as its price for staying. Rhovan defies Calyne
publicly to hold the Wake in line — and is disavowed by his own faction.
**Turn: Rhovan chooses the rising over the Wake, and pays with his name.**
*PLATE: Rhovan breaking his Wake-token over a knee.*

**27. The Eye.** Floor 78, the Eye Wall: the scattered survivors shelter in
the storm's uncanny calm eye — one still, rain-hushed chapter of the army
breathing, burying, and singing Dun's muster-song. The calm ends when the
Huntsman's horns sound through the eye-wall. **Turn: the Pale Court has
noticed the leadership travels ahead of its army.** *PLATE: an army asleep in
a circle of standing rain.*

**28. The Coursing.** Floors 74–80: the chase sequence. The Pale Huntsman
courses the three leads and Nix as quarry across the Snare Woods and the
Quarry Runs — hedge-funnels, wire-snares, horns always one glade behind.
Evasion, not battle; the hunters' rules learned from floor-file lore (the
Huntsman never takes quarry that turns and walks toward the horns). Ede,
last of her strength, *walks toward the horns*. **Turn: the Huntsman,
bound by his own court's law, must receive her — quarry no longer.** *PLATE:
Ede walking into a horn-lit clearing, hounds parting.*

**29. The Pale Court.** Floor 80. In the Huntsman's court, Ede bargains
under hunt-law for the scattered army's safe passage — and sees, mounted on
the trophy wall, a Shieldwall shield lost at floor 20, sold upward. Voss's
toll-marks are on its rim; the sale routed through Roothollow the same week
Berrin's granary was rebuilt. She says nothing. **Turn: Ede knows — and must
climb the rest of the way beside him, pretending.** *PLATE: a trophy wall of
shields, one of them hers.*

**30. What Ede Carries.** Short Ede-POV chapter, all interior: the
loneliness of knowing. She tells only Sliver — and Sliver, unprompted,
confesses *its* secret early: the shard network's bones carry the Yoke
(planting Part V's revelation as a shared burden). Calyne, half-guessing,
hands Ede her cipher-book "in case I stop climbing." **Turn: the book's two
hunters of the traitor finally, silently, ally.** *PLATE: Ede alone at a
watch-fire, the splinter glowing on her palm.*

**31. Breaking the Hunt.** The Huntsman's fall — the last stolen-realm
Warden, the army reunited under the pale kennels' freed hounds. The muster
before the Made: seventy banners, thirty-one peoples, one charter. **Turn:
the stolen world is behind them; above is only the thing Vharuk built.**
*PLATE: the army at the foot of the iron outworks, green behind, black
above.*

*Interlude — Malgrim's ledger: "Item: the Huntsman kept better books than the
Queen; both are spent. Item: our friend reports the woman no longer sleeps.
Good. Sleepless generals sign anything."*

### Part V — THE MADE (floors 81–100 + the descent) — 9 chapters

**32. The Siege of the Outworks.** Floors 81–84: trench-and-rampart war
against manufactured legions — Unrivet-sappers, rivet-steel shields, Vega's
forge-crews unmaking the tower with its own tools. Nix leads collar-breaker
squads that free enemy imps mid-battle. **Turn: the rising's line of battle
now *grows* as it advances — every broken collar is a recruit.** *PLATE:
full-spread siege — trenches, drums, a wall-flesh rampart rearing.*

**33. The Welded.** Floor 84, the Welding Halls. What the cages fed: the
taken of Men welded into soldiers. Among the fresh-welded, a Shieldwall
face — one of Ede's thirty, sold twenty years ago, now iron from the jaw
down. He knows her. **Turn: the sale's full price is finally visible — and
the freed man's first word is the courier's name: "Bread."** *PLATE: a welded
soldier's human eyes above an iron mouth.*

**34. The Vizier's Floor.** Floors 85–89 compressed; then the confrontation
staged *before* Malgrim: Calyne springs her trap on Berrin with the
serving-list, the toll-ledger, and the granary dates — publicly, in council.
Berrin, cornered, is faster than his years: Calyne dies proving him guilty.
He flees *upward*, to his collar's owner. **Turn: the traitor unmasked at the
cost of the one who always knew — and the rising marches on the Herald's Gate
angry.** *PLATE: Calyne's cipher-book in Ede's hands, blood on the margin.*

**35. The Battle of the Herald's Gate.** Floor 90. The book's Pelennor: the
hundred banners against Malgrim's mustered legions under the drum-throne.
The turn of the battle is not steel: Vega, Nix, and the freed chantry-priests
cast the mass Break-collar the whole book has been assembling — and the
Pressed ranks halt mid-charge, collars snapping like frost, an army freed
*inside* the battle. Zephyra's gales arrive one chapter late and merely tidy
the rout. **Turn: the rising wins its greatest battle by refusing to fight
most of it.** *PLATE: double-spread — the drum-throne, collars bursting in a
wave across the ranks.*

**36. Malgrim.** The parley in the wreckage. Malgrim and Ede, two freed
captives of the tower, one of whom re-collared himself. He is offered
everything the book believes in — and clutches the chain, because it chose
him and no one else ever had. Ede puts him down grieving. Berrin, delivered
by Malgrim's own clerks as a closing courtesy ("a ledger entry does not
retire"), faces the freed Shieldwall — and Ede gives judgment to the thirty.
They sentence him to *live*: to walk down the freed tower and re-provision
every floor he sold, forever, under Nix's parole. **Turn: the rising's
justice is labor, not blood — and it costs Ede more than execution would.**
*PLATE: Malgrim's empty gauntlet clutching a broken chain.*

**37. The Obsidian Stair.** Floors 91–92: the leadership climbs the black
glass among their own reflections; each POV meets their worst self (Ede meets
the general who'd have hanged Berrin; Rhovan the agent who never turned; Vega
the smith who kept selling; Nix the cup-bearer who never dropped the cup).
**Turn: the book's characters pass their own final exams — the reflections
break.** *PLATE: four figures on a mirror-black stair, their reflections not
matching.*

**38. Scram.** Floor 93, the Reactor Galleries. Ulfar's temptation: hand on
the core-controls, his gentle-lowering plan finally *possible* — keep the
Yoke lit a generation, inherit the tower warm. Vega talks him down not with
ethics but with their mother's forge-oath, and Ulfar himself throws the
scram. The tower goes quiet; the Yoke dies mid-broadcast on a hundred floors
at once. Sliver's kin fall silent too — the shard network's old bones going
dark — and Sliver stays lit, alone. **Turn: the Forge-Court's dream dies by
the Forge-Court's own hand.** *PLATE: a giant's hand on a lever, colorless
light dying.*

**39. The Last Door.** Floor 99. The Champion in the door: **Aldis Bright**,
founder of the first Shieldwall banner an age ago — the very banner Dun
carried, the company Ede rebuilt. Freed by name, weld, and Recant, she takes
one look at the muster-roll of the rising and asks Ede: *"Who commands?"* —
and, told, *salutes.* **Turn: the book's oldest debt (Shieldwall's honor)
is paid by its oldest soldier.** *PLATE: a woman stepping out of a black
door, sword offered hilt-first.*

**40. The Throne.** Floor 100. Vharuk — starved of the core, deaf to his
tower, alone above a hall filling with every people he stacked apart. He
offers Ede the Zephyra-deal at scale, the Forge-Court plan in the source's
own voice: rule the floors gently; someone must. The hundred banners answer
before she does. The shortest conversation in the book. **Turn: the fall of
the tower's will — and the stolen aether goes home.** *PLATE: double-spread —
the throne-hall from behind Vharuk: one man on a chair, a world at the door.*

### EPILOGUE — The Descent
The long walk down, floor by freed floor: Vyx receives her annual story
(Rhovan keeps the contract — he tells her the whole book); Voss's tolls
become ferry-fares; Berrin's first delivery, under Nix's eye, to floor 20;
the Frosthall rebuilt; Hobb Fennick's counts, at very long last, matching.
Ede plants the Shieldwall banner not on the summit but in Roothollow's
market, where the recruits drill. *PLATE: the tower behind, a market square,
a banner leaning against a horse trough.*
