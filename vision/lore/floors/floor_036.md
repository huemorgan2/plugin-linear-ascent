# Floor 36 — The Processional Way (Men)

> *"The bell tolls for a funeral that's been walking a thousand years and never reaches the grave. Don't fall into step. You won't reach it either." — Bell-warden Cole Mourngate*

**Cut from:** the funeral country of Men — the great burial causeway · **Gate-town:** Mourngate · **Warden:** Mournhide

**Sky:** low — a bare mile of air; the lid leans on the willows
**Veil:** heavy — a grief-grey gloom thick enough to breathe
**Land:** Rimlands seven miles of willow water-meadows; the deep middle
hides the grave-isle landing
**Gates:** down-gate by Mourngate at the causeway's head on the rim; up-gate
in the lych-arch at the causeway's end, out on the grave-isle in the
drowned middle — the funeral road runs inward, and so must you

## The land
A long willow-lined causeway raised over drowned water-meadows — the
processional road along which Men carried their dead to the barrows, hung the
whole length with passing-bells that toll on their own in the wind. A
spectral funeral shuffles the Way endlessly, coffin borne high, mourners
grey, and the living who fall into step are swept along and never let go. The
willows weep real water; the bells never stop; grief hangs in the air thick
enough to breathe. The meadows bend down toward the drowned middle, and the
causeway runs the same way — rim to heart, out over deepening water to the
barrow-field's grave-isle. There is no way up that is not the funeral road
walked to its end, with the whole procession between you and the arch. The
stair-lift is set in a lych-arch at the causeway's end, and Mournhide bears
the coffin across the road before it.

## Flora
- **Trees / canopy:** weeping-willow avenues, and the black funeral-cypress
  at the causeway's bends.
- **Ground & water plants:** grave-rush in the flooded meadows, mourning-iris,
  and the pale bell-flower that rings faintly with the passing-bells.
- **Under the Yoke:** the willows' trailing withes snare and pull toward the
  water; the bell-flowers ring a compulsion to join the procession, clearest
  in the gloom.

## Places of interest
- **The Lych-Undercroft.** The crypt-vault beneath the causeway where
  processions rested the dead overnight. *Seed: the true burial-rite — the one
  that lets a funeral *end* — is carved on its lych-slab.*
- **The Bell-Tower.** The great tower at the Way's head that hung the master
  passing-bell, whose toll drives the procession. *Seed: still or re-hallow the
  bell and the ghost-funeral can finally reach its grave.*
- **The Barrow-Field.** The causeway's destination — an isle of low green
  burial-mounds out in the flooded middle, which the procession has walked
  toward for a thousand years and never reached, their doors open and
  waiting. *Seed: a dead one laid in an open barrow is a
  dead one that stays down, and the field is the one ground the procession
  cannot compel you across.*

## The people
Mourners — Men of the funeral-town: bell-ringers, bearers, and grave-callers
whose whole craft is seeing the dead decently to rest. Cut off with a
procession still on the road, they shelter in Mourngate, tolling for a
funeral that cannot end. They love a grief well-honored, a bell rung true, and
a dead one finally laid down. They fear the shuffling procession and the pull
to join it.
**The keeper:** Cole Mourngate, bell-warden — keeps the master bell and the
true rite, and stops his own ears against the toll that would take him.

## The six
1. **Causeway raven** (N) — *was:* a funeral-following raven. *now:* grey and
   massing along the willows, crying the toll. *Cured, they lift and settle,
   quiet.*
2. **Marsh hare** (N) — *was:* a water-meadow hare. *now:* grief-frantic,
   bolting under the marchers' feet. *Cured, it stills, then bounds off the
   causeway.*
3. **Bell-toad** (N) — *was:* a rush-pool toad. *now:* swollen, its croak
   matching the passing-bells to deepen the compulsion. *Cured, it hunkers
   silent in the rush.*
4. **Verge ghoul** (W) — *was:* a bearer who drowned off the causeway, aether
   in the flooded meadow. *now:* a dripping figure that walks the verge
   pulling marchers into the water. *Broken, it sinks and drains away.*
5. **Coffin-bearer** (W) — *was:* a pall-bearer of the endless funeral, aether
   in the bier he cannot set down. *now:* a grey bearer that presses a corner
   of the coffin onto any passer to make them carry it. *Broken, the bier
   lowers at last and it fades.*
6. **Passing-bell light** (W) — *was:* nothing — aether ringing loose in a
   cracked bell. *now:* a tolling will-o'-light that drifts the Way, calling
   the living into step. *Broken, the toll cracks off and it drains into the
   water.*

## Finds  *(quest seeds)*
- **Dirge — spell.** A mourning-word that stills grief-compulsion and lays a
  restless dead to true rest · cut on the Lych-Undercroft's slab · *Seed: learn
  the true rite to end the ghost-funeral and, above, to quiet the tower's
  grief-snares.*
- **Bell-bronze — salvage.** The deep-ringing bronze of the passing-bells,
  cast into weapons whose ring shakes the undead · won by recasting a cracked
  bell · *Seed: arm the host against the tower's raised dead.*
- **Widow's draught — brew.** Steadies a climber's heart against sorrow, fear,
  and the pull to give up · a mourner's brew against despair · *Seed: carry it
  through the grimmest floors ahead.*
- **A hallowed passing-bell — relic.** One master bell re-rung true, whose
  toll frees any soul caught in a compulsion · re-hallowed in the Bell-Tower ·
  *Seed: a portable end to the tower's grief- and music-snares above.*

## The Warden — Mournhide
A coffin-bearer grown vast, welded under the master bier and hung with
passing-bells, tolling the whole Way as it comes — it fights by drawing the
living into the procession's step and pressing the endless coffin onto them,
its bells ringing the will out of the unwary. Break it (and ring the true
rite) and the funeral reaches its grave at last; the bells fall silent; the
stair-lift opens.

## When it falls
Mourngate rings the true rite from the Bell-Tower and the spectral funeral
lays its dead down and disperses; the Way goes quiet after a thousand years.
The grief-grey gloom unravels in a day — the first honest sky over the
meadows in eighty years — and the willows lift; green comes back along the
causeway where only grey grew.
The mourners climb as the host's dead-layers and heart-keepers, carrying
**Dirge** and bell-bronze — the folk who lay the tower's raised dead to rest
and keep a grieving army marching.
