# Floor 100 — The Throne (Made) · Vharuk, the Demon King

> *"He built a hundred floors so we would never stand in one room. Look behind you, climber. Every floor you freed is standing in this one now. That was the one thing he could never allow — and here it is." — the freed host at the throne*

**The Made — the summit:** the throne of the tower, the top of the stolen world · **Gate-town:** none — beyond here there is only sky · **Warden:** **Vharuk, the Demon King** — the source of the Yoke, the architect of the Ascent, the last enemy

**Sky:** cathedral — and then none: above the summit there is only true sky,
black and starred
**Veil:** thin — no gloom kept over the King's own head
**Land:** Rimlands two miles — open iron floor; the deep middle is the
throne
**Gates:** no up-gate — the Last Door opens from below into the one hall,
and there is nowhere left to climb

## The land
The summit of the Ascent — a single vast throne-hall open to a black sky,
crowning the hundred floors: a floor of raw broadcast-aether and cold iron
where Vharuk sits above the whole stolen world. The tray is a perfect
circle, the hundredth and plainest, and even it obeys the rule that built
all the rest: the open iron floor bends shallowly down toward its middle, so
the throne stands at the lowest point of the highest place — the King at the
bottom of his own lens. From here the Yoke went out to
every floor; from here the fever was sown, the collars forged, the constructs
made. It is the highest and emptiest place in creation, built by a King who
wanted to be alone above a world he'd cut into a hundred pieces so it could
never rise against him. With the reactor scrammed far below, the hall is
*quiet* for the first time in an age — and into that quiet, up the open Last
Door, comes the one thing he forbade: the whole freed world, gathered.

## Who Vharuk is
Not a devil from any hell — a **usurper of nothing,** a sorcerer-tyrant of the
old world of Aldervale who learned to steal aether from living souls and
mistook that theft for godhood. He did not conquer the world so much as
*disassemble* it: he raised the Ascent and dealt its peoples out across a
hundred floors — Men, Giants, Elves, the Deep-folk, the Waste-folk — precisely
so that no two of them could ever meet, agree, and march on him together. The
Yoke kept them mad; the fever kept their beasts as guards; the collars kept
their soldiers as his own. Every horror the climb has fought was a symptom of
this single, cowardly idea: **divide the many so the one can rule.** Vharuk's
power was never courage or right — only the aether of everyone he'd cut apart,
and with the core dark, even that is failing.

## The throne — the last fight
Vharuk fights as what he is: a hoard of stolen strength wearing a crown. He
wields the last of the un-scrammed aether, hurls the Made's every art at once,
and — cruelest of all — tries to turn the climb's own liberated host against
itself with a final blast of the Yoke. But everything the ascent has gathered
answers him:
- **Scram** (floor 93) keeps the core dark, so his power cannot renew — he
  spends what he has and gets no more.
- **Disband** (floor 90), **Silence** (98), and **Recant** (learned deep
  below) break his last hold on the freed, so the Yoke's final blast fails and
  the host holds.
- **Unmake** (floor 99) and the King-steel blade cut through the borrowed
  aether he's armored himself in.
- The **Champion** (floor 99), if she was freed, stands at the climber's side,
  and the whole liberated tower — every keeper, every freed soldier, every
  woken captive from floors 1 to 99 — fills the hall at the climber's back.

Vharuk is offered nothing, because he is the source: there is no collar to
break on him, no fever to cure, no weld to undo. He is not a victim of the
horror — **he is the horror.** He is *ended,* and with him the theft that
powered everything.

## What his fall sets free — the whole tower
When Vharuk falls, the last stolen aether he was hoarding lets go, and it does
not vanish — **it goes home.** Every soul he cut out of the world is made
whole; the Yoke is silent forever; the fever lifts from every beast on every
floor at once; every remaining collar falls open; every construct drains to
dead metal. The hundred floors — Men and Giants and Elves, Deep and Waste and
the freed of the Made — are no longer a hundred prisons kept apart. They are
one world again, and for the first time since the Theft its peoples **stand in
a single room** at the summit: the exact thing Vharuk built the entire Ascent
to prevent, achieved on his own throne-floor over his fallen body.

## When it falls — the descent
There is no floor 101. The climb does not go up from here; it goes *down* — the
liberated host descending the whole tower it freed, floor by floor, gathering
every people it woke, until the Ascent itself, its purpose gone, is only a
mountain again with a stair. Below, the last veils die together — the whole
ladder of lands turning gold at once, each floor getting back its weather,
its stars, its honest nights; even the Made's iron miles will green in time,
given light and left alone. Aldervale is not restored to what it was; it is
made into what it never got to be — the many, no longer kept apart, deciding
together what to build now that no one is dividing them. The keepers become the
new world's memory; the freed soldiers lay down their collars as its guard;
the climber, who freed a floor at a time until the whole was free, walks down
out of the story into a world that can finally gather.

*This is the top of the Linear Ascent. Everything the game is about is this
last truth: that the hundred floors were one world all along, cut apart by fear
of what it could do together — and that liberating it, one floor at a time, was
only ever the long work of putting it back in the same room.*
