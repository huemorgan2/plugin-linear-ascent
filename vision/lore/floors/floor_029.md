# Floor 29 — The Tithe Barrows (Men)

> *"Every soul owed the barrows one grave-gift. The tower owes them a hundred graves it dug, and won't pay." — Barrow-warden Sef Wakewater*

**Cut from:** the ancestral grave-country of Men · **Gate-town:** Wakewater · **Warden:** Tithegrim

**Sky:** standard — four miles of still air under the grey lid
**Veil:** thin — a mourning grey, gentle as the turf
**Land:** Rimlands nine miles of soft barrow-turf; the deep middle hides the
eldest waking mounds
**Gates:** down-gate among the newer mounds near Wakewater; up-gate in the
turf-covered rock mound at the field's edge, its arch a standing lych-stone

## The land
A green country of grassed burial mounds — the old barrow-fields where Men
laid their dead with a tithe of grave-goods, mound after mound to the
horizon under a still grey sky. The turf is soft, the mounds are quiet, and
they should stay quiet — but the tower's fever has soaked into the grave-
gold, and the barrows have begun to *wake*. Beetle-runs riddle the mounds;
the grass is too green over the newest graves. It is a solemn place turned
restless, where the dead are stirring and the living keep vigil. The field
bends shallowly down toward its middle, where the eldest and greatest
mounds were raised on the deepest earth; the newer graves, and the living
who tend them, keep to the rim's soft turf. The
stair-lift stands in a turf-covered rock mound at the field's edge, its
arch a standing lych-stone; Tithegrim keeps the vigil-ground before it.

## Flora
- **Trees / canopy:** graveyard yew, ancient and black, one to each great
  mound.
- **Ground & water plants:** barrow-grass (rich over the dead), grave-nettle,
  and the pale death-cap ring on the newest mounds.
- **Under the Yoke:** the yews drip a waking-sap in the gloom; the grave-
  nettle stings a cold numbness; the death-cap rings mark where a barrow is
  about to open.

## Places of interest
- **The Barrow Chamber.** An opened mound's stone burial-chamber, grave-
  goods spilled across the floor. *Seed: the oldest chamber holds the rite
  that lays a wight to rest — and a warning cut beside it that the rite was
  tried once already, and failed.*
- **The Lych House.** The turf-roofed mortuary hall where the wardens wash
  and watch the dead before burial, its slabs never empty now. *Seed: the
  keeper's vigil-book here lists which mounds have begun to stir, and in
  what order.*
- **The King's Mound.** The largest and oldest barrow, crowning the field's
  deep middle and still, so far, unwoken. *Seed: keep it sleeping — or wake
  it ready —
  and you decide what waits on the floor above.*

## The people
Grave-wardens — Men who tend the barrows and keep the dead's tithe, quiet
patient folk who measure time in vigils. They shelter in Wakewater, keeping
watch as the mounds stir, laying back to rest what wakes. They love a grave
kept, a tithe paid, and a dead one sleeping sound. They fear the waking
barrows and the wights that walk in their own grave-gold.
**The keeper:** Sef Wakewater, barrow-warden — keeps the lych-gate and the
lay-to-rest rite, and has not slept since the mounds began to stir.

## The six
1. **Grave beetle** (N) — *was:* a mound-tunneling beetle. *now:* iron-cased,
   swarming out of the beetle-runs, gnawing the buried. *Cured, it burrows
   back into a quiet mound.*
2. **Barrow rat** (N) — *was:* a grave-robbing rat. *now:* gold-toothed,
   nesting in the grave-goods. *Cured, it bolts down a beetle-run.*
3. **Mound-owl** (N) — *was:* a yew-roosting owl. *now:* pale and silent,
   stooping on anything that keeps vigil. *Cured, it wings up into a black
   yew.*
4. **Barrow-hound** (N) — *was:* a grave-guard dog buried with its master.
   *now:* risen gaunt, guarding a grave from the living. *Cured, it lies back
   down on the mound and sleeps.*
5. **Tithe-wight** (W) — *was:* a dead one denied its grave-tithe, aether in
   the withheld gold. *now:* a mound-figure that walks demanding its due,
   snatching the living's goods. *Broken (and its tithe laid), it lies back
   to rest and drains into the mound.*
6. **Barrow ghoul** (W) — *was:* a grave-robber caught and buried alive,
   aether in the old crime. *now:* a hunched feeder that digs up the newly
   dead and drags the living down to join them. *Broken, it collapses into an
   open grave and is still.*

## Finds  *(quest seeds)*
- **Lay-to-rest — spell.** Settles a waking dead back into true sleep — the
  grave-wardens' rite · cut into the oldest Barrow Chamber · *learn it to
  quiet the field and, above, to unmake the tower's raised dead.*
- **Barrow-gold — material.** Grave-gold of the old tithes, aether-charged
  by its long lying; the richest enchanting-metal on the tier · in the
  waking mounds · *pay back a wight's tithe first, then claim the rest
  freely.*
- **The vigil-book — knowledge.** Sef's rolling account of which barrows
  have stirred and the order they will open · kept in the Lych House · *read
  it and you know where the next dead will walk before it does.*
- **Wake-tonic — brew.** A vigil-keeper's draught against dread and sleep;
  steadies the nerve to stand watch over the walking dead.

## The Warden — Tithegrim
A barrow-hound risen and welded, saddled with a rider of grave-gold and
lych-iron — the field's collector, walking mound to mound to wake the dead
and take their tithe by force. It fights on the vigil-ground, raising the
lesser dead around it. Break it (and lay its own tithe to rest) and the
mounds settle; the field sleeps; the stair-lift opens on the King's own
mound above.

## When it falls
Wakewater lays the waking dead to rest and the barrow-field goes quiet and
green again; the old tithes are paid at last. The gloom lifts in a day and
the field keeps its first vigil under real sky in eighty years; the yews put
out honest growth over mounds that sleep. The grave-wardens climb as the
host's dead-layers and vigil-keepers, carrying **Lay-to-rest** and barrow-gold
— the folk who can put down whatever the tower raises on the floors ahead.
