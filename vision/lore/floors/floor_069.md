# Floor 69 — The Lightning Fields (Men)

> *"We grew copper like corn and harvested the sky. Best power in the world, till the day the sky started aiming." — Field-warden Mera Copperpole*

**Cut from:** the storm-harvest country of Men · **Gate-town:** Copperpole · **Warden:** Groundstrike
**Sky:** standard — four miles of storm-ceiling that never rains.
**Veil:** thin — the flashing storm is lid enough.
**Land:** Rimlands six miles wide — the earthed farm-belts; the deep middle
holds the earthing-vaults.
**Gates:** both at the rim — the down-gate among the farm-belts, the up-gate
in the earthed bluff ringed by copper poles at the field's edge.

## The land
A vast plain bristling with copper lightning-poles — rank on rank of tall
verdigris rods set to catch the sky's lightning and pour its power down into
earthing-vaults, under a permanent storm-ceiling that never rains but never
stops flashing. The air smells of ozone; the poles hum and spit; a strike can
come down anywhere, but comes down oftenest on the tallest thing standing —
which, out on the field, is you. Men harvested the storm here and grew rich on
its charge. Now the storm is fever-mad and aims to kill. The plain bows
toward its middle like every stolen country, and the harvest followed the
shape of it: the earthed farm-belts and Copperpole keep to the rim, and the
poles march down and inward to the earthing-vaults sunk at the floor's deep
heart, where the charge runs to ground. The stair-lift is set in an earthed
bluff ringed by copper poles at the field's edge; Groundstrike calls the
strikes before it.

## Flora
- **Trees / canopy:** none — copper poles and storm-sky.
- **Ground & water plants:** conductor-grass (that carries stray charge),
  ozone-moss on the poles, and the copper-flower that blooms toward the last
  strike.
- **Under the Yoke:** the conductor-grass carries a strike sideways across
  the field; the copper-flowers draw the next bolt to where the living
  stand; the ozone-moss sparks underfoot.

## Places of interest
- **The Ground-Vault.** The earthing-cellar beneath the field where the
  harvested charge is stored and bled safely to earth. *Seed: the master
  earthing-switch is down here — thrown one way it makes the whole field safe
  to cross, thrown the other it becomes one great weapon.*
- **The Master Pole.** The tallest lightning-rod on the plain, crowned in a
  standing crown of fire, drawing to itself every strike the sky throws.
  *Seed: stand clear of it and the field is survivable; claim its head and the
  storm answers to you.*
- **The Glass Garden.** The stretch of field where a thousand strikes have
  fused the sand to branching fulgurite, a silent grove of lightning-glass
  grown where the bolts came down. *Seed: the glass records where the storm
  strikes oftenest — read the garden and you read the safe lanes across the
  whole field.*

## The people
Field-folk — Men of the storm-harvest: pole-wrights, charge-wardens, and
earthing-crews who grew the copper and tapped the sky, rubber-soled and
storm-wise, who read the next strike by the hum of the poles. They shelter in
Copperpole in the earthed vaults, crossing the field only by the safe lanes.
They love a caught strike, a full charge-vault, and a lane run safe. They fear
the aiming storm and the charge-thief things.
**The keeper:** Mera Copperpole, field-warden — keeps the master switch and
reads the storm's aim by the crawl of the charge on her skin.

## The six
1. **Charge-thief harpy** (N) — *was:* a pole-roosting raptor. *now:* fever-
   drawn half-human, crackling with stolen charge, diving to earth it on the
   living. *Cured, it folds to a plain kite and wheels off.*
2. **Fieldborn drake** (N) — *was:* a storm-riding drake. *now:* wreathed in
   St. Elmo's fire, strafing the lanes with arcing breath. *Cured, it glides
   off up the storm.*
3. **Copper rat** (N) — *was:* a pole-base rat. *now:* copper-furred, running
   the conductor-grass and shorting the lanes. *Cured, it bolts down an
   earthing-drain.*
4. **Arc-adder** (N) — *was:* a field adder. *now:* charge-scaled, striking
   with a shock along the grass. *Cured, it pours into a pole-base.*
5. **Spark-hare** (N) — *was:* a field hare. *now:* crackling and bolting,
   drawing strikes to where it runs. *Cured, it's a blue blur and gone.*
6. **Lane-stone mover** (W) — *was:* an earthing-crew who died in a strike,
   aether in the fused sand. *now:* a glass-and-copper figure that shifts the
   safe-lane marker-stones to lead the living under the poles. *Broken, it
   earths out and drains into the field.*

## Finds  *(quest seeds)*
- **Conduct — spell.** Channels a bolt of lightning through the caster and
  hurls it — the field-folk's offensive art, the striking twin of floor 15's
  defensive Earth. *Seed: learn it to cross the field and turn the storm on
  the throne's own charge-works above.*
- **Storm-copper — material.** Charge-hardened copper drawn from the poles,
  the finest aether-conductor and shock-weapon core in the tower. *Seed:
  strip a spent pole.*
- **The strike-map — knowledge.** Mera's read of the storm's aim — the hum of
  the poles, the lean of the copper-flowers, the fulgurite of the Glass Garden
  — that shows where the sky will hit next. *Seed: win her trust and she walks
  the host safe across the killing field.*

## The Warden — Groundstrike
A fieldborn drake welded to the Master Pole, crowned in captured lightning —
it fights by calling the storm's strikes down onto the living wherever they
stand, shifting the safe lanes, and hurling arced bolts. Break it and the
aiming storm goes wild and harmless; the safe lanes hold; the master switch
can be thrown and the stair-lift reached.

## When it falls
Copperpole throws the master switch and the field earths safe; the harvested
storm pours into the rising's vaults instead of down on its people. The
Warden's gloom goes in a night; the storm-ceiling stays — it is the
country's own stolen weather — but under it the safe lanes hold, and green
comes back along the earthed belts where the grass no longer bites. The field-
folk climb as the host's storm-callers and charge-wardens, carrying **Conduct**
and storm-copper — the folk who turn the sky's own lightning on the throne and
keep the army safe under any storm the tower calls.
